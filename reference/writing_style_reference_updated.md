# Writing Style Reference

## Example 1: Charged Dialogue with Subtext
Soren leaned back, the brass lion's head of his walking stick catching the amber glow of a dying hearth. Across from him, Miri sat perfectly still—a shadow tethered to a high-backed chair, her presence as silent as the fog rolling off the harbor.
"The Trade Syndicate is twitching, Miri," Soren said, his voice a low, gravelly rasp. "They've lost their appetite for the new tariffs, and they're looking for someone to blame for the missing shipments."
Miri didn't blink. "You mean they're looking for a thief they can actually catch."
"I mean they are looking for a distraction. I'm giving them one." He slid a heavy, wax-sealed envelope across the scarred oak of the table. "I need you to drop this in the Governor's carriage tonight. Don't break the seal."
Miri glanced at the envelope, then back at his gloved hands. "You're playing a dangerous game with the blue-bloods, Soren. Even for you. If they find out you're the one feeding them the ledger leaks—"
"They won't. Not unless a Ghost decides to whisper in the wrong ear."
The silence that followed was heavy, taut as a piano wire. Miri didn't flinch at the accusation; she let it hang there, a cold, sharp thing between them. "I've never been a fan of whispers," she said softly. "I prefer the sound of a steel edge finding its mark. You know that."
"I know what you prefer," Soren replied, his eyes narrowing to flinty slits. "I also know you've been spending more time at the western docks than the job requires. Looking for flags that don't belong to this city."
"The horizon is free, Soren. Unlike most things in this gutter."
"Nothing is free." He leaned forward, the distance between them vanishing, though neither moved an inch. "I bought your freedom from the workhouses, Miri. I don't pay for your daydreams about the southern Isles."
"You bought my debt," she corrected, her voice like silk over a razor. "You didn't buy my eyes. And you certainly didn't buy my silence regarding your own... ghosts."
Soren's grip tightened on his cane until the wood groaned. He didn't ask what she meant. He didn't have to. The memory of a name he never spoke—a boy lost to the fever-streets years ago—seemed to rattle the glasses on the shelf behind them.
"Just the envelope, Miri," he said, the ice returning to his tone.
"Just the envelope," she echoed, rising from the chair without a sound. "But be careful, Soren. Even the King of the Wharves can trip if he's too busy looking backward."

**Style Notes:**
- Subtext over exposition; what's *not* said matters as much as what is
- Short sentences during tension; longer during reflection
- Dialogue carries character voice immediately
- Minimal internal narration; let action/dialogue do the work
- Physical details ground emotional stakes (grip on cane, silence as weight)

## Example 2: Physical Intimacy with Character Voice

"Shit. Are you all right?" Theo asked, his voice strained.
"I'm fine," Jackie breathed, her eyes locked on his. "You won't break me."
He pushed inside that first, tight inch of her, and she gasped at the fit, the sudden stretch of him. She was too busy kissing him to find her words, her body arching in a wordless, frantic demand. More. She needed all of him.
"You're going to be the death of me, Jackie," Theo ground out. Whatever was left of his control snapped, and he took her completely with one hard, uncompromising thrust.
Jackie moaned into the kiss, the sound vibrating between them. He was so deep that she felt him everywhere, an invasion of heat and power.
"Tell me you're all right," he demanded, already moving.
"I'm perfect," she whispered. Better than perfect. Power began to blaze beneath her skin again, buzzing with a frenzied, electric need.
"You feel so fucking good." Theo slammed back into her, setting a brutal, steady pace. His mouth slid down the column of her neck while his hand rose to cup her breast, his touch searing.
Jackie couldn't even think around the maddening pleasure. Her back pounded against the armoire door with every thrust, filling the room with the rhythmic sound of straining bodies and creaking wood. Every stroke was better than the last, making her breaths stutter in her chest.
"Fuck, I'm never going to get enough of you, am I?" Theo muttered, his face buried in the crook of her neck as she arched into him.
"Shut up and fuck me, Theo," she countered. Tomorrow was soon enough for regrets; right now, there was only this. Reaching up, she grasped the top edge of the armoire with one hand so she could rock back with more force, meeting the drive of his hips and taking him deeper.
Theo dragged one of her nightgown straps off her shoulder. The cool night air kissed the hardened peak of her nipple for only a heartbeat before his hot mouth covered it. The sensations spiraled, spinning and coiling into a tight knot of pleasure so deep within her that the tension became sublimely unbearable.
The armoire door groaned, then splintered off its hinges. Theo's shadows whipped out instantly, protecting her as the frame snapped and wood crashed around them. Jackie's power flared in response, rising to meet his, sizzling beneath her skin as she gripped his shoulders and found his mouth with hers.
There was no stopping. They couldn't stop.
"Fuck," Theo cursed, taking her over and over. He turned them again, seeking a different support until there was cloth against her back—not the bed, but the heavy curtains shoved to the side of the window.
Energy crackled again as their mouths met. He drove on, winding that internal knot painfully tighter with every movement. The power was becoming too much; it was burning her, heating her blood with a desperate need for release.
"Theo!" she cried out, writhing against him even as she held on for dear life.

**Style Notes:**
- Sensory-specific without being flowery
- Characters' distinct voices persist even during intimacy
- Physical descriptions grounded in rhythm/breath/sensation
- Emotional stakes visible through physical reactions
- Action reveals character (urgency, control, passion) simultaneously with intimacy

## Example 3: Tension & Control

Julian sat in the high-backed velvet chair, his spine a rigid line of defiance that he hoped looked like casual grace. Across from him, Commander Vane was a smear of intoxication and resentment, leaning against the mahogany desk as if his own bones had grown weary of the City's weight.
"You think you've won, don't you?" Vane's voice was thin, a frayed wire. He didn't look up from the amber glass in his hand. "Because the girl performed. Because the crowd cheered. You think you've mastered the trials."
Julian felt a flicker of heat in his chest—the familiar, snarling hunger of his bloodline—but he smothered it. A Thorne landed on top. A Thorne did not scowl like a street urchin.
"I think the recruit performed her duties, Commander," Julian replied. His voice was smooth, a polished stone. "My role was merely to ensure she had the platform to do so."
"Duties." Vane let out a wet, jagged laugh. "You speak of her like a clockwork toy. I saw the way you watched her at the gates. Was that duty, too? Or was that the scent of old sentiment getting to your head?"
Julian tightened his grip on the arms of the chair, his knuckles turning a waxy white. He hated the way Vane could peel back his skin with a single, slurred observation. He thought of Elara—the warmth of her hand, the defiant tilt of her chin—and immediately categorized the memory as a liability. It was a tactical error to feel, and an even greater one to show it.
"A handler must build trust to ensure performance," Julian said, his tone dropping into a practiced, academic chill. "If the girl believes I am her champion, she fights harder. If she fights harder, I win the commission. It's simple arithmetic, isn't it?"
Vane finally looked at him, his yellowed eyes narrowing. "You're a monster in a very fine suit, Julian. Just like your father. He always knew how to dress up a slaughter."
Julian didn't flinch. He let the insult hang in the air, a trophy of his own restraint. He watched a single drop of condensation slide down the Commander's glass, measuring the seconds of the man's silence. He wasn't just winning the argument; he was outlasting the man.
"My father is dead," Julian said softly, standing up and smoothing the front of his Academy tunic. "And I have a recruit to keep alive. If you'll excuse me, I have a game to finish."
He turned and walked toward the door, his steps measured and rhythmic. He didn't look back. Control was the only thing that kept the world from dissolving into the chaos of the outer rings, and as long as he could button his jacket and steady his breath, he was still the master of the board.

**Style Notes:**
- Precise word choice; no wasted language
- Internal calculation visible in action/dialogue
- Restraint is its own form of tension
- Small physical details carry emotional weight (grip tightening, the drop of condensation, smoothing jacket)
- Subtext in observation (watching him instead of responding directly)

## Banned Constructions — "Sonnetisms"

These are recurring prose tics that create the illusion of precision without delivering it. They should be caught and cut at every instance.

### The "particular/specific" abstraction
Using "particular" or "specific" to dress up a vague noun — gestures at depth without achieving it.
- ❌ "the particular quality of attention" / "the particular exhaustion of waiting" / "the particular focus required to"
- ✅ Say the actual thing. If it can't be named plainly, the image isn't ready yet.

### The "there's a difference" announcer
Ending a contrast by explaining that a contrast was just made. The distinction should stand on its own.
- ❌ "I'm not here to break you. I want you mine. There's a difference."
- ✅ "I'm not here to break you. I want you mine." — cut the announcement.

### The "found himself/herself" passive interiority
Passive distancing from internal state. Never use "found himself [adjective or verb]."
- ❌ "he found himself entirely without words" / "she found herself unable to look away"
- ✅ "He had nothing to say." / "She didn't look away." Direct. Present. Active.

### The "the X of Y" noun stack
Abstract nouns stacked in the pattern "the [quality] of [situation]" as a substitute for showing.
- ❌ "the weight of everything unsaid" / "the exhaustion of waiting without knowing what he was waiting for"
- ✅ Show what the weight looks like. Show what the exhaustion does to the body or the voice.

### The "something" placeholder
Using "something" when the specific thing can be named.
- ❌ "something passed across his face" / "there was something in the way she said it"
- ✅ Name what passed. Name what the something was. If you can't, dig until you can.
