# 145 BERKELEY PLACE — HAVEN HOUSE
### Room-by-Room Interior Descriptions

---

## **CELLAR CORRIDOR**

The stairs descend into a short stone corridor, cool and slightly damp in the way of old London foundations. A single iron-bracketed sconce casts more shadow than light. The temperature charm Draco applied keeps it stable year-round — cold enough to preserve, not cold enough to bite.

A small wine rack Jackie bought second-hand from a market on the Portobello Road lines the left wall, already half-stocked with bottles she chose herself. Wooden shelving on the right holds root vegetables, dried herbs, and the accumulated detritus of a house still settling into itself — a sealed crate that was Draco's, two of Jackie's duffel bags that never quite made it upstairs. The corridor functions as a transitional space, a brief pause before the rooms beyond.

Three doors branch off: the gym to the left, the dueling room straight ahead, and between them, a narrower door that leads somewhere quieter.

---

## **GYM**

A practical space carved out of the cellar's footprint — low-ceilinged, functional, unadorned. Rubber matting covers the original flagstone. Weight benches and free weights occupy one half; a rack holding practice blades lines the far wall — swords, daggers, quarterstaffs, all well-maintained and frequently used.

Jackie's heavy bag hangs from a ceiling beam, the canvas repaired twice already. The room smells of exertion and leather and the faint metallic tang of sweat. No mirrors here. No audience. Just the work.

---

## **DUELING ROOM**

The longest space in the cellar, running parallel to the corridor. Jackie drew inspiration from two sources: Nott Manor's formal training room and Geneviève Lavigne's practice hall at the château. The result is something between the two — serious without being ostentatious.

The floor is sprung hardwood, polished smooth and scuffed at the center from daily use. Practice dummies line the far wall, surrounded by crates of target discs in various sizes. Mirrors run along one side — Draco's addition, installed without discussion — reflecting movement and form back to whoever's training.

The dueling strip runs the length of the room, marked in chalk that keeps getting refreshed. At the far end, a narrow bench holds a water jug and two cups. Jackie uses this room every day. Draco uses it when his thoughts get loud.

The room doesn't feel like a gym. It feels like somewhere people go to think.

---

## **THE QUIET ROOM**

A narrow door between the gym and dueling room opens onto something smaller. Windowless. Spartan.

The room is perhaps eight feet by ten — just large enough for necessity, no larger. A metal cot is bolted to the floor against the back wall. A small desk and chair occupy one corner. A travel refrigerator hums softly, stocked with blood bags on a preservation charm. The walls are bare stone, naturally cool, completely lightproof.

It looks like a cell. It's meant to be a sanctuary.

Jackie designed it after Filippo described the quiet room in Florence — the place Theo went when the hunger became too much, when he needed to disappear until the worst of it passed. She recreated the essentials: the isolation, the temperature, the emergency blood supply. But she left out the bars. No locks on the inside. No cage.

The room exists for the worst days. The ones they hope never come.

## GARDEN FLOOR

### Herbary

Jackie's domain, and it announces itself immediately. The herbary runs the full depth of the garden floor, connected to the outside patio by a set of glass-paned double doors that she leaves open when the weather permits. The air inside is green and thick — warm-humid in a way that's distinctly different from the rest of the house, layered with the scent of soil and growing things: rosemary and damp peat and something sharper underneath, medicinal and faintly sweet.

She organized the shelving herself over two days, not consulting either of them. The result is logic only she fully understands — tall specimen planters along the south wall for the light-hungry varieties, hanging racks for the drying herbs, a lower shelf of ceramic pots near the door where she keeps things she reaches for often. Labels in her handwriting, some in English, some in French, a few in a shorthand no one else would parse. There's a stool she drags from corner to corner depending on what she's tending. Soil still on the floor in two places where she hasn't gotten around to cleaning it up.
It's the only room that has windows that aren't spelled to filter sunlight, so Theo cannot come in during the day.

It doesn't look like a professional herbary. It looks like hers.

### Potions Station

Attached to the herbary, separated by an archway rather than a door. Counter space and cupboards, burner setup, a rack of copper-bottomed cauldrons in graduated sizes. Draco organized this half — his influence is unmistakable in the precision, the labeled jars of ingredients aligned by category, the small framed card pinned near the burners that lists safety protocols in his handwriting. He did it without being asked, partly because organization is how he processes anxiety, partly because he didn't want Jackie blowing up a wall.

Jackie has already rearranged one shelf. He noticed. He hasn't said anything yet.

### Office / Study

The office sits at the front of the garden floor, a quieter, lower-ceilinged room that catches western afternoon light. It was designated communal — the desk large enough for two, a second chair pulled up for whoever needs it. In practice it functions mainly as Jackie's. Her Auror training materials have claimed the desktop: notebooks, a duty roster, a copy of the Ministry's standard field protocols guide with pages flagged. A framed Instax photograph on the desk corner — the three of them in Tuscany, taken before everything changed, sun-washed and slightly overexposed.

One of Theo's law texts sits on the corner of the shelf with a bookmark still in it. No one has moved it.

## PARLOR FLOOR

### Entry

Black-and-white Victorian tile in the entry hall, original to the building. High ceiling, a narrow console table along one wall in dark walnut. A mirror with a gilt frame Jackie found and Draco disparaged and she bought anyway. A coat rack that currently holds one of Jackie's jackets, a scarf, and nothing else — the house hasn't accumulated the clutter of long habitation yet. The entry feels formal and slightly bare, like someone set the stage and then forgot to populate it.

### Living Room

The largest room on the parlor floor, running toward the deck at the back. The trio negotiated this space with visible evidence of all three of them — and the negotiation shows.

The sofa is deep and charcoal grey, oversized, chosen by Draco for exactly those reasons and nothing else. It holds its shape. Two armchairs flank it, mismatched — one a dark bottle-green velvet that was clearly Theo's choice, one a warm cognac leather that was Jackie's. The coffee table is a thick slab of reclaimed oak on iron legs. On it, at any given time: someone's teacup, a folded Prophet, a book left face-down.

The walls are a deep, almost-black forest green. This was a compromise that took forty-five minutes and produced a color none of them would have chosen alone and all three of them privately like. Brass fixtures, low-wattage. A fireplace on the north wall with a plain limestone mantle — no decoration yet except for a single Instax photograph tucked against the left edge, leaning rather than hung.

French doors open to the deck. When the weather holds, they stay open.

### Deck

Narrow but deep enough for a wrought-iron table and two chairs. A third chair was purchased and exists in the living room; no one has moved it out yet. The patio garden beyond is Muggle-maintained for appearances; Jackie tends the window boxes herself.

### Dining Room

A rectangular room at the front of the parlor floor, slightly more formal than it has any right to be in a house of three twenty-one-year-olds. A dining table in dark walnut seats six, though they've never had six. The chairs are mismatched by design — four in charcoal linen, two in a deep burgundy velvet at either head. Candles on the table in brass holders. A sideboard against the wall that came with the house and is slowly being colonized by a wine decanter, a set of crystal glasses Draco brought from his flat, and a stack of unopened post.

The dining room is where the house most feels like it belongs to someone else. They use it, but they haven't claimed it yet.

### Kitchen

Compact, 9x11, but efficiently laid out. White tile, brass fittings, a ceramic sink deep enough to be useful. Jackie cooks; this is established. Her presence is in the organized chaos of it — a wooden knife block, a bunch of dried lavender hanging from the overhead rack beside the copper pans, a handwritten shopping list on the fridge held by a magnet that says BRIGHTON in block letters (origin unknown).

Draco makes tea. This is the full extent of his culinary engagement, and he does it with unnecessary precision, as if the act of measuring loose-leaf requires the same focus as a Potions exam. There is a specific mug he uses. He would deny having a specific mug.

## THIRD FLOOR

### Jackie's Bedroom

The largest of the three bedrooms, at back of the house with two windows that look out over the garden. The bed is a wide iron-framed four-poster, but dressed warmly rather than formally — layered quilts, a deep navy-blue coverlet, more pillows than anyone needs. A knitted throw draped over one corner that she brought from the Nott estate.

The room smells faintly of the herbary two floors down, green and living, because she's brought small pots up and arranged them on the windowsill and on the low shelf along the east wall. A trailing pothos has already claimed the corner of the wardrobe. There's a jewelry tray on the dresser, not organized, catches of every day — earrings, a bracelet, the chain she wears sometimes and sometimes doesn't. Her trainers kicked off at the foot of the bed.

Collected objects on every surface that don't match but belong together: a piece of quartz from the Lavigne vaults. A Muggle snow globe she bought in Rome in April. Theo's Instax camera, currently sitting on the nightstand because she borrowed it and hasn't given it back. A small framed photo of Samaya, and another one of Jackie, Anne, Caroline and Luna in their Hogwart's uniforms, smiling. A moving poster of Samuel Carrow with the Second Sons logo is taped to the back of the door. 

The room is warm. It is the warmest room in the house.

### Library / Music Room

A room at war with itself, and better for it.

The shelves run floor-to-ceiling on three walls, installed by Draco with the help of an Extending Charm that makes the room hold more books than it has any spatial right to. His side is organized by subject, then alphabetically within each category — History of Magic, European Magical Law, Continental Theory, a shelf of Muggle literature he'd rather not explain. Green-shaded lamps on the reading table. A wingback leather chair in the corner with a side table for a glass of something.

Jackie's shelves are more loosely organized — heavier on magical theory, herbology, a growing section of Auror training materials she's accumulating and hasn't told anyone about yet. Her presence in the room is in the armchair positioned near the piano, angled toward it rather than toward a book. She sits there to read, but she also just sits there. Draco knows this and plays anyway, which is its own kind of conversation.

The piano occupies the south wall — an upright, dark-lacquered, slightly older than it should be for its quality, which means someone knew what they were looking for when they bought it. Draco's sheet music is organized on the stand and in a box beside it; when he's working through something difficult there are pencil markings in the margins. The room was set up with the knowledge that Theo will eventually come home, and when he does, there will be space for him here too.

Jackie's armchair is the worn one — deep-cushioned, slightly too large for her, a blanket thrown over the back. It faces the piano at an angle that means she can see Draco's hands when he plays.

### Jackie's Bathroom

Attached to Jackie's bedroom, a Victorian-proportioned bathroom with original period fittings the previous owners had the sense not to replace. Black-and-white hex tile floor, a deep claw-foot tub, a wide mirror in a dark wood frame that steams over faster than it should. Jackie's products have colonized the shelves in organized abundance — oils and creams and a pot of something that smells of eucalyptus that Draco uses and has never acknowledged using.

---

## FOURTH FLOOR

### Draco's Bedroom

The room of someone who has not yet decided to stay.

The bed is made — tightly, hospital corners, the way a person makes a bed when they were raised by people who considered untidiness a character flaw. The furniture is good quality and completely impersonal: a walnut wardrobe, a narrow writing desk, a single armchair. His clothes are in the wardrobe, his toiletries on the shelf, his wand holster on the hook by the door. Nothing on the walls. Nothing on the desk except a single book, closed, and an uncapped pen lying beside it as if he just set it down.

The room is clean. Draco's rooms are always clean.

There's one small thing: a Kneazle toy on the chair — Luna's, carried up here and abandoned. He hasn't moved it. This is the closest the room comes to saying anything personal.

### Dressing Room / Office

This room is Draco's only fully inhabited space in the house. The wardrobe system is meticulously organized — not extravagantly, but deliberately, the kind of order that requires maintenance. His Ministry suits pressed and hung. The desk against the window holds his real work: case files from DIMC, correspondence in careful stacks, a pot of ink and several pens, a leather-bound planner he actually uses. A second, smaller shelf holds books he's in the middle of — three of them, bookmarked, because he can't read just one thing at a time and would be irritated if anyone pointed this out.

One framed document on the wall: his Ministry certification, hung without sentimentality but hung nonetheless. A small potted plant — a succulent, low maintenance, the only kind he could reasonably claim to care for — sits on the corner of the desk. Jackie bought it for him. He watered it this morning.

Luna's cat tree occupies the corner by the window — tall, sisal-wrapped, slightly battered at the top platform where she sharpens her claws. It was the first piece of furniture Draco placed in the room. He would explain this, if asked, as a practical matter of keeping her out of the wardrobe. No one has asked.

### Theo's Bedroom

Theo never saw this house before he left for Florence. Jackie and Draco furnished it without him — which means his room was furnished for him, by people doing their best to guess at a person they know well and still couldn't fully represent in an empty room.

The result is generic in the way that reveals the gap. A good bed, dark linen, a walnut wardrobe, a nightstand. Furniture chosen because it suited the space and wouldn't offend him. Nothing wrong with any of it. Nothing of him in any of it either.

What makes it his: the things Jackie moved in afterward. His jackets hung in the wardrobe — the ones he didn't take to Florence. A small stack of his books on the nightstand, brought up from the office, spines she knows by sight. His charmed lighter on top of the stack. His Instax photographs pinned to the wall beside the window, twelve or fifteen of them — Jackie in Tuscany, light-wrecked and laughing; Draco at a restaurant table looking annoyed at being photographed; a horse in a field; a badly framed Rome skyline where the point was clearly never the skyline.

The room smells of cedar and leather now only faintly. His cologne, absorbed into the fabric, still fading. Draco has stood in the doorway twice. He doesn't go in.

---

## PATIO / GARDEN

The garden runs forty-four feet from the back of the house to the rear wall, narrow and long in the way of London gardens. Jackie has plans for it. Currently: a small wrought-iron table, two chairs, the ghost of a previous owner's rose bed running along the left wall, already pruned back and waiting. Flagstones. A pot of herbs by the back door that she put there and forgets to bring inside when it rains.

In the evenings when the light is good, she comes out here. Sometimes Draco brings tea and sits without being asked. They don't always talk. The garden is one of the few places in the house where the absence of a third person doesn't announce itself in every corner.

It will be different when Theo comes home.
