They came back through the Floo three minutes apart, because neither of them would go first and neither would go second.

The foyer smelled of fresh plaster. Caramel had filled the gouges a week ago, five long scores down the wall at the height of a tall man's reach. Theo still saw the words every time he crossed it. *Three days.* Draco walked through without looking at the wall at all, which was how Theo knew he saw them too.

Dinner was venison stew and bread still warm from the oven, set out in the small dining room under the window. Caramel had laid three places. She'd laid three places every night for a week. Neither of them had told her to stop, and neither of them looked at the third chair.

Theo's bowl wasn't burnt. In August, while Jackie was angry with him, Caramel had burnt his dinner every night on principle, a quiet household war he'd been losing gracefully. She'd stopped the night Jackie was taken. He found he missed it. A burnt dinner meant someone in the house had an opinion about him that could change.

They ate without speaking. Rain needled the window. The spoons were too loud. Draco cut his bread into exact squares and left half of them.

Afterwards Theo meant to go upstairs. He went to the library instead.

It was the warmest room in the house and the only one that still felt like her. Old paper and beeswax, the peat fire Caramel kept banked from four o'clock, and under it all the faint sweetness of the orchard apples set out in a bowl on the side table to ripen, gone a little winey at the edges. Jackie had spent August afternoons in here with her feet up, three transfiguration books open at once and a pencil behind her ear, chasing the Animagus idea. One of them still lay face-down on the arm of the sofa where she'd left it, its spine cracked open at the page she'd been on.

The sofa looked wrong without its blanket. That was at St. Mungo's now, folded at the foot of a hospital bed.

Theo sat at the end of it anyway, pulled the Ministry files out of his bag and spread them across his knees. Inventory reports. Columns of numbers that didn't care. He read the same line four times.

Draco came in twenty minutes later. He paused in the doorway as if he'd expected the room to be empty, then crossed to the wingback chair by the fire without a word. He reached past Theo, so close Theo smelled cold air and cedar on his sleeve, and picked up Jackie's book from the arm of the sofa. He didn't close it or move her place. He sat down and started reading from the cracked page, as though he could catch up with wherever she'd been.

The fire ticked. The rain kept on.

"If she doesn't wake up soon," Draco said to the book, "I'll go back to Malfoy Manor."

Theo didn't look up from the column he wasn't reading.

"There's no point in the two of us forcing ourselves to breathe the same air," Draco went on, quite evenly. "I said I'd tell you both before I left, so I'm telling you. One of you, at least."

The page in Theo's hand had gone soft where his thumb pressed it.

"I'm not throwing you out, Malfoy," he said. "But if you want to go, I'll be the last to stop you."

Draco turned a page. It was a long time before he turned another.

Neither of them moved. Theo noted, at the edge of his mind, that this was the closest they'd sat to each other in a week. In August they'd duelled behind the house every evening at sunset, circling in the long grass. He'd learned exactly where Draco would step before Draco knew it himself, and he'd liked it far too much. They hadn't duelled since. There was no one to come out afterwards and call them idiots and drag them inside for tea.

The lamp on the side table burned low and amber. At some point Draco's page stopped turning.

Theo looked up.

Draco had fallen asleep the way a careful person does when carefulness finally runs out. His head had tipped against the wing of the chair and his mouth had softened. The book lay open on his thigh, one long hand still spread across the page to keep her place. In sleep the lines between his eyebrows had gone, the ones Theo thought of as permanent. The firelight caught his hair and turned it gold at the edges and almost white at the crown, and threw the shadows of his lashes down across his cheekbones. There was a cut across his knuckles, healing badly, that he'd got on the night in the forest and never let anyone see to. He'd knelt in the dirt wearing a monster's face, put his coat over her, and watched her flinch from his hands because they weren't his hands.

Theo let himself look. Just this once, while there was no one to see.

He hated her a little for this. It was a small, ugly, honest thing, and he made himself hold it instead of pretending it wasn't there. He hated Jackie a little for building a life with both of them in it and then leaving him alone in it with *him*. For putting Draco across the breakfast table and down the hall and in the chair by the fire, a hand's width away and further off than London. For making him share everything with the one person he'd have given the rest away to keep.

Draco infuriated him. The exact bread squares, the "I am aware, Nott", the way he picked the cruellest true thing and said it in his politest voice. *You were down the hall.* As if Theo didn't know. As if Theo didn't lie awake with it every night.

And every time Draco cut him, Theo only fell harder. It was humiliating. He'd made his peace with a lot of humiliating things, and he couldn't make peace with this one.

The fire settled with a sigh of sparks. Somewhere in the house Caramel was moving about softly, putting things where Jackie would want them.

Theo checked the bond one more time. Grey. Far away. A lamp left on at the end of a long road.

He didn't wake Draco. He didn't cover him either. That felt like a thing you had to earn, and he wasn't sure who was owed it. He banked the fire so it would last until morning, turned the lamp down but not out, and went back to his columns of numbers.

He stayed until the fire was only embers. He told himself it was the reports.
