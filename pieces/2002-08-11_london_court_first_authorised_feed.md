### Draco

The blood was cold, and it was dead, and Theo drank it anyway.

The first swallow hit like water on a burn — relief and insult in the same instant. Stored blood carried no pulse, no heat, none of the bright living current that made feeding worth the name. It was arithmetic. It went in, and the shaking in his forearms eased, and the room stopped tilting every time he moved his head. He kept both hands around the flask and drank in long, steady pulls, because gulping would have told everyone in the room exactly how far down they'd taken him, and he'd be damned if he gave London that too.

*Glass,* the Malice said. *They bled us for four days and the apology comes in glass.*

*Drink it,* Theo told Him, and did.

*I am drinking it. I'm simply noting that the warm ones are upstairs and the warmest one is across the landing, and we are here, nursing a bottle like an invalid.*

The flask stilled against Theo's mouth for a moment. Filippo's hand hovered beneath it, patient, not touching. Theo made himself finish the swallow before he answered.

*You put her on the floor,* he said. *At the door. I felt you go through me to do it.*

*I said hello.* A pause, and the voice lost some of its lazy amusement, which was worse. *I was loud. I've been hungry, Theodore. You know what that does to my manners.*

*You don't reach for her again until I can hold the line. Not a word, not a push. Nothing until she opens that door herself.*

*You can't feel her,* the Malice said, almost gently. *I can. You're asking me to stand between you and the only part of her either of us can touch, and hold my breath.* A beat. *Ask me how she is. You want to.*

He did want to. It sat in his chest like a swallowed stone, the wanting. He could see her at the door in his mind — her voice through the wood, the thud, the silence afterward — and he had no thread of his own to follow her by. Death had cut it. What was left of the bond lived in the thing riding his skull, and the thing riding his skull had already hurt her with it once tonight.

*No,* Theo said. *Not from you. She'll tell me herself.*

The Malice didn't argue. That was the fed blood working — He was quieter now, settling back from the front of Theo's mind like a tide going out over stones. But Theo felt Him turn, once, deliberate and small where before He had been an avalanche, and send down the bond a single measured thing, the way a man leaves a card at a door: *Fed now, Little Wolf. Softer.*

Theo couldn't stop it. He noted that fact and set it aside with all the others he'd deal with when he could stand.

He lowered the empty flask. Filippo took it from him without comment.

By the door, Draco had not moved. He had watched the whole feed with his shoulders against the wood, and what he noticed first was the colour — not returning, exactly, since Theo had no colour to return, but the grey receding, the skin going from paper to marble. The eyes were the clearer measure. When the door had opened they'd been crimson to the edge of the iris, and Draco had catalogued that the way he catalogued everything he couldn't afford to react to. Now the red was thinning, pale blue coming up underneath like ice under running water. He found he had been holding a breath's worth of tension in his jaw and released it carefully.

Nell moved in as soon as the flask left Theo's hands. She unwound the marked dressing without asking, examined what was beneath it, and her mouth flattened in a way Draco filed for later. She checked the other arm, tilted Theo's chin toward the light with two fingers, watched his eyes.

"Grip," she said, offering her hand. Theo took it and squeezed, and something in her expression conceded a point. "You'll stand tonight. You won't fight anyone, and you shouldn't try stairs alone." She began redressing the arm, quick and neat. "Whether you leave this building is the Seneschal's question, and Viola's. Not mine. I only answer for whether you'd survive the walk."

"Then answer a different one." Theo's voice was steadier than it had been through the door — lower, evener, his own. He looked past her to Draco. "Can she stand to be near me. You've been with her. Not what she wants — what she can bear."

Draco held his gaze. There were a dozen careful sentences available and he discarded them all, because Theo had asked the exact question and deserved the exact answer.

"I don't know," he said. "It cost her to be at your door. I'm not going to guess what it costs her now, and I'm not going to promise anything on her behalf. I'll ask her." He straightened off the door. "That's all I'll do. Ask."

Theo nodded once, slowly, and did not look away, and Draco understood that the nod was the hardest thing in the room.

He let himself out. The landing was cold and quiet, Filippo's low voice continuing behind him through the wood — something to Theo about Florence, about time, none of it his to settle tonight. Draco crossed to the opposite door, lifted his own muffling charm with a short pass of his wand, and opened it.
