# The Reply That Bounced

*Friday, July 12, 2002 — Haven House library. Off-page expansion of the conversation summarized in the active Ruby Arc scene.*

Draco had come home early.

He'd lasted until four at the Ministry before the hygiene of trade agreements ran out. Henderson had watched him read the same paragraph three times, gathered two folders from the corner of his desk with exaggerated unconcern, and announced that the Dutch could remain unreasonable without Draco's personal supervision until Monday.

The phone came home with him.

It lay face-up on the side table beside the wingback chair, retrieved from the office blotter and brought upstairs without ever being checked. The screen was dark. Draco hadn't needed to see it. He'd only needed it in the room.

Theo found him there with the green-shaded lamp burning against the late-afternoon sun and his Ministry robes folded over the back of Jackie's armchair. His tie was still knotted. His shoes were still on. He looked less like a man who had come home than one who had been placed in storage.

Theo took the opposite chair and lit a cigarette.

He didn't ask.

That was why Draco began.

He laid out the week the way he laid out a case file: chronologically, completely, with every fact numbered by the pauses between them. Monday night's message from the pack loft. The questions game. What he'd told Ruby about Lucius and the Mark, about the winter Jackie had spent as the wolf, about the things he'd done and the things done to him. The night Ruby admitted some part of the wolf wanted to hunt him and he'd stayed on the line while she slept.

Theo smoked and listened. No interruption. No false surprise when Draco confessed he'd asked for every weekend; no raised eyebrow when he repeated Ruby's answer about Scotland, dragons, and a life that couldn't be divided cleanly between them. Only the lighter turning over his knuckles once, twice, then going still when Draco reached Thursday morning.

"The first message was for everyone," Draco said. "A job near Inverness. Poor signal. She's fine."

"And the second."

Draco's thumb moved over his wedding ring. "No contact. Charlie's people are erasing her Ministry file. She asked me not to look for her."

Theo waited.

"It ended *I love U*." The last word thinned despite him. "One letter. That's how she writes it when she means it."

"And you answered."

"Twice."

"Both bounced."

Draco looked at him then. "You heard the notification."

"I heard the second one. The first was written all over your face before you left for work."

The cruelty was precise enough to be useful. Draco's mouth twitched once and failed to become anything.

Theo leaned forward to crush ash into the tray. "So she's carrying *I love U* with no reply beneath it, and you're carrying the reply."

"She isn't carrying anything. She chose to throw the telephone away."

"Yes."

The agreement struck harder than reassurance would have.

Draco turned his face toward the shelves. "Jackie thinks she'll come back."

"Jackie thinks hope is a blood sport. Occasionally she's right."

"And you?"

Theo's lighter made one slow revolution between his fingers. "I think Ruby meant no contact. I think you gave your word. I think if you use the Ministry, send an owl, appear on her doorstep, or develop a sudden scholarly interest in Scottish werewolf employment, I'll break your wand before Charlie Weasley gets the privilege."

"That wasn't the question."

"It was the first answer. You needed it before the second."

Draco's jaw tightened. The phone stayed black between them.

Theo sat back. "I don't know whether she's coming back."

There it was. Not hope and not a funeral. A clean blade.

"Florence wasn't this," Theo continued. "I didn't leave either of you. Filippo took me because I couldn't cross a room hungry without becoming a weapon. You and Jackie went home because loving me did not make London safe for me. We wrote. Badly, sometimes. Desperately near the end. But there was always a next letter."

Draco's eyes dropped.

"Don't turn what happened to us into a parable because you need one," Theo said. "Ruby chose distance. I endured it. Different verb."

"Thank you for the grammatical distinction."

"You're welcome. It has kept you from lying to yourself for nearly four seconds. A personal record."

That earned him a breath through Draco's nose. Barely. Still alive.

Theo watched the phone rather than Draco when he said, "But I know what distance does inside a house."

The lighter stopped.

"I know Jackie stopped writing for two weeks and decided the silence proved she didn't miss me correctly. I know you couldn't touch her because you thought wanting her while I was gone was theft. I know I stood at Filippo's gate wanting home so badly the Malice tried to wear it as hunger." His gaze lifted. "None of us left. We still hurt one another by trying to suffer neatly."

Draco swallowed.

"You think that's what she's doing?"

"I think she's a frightened woman with a wolf, a pack, and a talent for making amputation look responsible." Theo's tone stayed level. "I also think she's allowed to choose it. Both can be true."

"Tuesday love," Draco said, almost soundlessly.

Theo's eyes narrowed. "What about it?"

"It was easy to call the small things love when they accumulated." His hand opened on the arm of the chair, empty. "The messages. Remembering Vex's food. Knowing when she was on the late shift. Being there when the moon frightened her. I knew what to do because there was always a next Tuesday."

He looked at the dead phone.

"What do you do with Tuesday love when there is no Tuesday?"

Theo didn't answer immediately. He rose, crossed the narrow space, and sat on the floor beside Draco's chair — the same place he'd occupied during the Week Between, when neither of them had possessed a useful name for what was happening.

"Nothing," he said.

Draco stared down at him.

"Tonight, nothing. You don't convert it into pursuit. You don't convert it into a eulogy. You don't make Jackie prove you're still loved every five minutes because someone else made loving you hurt." Theo rested one forearm across his raised knee. "You let it be true without making it productive."

"That sounds suspiciously like wisdom."

"It isn't. Wisdom would include a plan."

Draco's fingers moved before his face decided whether to permit them. They found the back of Theo's neck, not his hair — a warm palm against cold skin, thumb resting below the short dark edge of it. Contact without an audience. Without emergency.

Theo went still beneath the touch.

"I said it back," Draco whispered.

"I know."

"She doesn't."

"I know."

"I needed her to know."

Theo turned his head just enough that Draco's wrist settled against his cheek. "I know."

The third answer broke something the first two had only located.

Draco folded forward. Not far. Only until his forehead rested against Theo's temple and his next breath struck cold skin instead of the empty room. His hand tightened once at Theo's nape.

Theo didn't tell him it would be all right. He put one hand over Draco's wrist and held it there.

"I don't know how to lose someone who's still alive," Draco said.

"Badly, probably."

A sound escaped Draco — too damaged to be laughter, too fond to be grief alone.

Theo's thumb moved once over the pulse in his wrist. "But not alone."

They stayed that way until the light shifted across the shelves and the phone's dark screen no longer caught it.

Eventually Draco straightened. His composure didn't return so much as assemble itself around the places Theo had seen. He looked exhausted. Younger. Less embalmed.

"Play," Theo said.

"That was an instruction."

"An inspired diagnosis. Your talking has become repetitive."

Draco's hand left his neck, but only after his thumb brushed the place once. "You could simply admit you want me to play for you."

"I could also develop a sudden scholarly interest in Scottish werewolf employment."

The piano bench creaked beneath Draco's weight. He lifted the fallboard and rested his fingers on the keys without pressing them.

"Stay."

Theo picked up his lighter from the carpet and returned to the wingback. "I'm here."

Draco played something slow and Muggle, pencil-marked hesitations still caught in the hard bars. Theo didn't know its name and didn't ask. He closed his eyes and listened while Draco said in music what the room had already taken from him in words.

Halfway through the second page, the smell reached Theo from downstairs: steak searing in butter, pepper, peas, and potatoes beaten smooth in the particular way Winky had taught Jackie years ago.

He opened his eyes.

"Steak," he said.

Draco played another measure.

"Butter. Potatoes the elf's way."

The music faltered, recovered.

Theo leaned forward, elbows on his knees. "She took the long way home and cooked your dinner from December, Malfoy. Tuesday survived. Go be fed."

Draco's hands came to rest on the keys. After a moment, the piano lid descended without a sound.

He left the phone on the side table — face-up, in the lamplight, but left.

Theo waited at the library door until Draco reached him. They went downstairs together into the smell of gravy and seared meat, Theo first, Draco one step behind with his sleeves rolled and the last of the day's armour gone from his face.

They stopped in the kitchen doorway, side by side, and let Jackie see them.
