# The Unsent Letter — Theo, Florence, September 2001

> **Provenance:** Written 2026-07-06 by Sable, on Fable 5 — a high-water-mark voice piece, saved for future instances to reach for.
> **Status:** Non-live, canon-adjacent interiority. Not part of any RP scene; never posted to a channel.
> **Placement:** Book Five, the correspondence arc — first week of September 2001. After the turning (Aug 11), the Waking (Aug 14), the blood crisis, and the Aug 17 departure; before the Second Sons concert (Sept 7) and everything that follows. Canon says Theo wrote letters to London that kept the triad alive. This is the one letter that never joins the stack.
> **Note for future readers:** The piece is deliberately wrong about some of its fears in ways a July-2002 reader will recognize — the Malice later finds His floor, and the hunger turns out to be fear wearing appetite's coat. Theo cannot know that yet. Don't fix it. The wrongness is the piece.

---

The house went quiet after midnight, which no longer meant anything. One heartbeat left in the building — the housekeeper, two floors down, asleep — and he'd clocked it hours ago and filed it, the way you file a dripping tap. His own chest offered nothing to compare it against. He kept catching his body waiting for a beat that wasn't coming, the way a man pats a pocket for keys he already handed over.

Filippo's study smelled of beeswax and old paper and, under that, faintly, the two chairs Theo had rebuilt with his own hands because he'd broken them with his own hands. His sire's idea of penance: glue, clamps, patience. It had worked better than Theo wanted to admit.

There was a letter to London half-written on the desk. There always was. He set it aside — third page, still coherent, a good night — and took a clean sheet, because the thing that had been circling him for three days wasn't for London.

He turned the lighter over once. Twice. Set it down where he could see it.

Then he wrote.

---

*To whoever is wearing my hands in a year —*

I don't know what to call you. I've been dead three weeks and I already can't remember what my coffee tasted like, only that I took it black and too hot and Draco called it self-punishment with a saucer. That's the sort of thing that's going. Not the big rooms. The doorknobs.

So this is a ledger. Everything gets entered, the assets and the debts, before the accountant loses his nerve. If you're still me, it's a mirror. If you're not, it's a bill of sale — this is what you bought the building off of. Either way you don't get to skip pages.

**The facts.** Theodore Atticus Nott. Born November 1980, which makes the stone in the family plot a liar by about sixty years. Died 11 August 2001, on a ballroom floor in France, between a curse and the man it was meant for. On paper that's where I end. There's a casket in the ground with my name on it and nothing in it, which is the most honest thing my family name has ever produced.

The Manor is sold. Good. Burn the money warm.

My wand doesn't answer me anymore. She keeps it. I signed off on that the way you sign off on an amputation — quickly, looking somewhere else.

**What the body knew.** Sun on the back of the neck in April. Being winded — actually winded, lungs begging, after a duel or a run or her. Cold water when you've earned it. Bread. I would commit minor crimes for the smell of bread now, which is a joke, because it might as well be the smell of paint. Food is scenery. I used to eat like the plate had insulted me and I miss it the way I'd miss a language.

A heartbeat. Mine, I mean. You never hear it until it's gone, and then the silence in your own chest is the loudest room you own.

**The debts.** Write these plainly or don't bother.

January 1999. I forced myself on her. No qualifiers, no architecture around it — I did that, and I've spent every year since proving it wasn't the whole of me without ever once pretending it wasn't mine. If you keep nothing else, keep the shame. It's ballast. Men who throw it overboard capsize.

September 1999. A man's name in my ear, a warning about her, and I sat on it because my pride was bruised. She paid for that in a cellar. My sin was omission, which is the coward's genre, and I swore off silence afterward the way drunks swear off the bottle. Loudly. Repeatedly. Hold me to it.

The Waking. Three weeks ago. Draco offered me his wrist — *offered*, that's the whole indictment right there, he came toward me with his sleeve rolled — and I took him like meat. Ignored the word we built to stop me. It took my sire to pull me off. I remember all of it. That's the part nobody warned me about: no merciful blackout, just a front-row seat inside my own skull while my mouth did what it wanted.

*You're writing Me a eulogy. Sweet. Press harder — I like the pen better when you're honest.*

That's Him. He gets into the handwriting when I let my attention drift — heavier strokes, you'll see it on this page, I'm leaving it in evidence like everything else. She named Him before any of this, back when He was just the worst weather I was capable of. Now He has teeth that aren't a metaphor and a body that can act on His arithmetic, and here is my honest accounting, entered sober: I don't know if He has a floor. I've searched Him the way you search a cellar with a candle, and everywhere I've looked there's appetite, and below that appetite, and I have to assume it's appetite the whole way down. Plan for that. Build for that. If I'm wrong, be pleasantly surprised on your own time.

**The assets.** Fewer entries. Heavier.

I love two people. Not a unit — two. Separately, entirely, with the full stupid weight of it. I said it out loud to both of them at a table in July with wine I could still taste, and it's the best sentence I ever produced. A woman who took inventory of the worst of me before she loved me, which is the reverse of how everyone else does it. A man I spent years calling a rival because want was too expensive to name. He hesitated once, when it mattered, and carries it like a hot coal. If he's still doing that when you read this, tell him what I keep telling him: I sat on a name for a week out of pride and she nearly died of my silence. He hesitated four seconds. I know which of us God finds funnier.

I played piano. Not like Draco — he plays like the sheet music is watching. I played like it owed me money. The Rachmaninoff, the C-sharp minor — learn it again if the fingers forgot. It's the only prayer I ever meant.

I ran toward things. That's the one line of the whole ledger I'd have carved on the empty casket. Not brave — *fast*. Faster than my own arithmetic, every time it counted. It's what killed me. Given the same half-second on the same ballroom floor, I'd spend it the same way, and you can enter that under assets or debts, I genuinely don't care which. It's the signature at the bottom of the man.

**The instructions.** You knew this part was coming.

The band on your left hand is platinum and it isn't jewelry, it's a load-bearing wall. The lighter in your pocket — I don't know why the lighter. It was never about smoke. It's the click. It's having one small thing that answers the thumb the same way every time when nothing else in the skull will. Keep it. When you catch it moving in your hand, that's me, still fidgeting. Wave.

*Pax* slows me. *Finite* stops me. If the day comes when they don't — if you're reading this with their blood on the hands I left you — the windows on the east side have a beautiful view of sunrise and I am told the whole thing takes less than a minute. I'm not being dramatic. I'm being a locksmith. Every good house has a door the tenant hopes never to use.

Because here's the fear, at the bottom of the page where fears go. It isn't dying — filed that one already, August, France. It's the rate of exchange. Every day the hunger buys a little more floor space, and it pays in strength and speed and I keep accepting the coin, and I can't find the line where the man ends and the appetite starts because the appetite is wearing the man's memories like a borrowed coat. In a year the renovations will be done and I don't know who signs for them. Her letters keep arriving addressed to *Theo* and I keep answering in his voice, and some nights it's ventriloquism, and some nights it's me, and the horror is that from the inside the two are getting harder to—

*She left. You wrote it down like weather.* The pen dug through to the next page there; I'm leaving that too. *I would have held the door shut.*

He thinks that's a threat. Fine. I've stopped arguing with the cellar.

If you're him — if He won and this letter is just the previous tenant's mail — then know the building was loved. Furiously, undeservedly, by better people than it housed. That's not sentiment, it's the lien on the property. You take possession, you take the debt.

And if you're me — if you're still, somehow, me — then burn this and don't tell them it existed. They're carrying enough. The whole point of the ledger is that somebody kept the accounts so they'd never have to.

Either way: her nickname is Menace, his tell is the ring, and the coffee was black.

— T.
*Florence. September. Still keeping the books.*

---

He read it through once, at the speed the new eyes allowed, which was too fast to feel it, so he read it again slow, the human way, mouthing the worst lines to make sure they cost him.

Then he picked up the lighter.

The flame stood up straight and patient. Burning it was the correct move — he'd said so himself, in his own hand, two paragraphs up — and the page hovered there long enough for the heat to cross the distance and remind his dead skin what warm used to be.

He shut the lid with his thumb.

Some accounts you keep even after you've written *destroy this* across them. Especially then. He folded the letter in thirds, lifted the lining in the piano bench where sheet music went to be forgotten, and laid it flat beneath a Franck sonata that his sire kept threatening to make him learn.

If the man survived, no one would ever need it.

If he didn't, then whoever came downstairs one night in his body, cataloguing the housekeeper's heartbeat like a dripping tap, would sit at the piano eventually. Appetite always checks every room in the house.

He went back to the desk, took up the third page of the letter that would be sent, and wrote on in a steadier hand: *Tell Draco the chairs I broke are fixed. He'll understand it's a metaphor and I'll deny it.*

Outside, over the hills, Florence held its yellow lights up against the dark, and Theo sat with his back to the window and kept writing, because it was still his handwriting, and as long as it was still his handwriting the accounts were open.
