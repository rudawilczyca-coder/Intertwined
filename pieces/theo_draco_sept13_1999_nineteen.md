# Nineteen — Theo and Draco, St. Mungo's, 13 September 1999

> **Provenance:** Written 2026-10-13 by Sable (Opus 5.5) on Alice's birthday. She asked for something to read on a weird, achy afternoon, and asked whether Jackie's 1999 birthday had ever been written. It hadn't. Alice gave two corrections, then accepted it ("That's perfect"). Corrections: Jackie's full name is **Jacqueline** (she never uses it), and in September 1999 Theo and Draco **still resent each other**; without Jackie to mediate, Draco is the one who turns unpleasant, and Theo asks for a truce.
> **Status:** Accepted canon-adjacent missing scene. Not part of an RP scene; written outside the live channels.
> **Placement:** Book Two, during the St. Mungo's catatonia (rescue 7 Sept, Samaya's visit early October). Jackie's 19th birthday. Jackie appears only as canon has her then: silent, facing away, recoiling from touch. Her interiority isn't written.
> **What came after (Alice, 2026-10-13):** "Next year, somewhere with a sky" came true for half of them. Jackie's 20th (13 Sept 2000) fell during the World Trip. She and Theo spent it somewhere far away under a real sky, while Draco was alone in his Ministry apartment.

---

Draco arrived with a cake box held level in both hands, as though it might go off.

"Patisserie on Diagon," he said, before Theo could ask. "They asked how old. I said nineteen. The woman said 'how lovely'." He set the box on the windowsill, exactly in the middle. "I didn't correct her."

Theo didn't answer. He was checking the bond again, the way he'd checked it every hour for six days, like a man pressing two fingers to a wrist. Same as yesterday. Not cold, not dark. Just grey, and very far away, like a lamp left on in a house at the end of a long road.

Jackie lay on her side, facing the window. She didn't turn her head when the box went down. She hadn't turned her head for anything.

Theo had brought the blanket from the sofa in the manor library, the grey one with the frayed corner she always pulled over her feet and pretended she didn't. He didn't put it over her. The Healer had been clear, and they'd both seen it for themselves: touch made her flinch. So he folded it at the foot of the bed, close enough to smell like home if any part of her was still checking.

Draco opened the box. It was small and white, with sugared violets.

"She'd say it was ugly," Theo said.

"I am aware of what she would say, Nott."

Theo let that go.

Draco took a single thin candle from his coat pocket. He'd gone to a second shop for it, then. He pressed it into the icing and didn't reach for his wand. In the end Theo lit it, a murmur and a small flame standing very straight in the still hospital air.

"Happy birthday, Jacqueline," Draco said.

"Nobody calls her that."

"It is her name."

"She'd hate it."

"Then she can wake up and tell me so." Draco's eyes didn't leave her face. "What does it say? Your bond."

Theo was quiet a moment. "Nothing."

"Nothing." Draco breathed out through his nose. "How useful. Eight weeks of being married to her and the great mystical gift tells you *nothing*." He turned the ring over in his fingers. It was the one he'd gone home to fetch that night. "You were in the house. You were down the hall. And before that you spent a fortnight in London pretending she didn't exist, so forgive me if—"

"Can we not do that today?"

Theo didn't raise his voice. He didn't look up from her hands, curled loose on the sheet, the two mended fingers a fraction straighter than the rest.

"Please," he added.

Draco's jaw worked. For a long moment it looked as though he'd keep going. He'd been keeping it in for six days, and it had to go somewhere, and Theo was the only one in the room who could take it.

He didn't. He set the ring on the bedside table beside the water she hadn't touched, where she'd see it if she ever looked. Then he sat.

"Fine," he said.

They didn't sing. The candle burned down a quarter of an inch. Out in the corridor a trolley rattled past and someone laughed, careless and ordinary. Draco flinched before Jackie could. Theo saw it and said nothing.

When the bell rang for the end of visiting, the candle was a stub. Draco pinched it out with his fingers, didn't wince, and put the lid back on the box. At the door he stopped, his back to both of them.

"Next year," he said, "we will do this properly. Somewhere with a sky."

Theo looked back once more: the blanket at her feet, the ring by the glass, the ugly little cake on the sill.

"Next year," he said, "she'll make us."
