### Draco

Draco had been pulling the chair away from the desk. He stopped with it halfway across the floor, then set it down carefully so the legs would not scrape again. For a moment he looked at her without answering. He had understood every word. It was putting them together with the closed door across the landing that took him time.

He went back to their own door and shut it. A Muffliato, then another careful pass around the frame; the familiar movement steadied his hand, though he had no way of testing it against whoever might be listening outside. He returned to the bed and sat on the chair, close enough that he could speak quietly without leaning over her.

"In the tunnel," he said. "When you caught my arm."

He remembered waiting on the stairs, lowering his light because he thought it might be in her eyes. She had said not now, and he had let it go. Since then he had watched her stand through the audience, heard her refuse Florence, followed her downstairs. He had been angry with Filippo, frightened for Theo, occupied with getting somebody to open a door. All that time there had been something else happening beside him.

"And just now. That was Him?"

His gaze moved towards the wall. He could no longer hear Filippo, which was what he had intended, but he disliked the silence almost immediately. Theo was still on the other side of the landing. Draco had no idea whether the distance between these rooms mattered to a bond that had survived things neither of them understood. He turned back before he could start treating the wall as something he could usefully watch.

"I thought you were going to faint." He rubbed a hand over his mouth. "I was looking for a bite."

It sounded stupid now. It hadn't been stupid when he did it, and that was worse: he'd been trying to work out whether somebody had hurt her while the person responsible was locked behind a door. He set his wand across his knees rather than put it away. There was nothing here to point it at.

He wanted to ask why she hadn't told him upstairs. The question reached the back of his teeth before he remembered Viola looking from one wedding ring to the other, deciding which of their attachments might be useful. He would not have wanted her to hear this either. Still, he wished there had been a moment on the stairs, a word he could have understood. He had told Theo they were both outside.

Draco reached out and laid his hand palm-up on the blanket beside her, leaving it there.

"I won't tell Nell," he said. "Or anyone else, until we've talked." He kept his voice low beneath the muffling charm. "But I need to understand what you mean. Are you feeling what He's feeling, or is He actually speaking to you?"
