### Draco

Draco stopped when her hand closed around his forearm. He looked back at Jackie, lowering the wandlight so it no longer shone between them. "What is it?" he asked quietly. Behind him, Filippo had stopped too, one hand still on Leandro. The Seneschal waited on the next stair. Draco gave her a look that kept her there, then turned his arm beneath Jackie's hand, offering his palm without pulling her towards him. From above came the clink of glass and a man's brief, easy laugh. He had been listening for a cry, a struggle, anything that might be Theo. The laughter was worse.

At the top of the stairs, the Seneschal opened a door onto a broad room with shuttered windows. The carpet was thick enough to silence Draco's first step. Lamps stood beside upholstered chairs, and someone had left a newspaper folded over the arm of a sofa. It smelled of furniture polish, tobacco and blood. A woman in a green dress sat on the floor beside the sofa with one shoe missing. Her head rested against the cushion near a vampire's knee. He was reading the newspaper. When he turned a page, it brushed her hair; neither of them moved away.

Draco remembered Rome. He had not forgotten any of it, but he had allowed the memory to remain there, among people he could leave behind. Across this room, a man offered his wrist before the vampire beside him had finished setting down her glass. He smiled when she took it. Draco looked for something that would tell him whether the smile meant anything, then hated himself for looking. There would be no useful distinction visible from the doorway. Another mortal lay curled beneath a coat on a chaise longue. The coat was expensive. His bare feet hung over its edge.

"This way," the Seneschal said. A young-looking vampire moved his chair to let her pass, leaving barely enough space between its leg and the woman on the floor. Draco went around the other side. Nobody watched him do it. Filippo's attention remained on the door beyond the sitting room; nothing in his expression suggested that their route had taken them through anything unusual. Draco wanted to catch his sleeve and make him look. Instead he put his wand away, within reach of his hand, and followed the Seneschal.

The next room held the long table. Viola sat at its far end with a file open before her, short auburn curls tucked behind one ear. She looked up at Filippo, then at Leandro's restraints and stained shirt. "You were expected back earlier." Leandro inclined his head. "There were complications." Her gaze rested on the ropes a moment longer. "I can see that." She closed the file and addressed Filippo. "You have finally come to discuss your childe."

"I have come to see him." Filippo stopped beside the nearest chair without sitting. "And to discuss why I learned of his detention from his family." Viola laid her hand flat on the file. "Your family proved considerably easier to reach than you did." She glanced towards a side door. "Nell. Attend to Velluti, please. Here." The woman who came through was small, with straight black hair brushing her shoulders and red eyes that went immediately to Leandro's side. She carried a case, which she set on an empty chair. "Sit down," she told him. "Unless you'd prefer me to work through the ropes standing up."

Filippo drew out a chair for Leandro and kept his hand on its back while he sat. Nell bent to examine the torn shirt without loosening the restraints. Draco stayed standing. He could still hear the sitting room through the open door, the newspaper rustling, a low conversation continuing while Theo was somewhere beneath or beyond them. "Where is he?" he asked. Viola looked at him properly for the first time. Her eyes passed over his face, his clothes, and stopped briefly at his wedding ring. "Mr Malfoy. You will be taken to him when we have settled the purpose of your visit."

"The purpose is to take him home." Filippo's fingers tightened once on the chair back, but Draco did not look at him. Viola turned her attention towards Jackie. "And is that your position as well, Mrs Nott-Malfoy, or have you come prepared to discuss the conditions under which he might remain there?"
