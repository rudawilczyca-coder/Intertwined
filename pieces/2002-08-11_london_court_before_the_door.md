### Draco

Nell stopped a few paces short of the stairs. She held her case against her leg and looked back at them. "They've been bleeding him to keep him weak. I close the wounds afterwards and check that he's still responsive." Draco had been walking quickly enough that he had to catch himself before he crowded her. She did not step away. "He damaged the containment when they first detained him. This is how they've kept him from trying again."

For a moment Draco could hear the room they had left, someone moving a chair, Viola speaking too quietly for him to distinguish the words. "And you've been feeding him?" he asked. Nell nodded. "Enough to keep him lucid. Not enough to restore his strength." She said it as an explanation, and he waited for something in her expression to acknowledge what she had explained. Her attention went instead to Filippo, who had stopped beside him. "He needs more before anyone attempts to move him."

"Then give him more," Filippo said. Nell shifted the case to her other hand. "That changes how securely he can be held. I'll need permission." Draco looked back towards the audience room. They had just stood in it discussing Theo's suitability for work while somebody decided how much blood he could have. He had known there would be conditions. He had imagined restrictions on where Theo went, whom he saw, things they could argue about without anyone having to measure out enough of him to remain conscious.

Filippo's voice brought him back. "Ask for it. Tell Viola I will remain with him while he feeds." Nell considered him briefly, then nodded. "I'll send word from downstairs." She resumed walking, taking them down a narrow flight with a handrail polished smooth by use. Draco kept his hand off it. The scar at his wrist had begun to feel tight beneath his cuff; he pulled the fabric clear and immediately wished he hadn't. He knew what had happened the last time he'd mistaken offering himself for knowing how to help.

At the bottom, Nell stopped outside a closed door. Light showed beneath it, and a chair stood against the opposite wall. Nothing about the arrangement told Draco whether Theo had spent his time sitting up, lying down, or watching that strip of light from the other side. Nell put her case on the chair. "Before we go in—is there anything unusual about how he feeds that I should know? He's been able to take what I've given him, but he hasn't had much."

Filippo looked at Draco. It was brief, and it was enough. "Jackie," Draco said, before he could find a way to explain without saying her name like a warning. He turned back to Nell. "Her blood. It used to be difficult for him to stop." The admission brought heat into his face. Theo had worked so hard not to be spoken about like this. "He's learned since then." Nell's hand left the door. She asked neither for a demonstration nor how they had learned to manage it. "Then we give him something else first."

From inside came a small sound, fabric shifting against fabric. Draco stopped watching Nell. He moved closer to the door without touching it.

"Theo?"

There was a pause long enough that he looked at her again. Then he heard his name, low and unmistakable, and the relief made him put his palm flat against the wood.

"I'm here," Draco said. "We're both here. Filippo's with us."

Another movement, followed by silence. When Theo spoke again, the words were clearer, as though he'd made himself sit up to say them.

"Don't open it yet."
