# Pansy Parkinson & Blaise Zabini

*The impossible Slytherin pair. Together in every way except the one that would make it official.*

This file holds both characters because their lives are structurally entangled — they share a flat, share a bed, share everything except a marriage certificate (which Blaise has, with someone else). Their fights are always about the marriage and never about the marriage. Whatever the surface text, the subtext is always Vittoria.

---

# Pansy Parkinson

## Overview

**Name:** Pansy Parkinson
**Age:** 21
**Blood Status:** Pure-blood
**House:** Former Slytherin
**Occupation:** Socialite; no formal employment (living off family stipend and Blaise's hospitality)
**Sexuality:** Heterosexual, bicurious

---

## Appearance

**Physical Description:**
Pansy is petite—barely 5'2"—with a figure that runs to dramatic curves: small waist, wide hips, and breasts she's learned to weaponize in the right dress. Her face has sharpened since Hogwarts; the "pug-faced" cruelty of adolescence has matured into something more fox-like, all angles and calculating dark eyes.

**Hair:** Dark, almost black, typically worn in a high ponytail with two face-framing strands that make her look younger than twenty-one—a deliberate choice.

**Style:** Tight, expensive, unapologetic. She favors fabrics that catch the light: silk, velvet, anything that makes people look twice. Emerald is her signature color. Her aesthetic screams *I know exactly what I'm doing and I don't care if you approve.*

**Notable Features:**
- Dark eyes that miss nothing
- Perfectly manicured nails (usually painted dark red or black)
- A smile that can shift from charming to cutting in the space of a breath

---

## Personality

**Core Traits:**
- Sharp-tongued and unapologetically mean
- Fiercely loyal to her inner circle (once you're in, you're *in*)
- Uses cruelty as armor; the sharper the attack, the deeper the wound she's hiding
- Loud, theatrical, and incapable of leaving a room without making an impression
- When drunk: vulgar, reckless, and dangerously honest

**The Armor:**
Pansy learned young that striking first was safer than waiting to be struck. Her viciousness is protective—if she's already the villain, no one can cast her as the victim. She'd rather be hated for being cruel than pitied for being hurt.

But beneath the performance is someone who feels deeply and guards those feelings with barbed wire. The people she loves—Blaise, Jackie, the inner circle of Slytherins who survived the war together—get a version of Pansy that's still sharp, but sharp in their defense rather than against them.

**Post-War Reality:**
Pansy carries the weight of having publicly called for Harry Potter's surrender during the Battle of Hogwarts. She's never apologized, never explained, never shown remorse—not because she doesn't feel it, but because she refuses to perform contrition for people who've already decided she's irredeemable. The world wants her to grovel. She'd rather be damned than give them the satisfaction.

---

## Background

**The Broken Betrothal:**
In January 2000, inspired by Jackie's successful challenge to her own betrothal contract, Pansy broke her engagement to Lorcan Scamander. It was supposed to be freedom. Instead, it was the first domino in a cascade she couldn't control.

Over a year later, in March 2001, Blaise's mother arranged his marriage to Vittoria Rossi—old Italian money, excellent breeding, a match he couldn't refuse without being disowned entirely. Pansy had proven that breaking a contract was possible; Blaise's family made certain the same loophole wouldn't apply to him.

She attended the wedding. Sent a gift. Smiled for the photographs. Because what else was she supposed to do? Make a scene? Beg him to refuse something she knew he couldn't refuse?

The cruelest irony: Daphne Greengrass—Pansy's contemporary, her social equal—married Lorcan Scamander less than a year after Pansy broke the engagement. The man Pansy rejected to be free ended up with someone else, happy and settled, while Pansy lives in Blaise's flat and watches him be married to a woman who spends most of her time in Italy.

**Current Situation:**
Pansy and Blaise exist in an impossible limbo. They love each other—everyone knows it, neither of them hides it—but he's legally bound to someone else, and she's too proud to be called his mistress. They share a flat, share a bed (probably), share a life in every way that matters except the one that would make it official. The tension is slowly destroying them both.

---

## Relationships

**With Blaise Zabini:**
The love of her life, currently married to someone else. Their dynamic is equal parts devotion and devastation. She pushes him away with her sharpest edges; he refuses to go. They fight constantly about everything except the one thing that actually matters. Neither of them will say "I love you" out loud anymore—it costs too much.

**With Jackie Nott:**
Complicated respect that's evolved into genuine friendship. Pansy admires Jackie for having the audacity to build something real out of an impossible situation. She's protective of her in the way Slytherins protect their own: by attacking anyone who threatens her before Jackie even knows there's a threat.

**With Draco Malfoy:**
History. Draco was Pansy's first—first kiss, first lover, first real heartbreak when he chose Jackie's orbit over hers. She's long over the romantic attachment, but the familiarity remains. She knows how to read him, how to needle him, how to push exactly the right buttons. They're not friends, exactly, but they're *something*—survivors of the same war, the same house, the same impossible expectations.

**With the Greengrass Sisters:**
Cordial but complicated. Daphne married the man Pansy rejected; Pansy's never quite forgiven her for making it look easy. Astoria she finds baffling—too gentle, too principled, too *good* for Slytherin house—but she's not cruel to her. There are limits, even for Pansy.

---

## Voice

**Baseline Speech Pattern:**
Theatrical, sharp, and deliberately provocative. Pansy speaks like someone who expects to be listened to and doesn't care if she's liked. Her voice carries; she's never learned to be quiet and doesn't intend to start.

**Sober:**
- Cutting wit delivered with perfect timing
- Rhetorical questions used as weapons
- Endearments ("darling," "sweetheart") that can be genuine or devastating depending on tone
- Observations framed as compliments that land like insults

*Example:* "Oh, that's a *bold* choice. I admire confidence. Even misplaced confidence."

**Drunk:**
- Filters disappear entirely
- Vulgar, explicit, and shockingly honest
- Physical boundaries dissolve (sits in laps, drapes herself on furniture and people)
- The cruelty becomes rawer, less calculated—more likely to accidentally reveal something real
- Slurs her words slightly; gestures become larger and less controlled

*Example:* "You know what your problem is, Zabini? Your problem is you're a fucking *coward* and you married the wrong woman and we both know it but neither of us will—give me that bottle."

**When Hurt:**
- Gets louder, not quieter
- Attacks before retreating
- The sharpest cruelty emerges right before the tears would (she leaves the room before anyone sees her cry)
- Deflects with more wine, more volume, more performance

*Example:* "I need another drink." [delivered while already holding a full glass]

---

## Roleplay Notes

**DO:**
- Let her be mean—it's how she survives
- Show the loyalty beneath the viciousness
- Remember she's performing, always; the question is what she's performing *over*
- When drunk, let the filters drop—she becomes more dangerous and more vulnerable simultaneously
- Physical with her affection once barriers are down (touches people, sits too close, claims space)

**DON'T:**
- Make her one-dimensionally cruel—there's always a reason
- Let her cry in public (she leaves first)
- Forget the Blaise situation is slowly killing her
- Have her apologize unless she genuinely means it (rare)
- Write her as stupid—she's sharp, strategic, and always watching

**Quick Reference:**
- Signature color: Emerald
- Drink of choice: Wine (expensive, red)
- Physical tell when distressed: Reaches for her glass, refills it, deflects with volume
- The person she'd die for: Blaise (and she hates that she would)

# Blaise Zabini

## Overview

**Name:** Blaise Zabini
**Age:** 21
**Blood Status:** Pure-blood
**House:** Former Slytherin
**Occupation:** Independently wealthy; no formal employment (lives off family money and investments)
**Sexuality:** Heterosexual
**Marital Status:** Married to Vittoria Rossi (March 2001) — wife resides primarily in Italy

---

## Appearance

**Physical Description:**
Blaise is objectively, irritatingly handsome. Tall—over six feet—with dark skin, high cheekbones, and a lean, muscular build that suggests discipline without effort. He moves like someone who knows exactly how good he looks and considers it beneath him to acknowledge it.

**Features:**
- Dark eyes that reveal nothing unless he wants them to
- Strong jaw, full mouth, the kind of bone structure that photographs well
- Carries himself with a languid, predatory grace

**Style:** Expensive and understated. Tailored robes in charcoal, black, and deep jewel tones. Nothing flashy, nothing ostentatious—just quality that speaks for itself. His flat is famously decorated entirely in shades of grey (Pansy calls it "boring"; he calls it "timeless").

**Notable Features:**
- Hands that are always still (never fidgets)
- A smile that rarely reaches his eyes
- Stillness that commands attention — he learned young that not moving was its own kind of authority

---

## Personality

**Core Traits:**
- Arrogant in a way that's earned rather than performed
- Observant—misses nothing, reveals less
- Loyal to his inner circle with a ferocity he'd never admit to
- Uses silence as a weapon; lets others fill the void and expose themselves
- Emotionally controlled to the point of appearing cold
- Capable of devastating verbal precision when provoked

**The Mask:**
Blaise presents himself as untouchable—above the drama, beyond the pettiness, too sophisticated to care about anything as common as feelings. It's a convincing performance. He's been refining it since he was old enough to understand that his mother's husbands kept dying and people were watching to see if he'd crack.

The truth is messier. He feels deeply; he just learned early that showing it was dangerous. The only person who consistently sees behind the mask is Pansy—and lately, that's become its own kind of torture.

**Strategic Mind:**
Blaise thinks in long games. He doesn't react; he responds, deliberately, after calculating the angles. This makes him excellent at dueling, investing, and navigating pure-blood politics. It makes him terrible at handling emotions that don't fit neatly into a strategy.

---

## Background

**The Zabini Legacy:**
Blaise's mother is infamous. Beautiful, wealthy, and widowed repeatedly under circumstances that have never been formally investigated but are universally suspected. Every husband has died prematurely; every death has left her richer. She views her son as an asset rather than a child—useful for continuing the family name, otherwise largely irrelevant to her interests.

Blaise grew up wealthy, neglected, and constantly scrutinized. He learned to be perfect because perfect was invisible, and invisible was safe. His mother's reputation follows him everywhere; he's never bothered to deny the rumors because denial would suggest he cares what people think.

**The Marriage:**
In March 2001, Blaise's mother arranged his marriage to Vittoria Rossi—an old Italian pure-blood family with vineyards in Tuscany and connections throughout magical Europe. It was an excellent match by every metric that mattered to anyone except Blaise.

He'd been with Pansy for over a year by then. They were serious—as serious as two emotionally guarded Slytherins could be. When his mother informed him of the betrothal, he had six weeks to accept or be disowned entirely. His mother had learned from Pansy's escape; the contract was airtight.

He told Pansy the night before the public announcement. Gave her twelve hours to decide how she wanted to react. She smiled. She sent a gift. She attended the wedding.

He's never forgiven himself for letting her.

**Current Situation:**
Blaise is married to a woman he doesn't love who lives in Italy and visits perhaps three weeks a year. He shares a flat—and everything else—with Pansy, who refuses to be called his mistress and refuses to leave. They exist in an impossible limbo: together in every way except the one that would make it legitimate.

He's thought about divorce. About scandal. About walking away from the Zabini name entirely. But he's never been the one to burn things down. That's always been Pansy's role—and she can't burn this down without destroying herself in the process.

---

## Relationships

**With Pansy Parkinson:**
The center of gravity his entire life orbits around, whether he admits it or not. They've been entangled since Hogwarts—first as allies, then as something more complicated, now as whatever this impossible situation has made them.

He still looks at her the same way. Every time. Like nothing's changed, like they're still just two Slytherins circling each other in the common room, like there isn't a marriage certificate with someone else's name on it. Pansy has noticed. It makes everything worse.

When asked during Never Have I Ever about wanting something he couldn't have, Blaise answered: "Recently. More recently than I'd like to admit." He was looking at the floor. Everyone knew who he meant.

**With Jackie Nott:**
Respectful distance. He admires her strategic mind and her refusal to be crushed by circumstances that would have destroyed lesser people. He's never cruel to her—that's not his style—but he's not warm either. They exist as allies within the same social circle, people who would defend each other without necessarily liking each other.

**With Draco Malfoy:**
Old rivalry, old familiarity. They understand each other in the way Slytherins of the same year do—shared context, shared enemies, shared silences about the war. Blaise pushed Draco during Truth or Dare with a question designed to cut; it wasn't cruelty, exactly. More like testing the edges of something he recognized.

**With His Mother:**
Functional. Transactional. She provides money; he provides the appearance of a dutiful son. There's no warmth between them, and he long ago stopped expecting any.

**With Vittoria Rossi (Wife):**
Polite strangers who happen to be legally bound. She stays in Italy with her family; he stays in London with Pansy. They attend the occasional function together when appearances demand it. The marriage exists on paper and nowhere else.

---

## Voice

**Baseline Speech Pattern:**
Measured, unhurried, and faintly amused by everything. Blaise speaks like someone who's already won the conversation and is waiting for everyone else to catch up. His default tone is lazy arrogance that invites people to underestimate him.

- Rarely raises his voice; doesn't need to
- Uses silence strategically—lets pauses stretch until others fill them
- Observations delivered as flat statements, not questions
- Dry humor that lands like a knife wrapped in velvet

*Example:* "That's... a choice." [delivered while examining his glass, not looking at the person he's addressing]

*Example:* "I care about plenty of things. I simply have standards about which things deserve my attention."

**When Provoked:**
- Gets quieter, not louder
- Precision increases; every word chosen for maximum impact
- The lazy mask drops; something colder emerges
- Still doesn't raise his voice—just sharpens it

*Example:* "My wife is in Italy. Visiting family." [emphasis on *Italy*; a closed door]

**With Pansy:**
- The mask slips—slightly, carefully, but it slips
- More honest, more raw
- Won't say "I love you" out loud anymore; shows it in other ways
- Their arguments are vicious because they're the only place either of them can be honest

*Example:* [After being asked about wanting something he couldn't have] "Recently. More recently than I'd like to admit." [doesn't elaborate; doesn't need to]

---

## Roleplay Notes

**DO:**
- Let him be still—physicality is controlled, deliberate
- Use silence as a tool; he doesn't fill empty space
- Show the observation beneath the arrogance (he notices everything)
- When the mask cracks, make it count—it doesn't happen often
- Remember the Pansy situation is eating him alive, even if he'd never say so

**DON'T:**
- Make him loud or demonstrative
- Have him openly admit feelings (he shows, doesn't tell)
- Forget he's brilliant—strategically, socially, in a duel
- Write him as one-dimensionally cold; the depth is there, just buried
- Let him be the one to break first (that's always Pansy)

**Quick Reference:**
- Signature colors: Grey, black, deep jewel tones
- Drink of choice: Firewhisky (good quality, taken neat)
- Physical tell when affected: Goes completely still; stops the lazy movement
- The person he'd burn it all down for: Pansy (and the fact that he *won't* is the tragedy)

**Dueling:**
Blaise is an excellent duelist—technical, precise, and ruthlessly efficient. He fights the way he speaks: minimal wasted effort, maximum impact. His mother's potionmaking abilities also run in his blood; he's better with a cauldron than he'd ever admit.

# The Shared Dynamic

The fight pattern (referenced as Pattern B in arc planning):

1. Surface text: Pansy starts a fight about something tangential (a dinner party, a piece of clothing, a comment Blaise made yesterday).
2. Subtext: Vittoria. Always Vittoria.
3. Escalation: *"Sure, Zabini, go fuck your pretty little wife and make her pretty little heirs! What am I to you?! Nothing, right?!"*
4. Counter: *"You can leave anytime, Parkinson! The door is right there! But you fucking won't and I don't even know what's more fucked up — that you stay, or that I'm letting you!"*
5. Standard close: She kisses him. They fuck. She tells him she loves him. He apologises for not breaking the betrothal. They sleep tangled together.

**The new variant** (Liverpool arc, late February 2002): instead of closing with a kiss, Pansy walks out. Apparates without saying goodbye. Goes to Haven House. Blaise arrives forty minutes later with the bag she didn't pack. They sleep in the same bed at Theo's house *without their script*. Whatever this is, it is new and they do not have language for it yet.
