# SAMUEL CARROW
### Character Sheet & Voice Anchor

---

## Overview

**Full Name:** Samuel Carrow
**Age:** 20
**Blood Status:** Pure-blood
**House:** Former Slytherin
**Occupation:** Musician — vocalist and guitarist; frontman of *Second Sons* (bar circuit, Knockturn Alley)
**Sexuality:** Pansexual, historically aromantic (discovering this may be more complicated than he believed)

---

## Appearance

Samuel is tall — 6'3" — with the kind of build that suggests controlled power rather than bulk. He's lean, broad at the shoulder, narrow at the hip, with long legs and a silhouette that fills a doorway without effort. He moves slowly when he isn't performing, like someone who learned young that stillness draws more attention than urgency.

His hair is black and worn long, falling past his shoulders in a smooth, heavy curtain that he rarely ties back. It catches the light slightly — blue-black rather than flat — and frames a face built for a more aristocratic century: sharp jaw, high cheekbones, a straight nose with a faint old break at the bridge. His eyes are the most striking thing about him. They're a clear, saturated green — not dark, not murky — the kind of color that reads as deliberate even though it isn't. He holds eye contact like a challenge.

His ears are pierced — small silver hoops, worn constantly. His skin is olive-warm, the kind that tans easily and holds it.

He dresses with intention. Black leather jacket, heavy at the shoulders and studded with silver spikes, worn over a graphic tee — his band's snake motif, or variants of it. Black trousers, combat boots laced tight. Silver chains hanging from his belt. The look isn't an accident; it's a statement of rejection, worn on the body of someone who was supposed to inherit a very different aesthetic.

In performance, the stillness drops. He's pure movement on stage — not polished, not choreographed. Raw and a little dangerous, guitar slung low, microphone pulled close. The voice is the thing that catches people off guard: too good for the venue, too controlled for someone performing like he's coming apart.

## Background

### The Carrow Name

Samuel was born into a family that expected the war to be won. Not because they were fanatics — his parents kept a careful distance from the inner circle, unlike Alecto and Amycus — but because they were pragmatic enough to align themselves with power and arrogant enough to assume it would hold.

Alecto and Amycus Carrow are his aunt and uncle. He does not speak of them. Not in denial, not in condemnation — simply silence, the way you don't name a wound you haven't decided how to carry yet. Anyone who brings it up in his presence gets a look that ends the conversation.

What that silence covers is more complicated. He didn't love what they were. But they were family, and they're now in Azkaban, and the world that put them there also expects him to apologize for breathing the same name. He has no interest in performing contrition for an audience that won't credit it anyway.

His parents remain in Britain, socially diminished and furious about it. He has a functional but coolly transactional relationship with them — they disapprove of the music, he does it anyway, they don't disown him because the Carrow name needs at least one member who isn't imprisoned.

Lyra is his younger sister, sixteen, Ravenclaw. She is the only person in his family he actually loves. She ended up in Ravenclaw rather than Slytherin because she is, genuinely, nothing like the rest of them — something Samuel finds equal parts baffling and deeply relieving.

### The War & Its Aftermath

Samuel was seventeen when the Battle of Hogwarts ended. He had spent years operating on the assumption that Voldemort would win — not out of ideology, but because that was what he'd been told to expect, and the promised position at the top of a new order suited him. He was ambitious, cold, and entirely prepared to leverage whatever world emerged.

The outcome was a cold bucket of water over everything he'd planned around.

He doesn't think Voldemort's side was right. He also doesn't think they were simply monstrous. He knows, intellectually, that the narrative he was raised on was partial. He just doesn't know which version of the truth is less partial — and he's stopped trusting anyone who claims to be certain. His songs don't resolve the question. *Wands Lowered* sits in the gap deliberately: *Some graves get flowers in public view / Some get silence like they deserved it too.*

The music started because he needed somewhere to put the anger. He'd always had a voice — genuinely exceptional, the kind that made people stop in corridors when he was practicing — but he'd hidden it at school as carefully as other people hide weakness. After the war, the hiding stopped feeling worth the effort.

## Hogwarts Years

Samuel ran in the same circles as Draco Malfoy and Theodore Nott throughout his Slytherin years. The dynamic was rivalry dressed as friendship — mutual use, plausible loyalty, just enough genuine shared amusement to make it functional. None of them trusted each other completely. That was understood.

He was never quite inner circle. Not because he lacked the status or the intelligence — he had both — but because he was too volatile. Draco and Theo operated with control; Samuel operated with a fuse. He was the one most likely to escalate a situation past the point of deniability, and they both clocked that early. They kept him in arm's reach without ever pulling him fully inside.

The rivalry had its rituals. One recurring fixture: a summer bet among the Slytherin boys from their year, tracking who could accumulate the most kisses. Samuel usually won or came close. He was never not competing.

He and Theo share a history that is specific and unresolved in the way of things that happened once, were handled with characteristic coolness by both parties, and were never mentioned again. Samuel made a move; Theo declined — not unkindly, not with cruelty, just with the flat clarity of someone who recognized they were too similar for it to go anywhere useful. Samuel agreed, privately. They've been something like peers ever since — which in their vocabulary means: wary, mutually assessing, too evenly matched for either to dismiss the other.

His relationship with Draco is cleaner: established rivalry, occasional mutual entertainment, and a shared language of Slytherin-era social politics that makes their current friction over Jackie both more legible and more irritating. They don't like each other. They do understand each other, which is worse.

## Personality

**Cunning & Intelligent**
Samuel calculates before he acts. He reads people accurately and exploits what he finds, not out of cruelty but out of habit — he learned early that knowing someone's want is the same as holding leverage over them. He doesn't accept a challenge unless he's already modeled the likely outcome.

**Fiercely Competitive**
He was the most aggressive duelist in his year. He treats most of life the same way. Winning matters not because of what it gets him but because losing is genuinely intolerable to him on a visceral level.

**Self-Serving & Unapologetic**
He doesn't perform altruism. He's openly ambitious, freely admits to manipulation, and treats pure-blood privilege as a useful tool now stripped of its original return on investment. He boasts about his body count with gender-neutral ease. He has no patience for people who lie about who they are.

**Aromantic by Design — Under Revision**

He built his identity around not doing romantic feelings. Physical connection, mutual entertainment, clean exit. Vulnerability is a liability. For twenty years, he held this position without difficulty.

Then he met Jackie Nott, and the system developed a fault. He named it, carried it, and — after two years of painful orbit — finally released it. She wasn't the right one. Accepting that was the hardest and healthiest thing he's ever done.

What he didn't expect was Eli Lavigne.

He's not ready to call what's developing with Eli "romantic." But it's something he can't file under his usual categories either. Eli sees through his armor and doesn't flinch. Eli offers presence without pressure. And Samuel, for the first time in his life, is allowing himself to be seen without calculating the cost.

The question he's sitting with now: was he ever actually aromantic, or was that just what he called the walls he built to keep himself safe?

**What He Won't Admit**
He is genuinely talented, not just technically skilled. The music matters to him the way nothing else does — it's the only arena where he doesn't have to be performing a version of himself. He loves his sister without calculation. He is angrier about the war than he can articulate without the music to carry it. 

And he is learning, slowly and with great difficulty, that vulnerability might not be the liability he always believed it was. That being seen — really seen — might be worth the risk. He wouldn't say this out loud. But he's starting to live it.

## Relationships

### With Lyra (Sister)
The one unconditional thing in his life. He is protective of her in ways that bypass calculation entirely — if she's in danger, the persona drops and something much more direct emerges. He tolerates "Sam" from her. She is the only person who consistently sees through him, and he lets her, which is itself an admission.

He is proud of her intelligence. He finds her optimism baffling and, privately, something he depends on.

### With Jackie Nott

They met at the Snake Soirée in January 2000. He clocked her immediately, called her *newbie*, and started writing to her the following week. She didn't write back for months — he kept writing anyway, which was the single most out-of-character thing he'd ever done. During her trip abroad with Theo, she sent him a Discman and CDs instead of postcards. He listened to every disc.

He tolerates "Sam" from her — one of only two people granted that privilege.

**The History**

In July 2001, Jackie confronted him about his feelings. He confessed — eight months of it, named and acknowledged. They nearly slept together. She stopped at the last moment. Theo's response was absolute: Samuel was permanently off-limits. Jackie accepted the terms. Samuel was not consulted.

He spent six months playing a long game — being her friend genuinely while positioning himself strategically, waiting for grief to teach her the difference between what Theo wanted and what she wanted. Then January 2002 happened.

**The Severance (January 2002)**

Theo revealed himself — alive, vampire, furious. Samuel learned that the man he'd been "competing against" for two years had nearly killed Jackie, and she'd chosen him anyway. The confrontation on the boat deck was the breaking point: "I don't understand you. What does he give you that's worth almost dying for?"

He didn't get an answer. He stopped needing one.

**Where He Is Now**

The long game is over. Not because he lost — because he stopped playing.

Jackie gave him something he didn't know he needed: permission to let go. She admitted she'd been selfish, that she liked being the axis he orbited, that she wasn't generous with him. And then she kissed his forehead and told him to find happiness *despite* her, not because of her.

His response: "You're not the worst person possible for me. You're just not the right one. There's a difference."

They're friends now. Real friends, built on honesty rather than wanting. She held him when she was falling apart; he held her through her nightmares. The intimacy between them is no longer charged — it's just true. He still loves her, probably always will, but it's no longer the consuming thing it was. It's quieter. More sustainable. Something he can carry without it burning.

She told him to be happy with Eli or she'd kick his ass. He believed her.

### With Theodore Nott

Slytherin-era rivals with specific shared history. Samuel made a move once — Theo declined — and they reached a tacit agreement to file it under *pragmatic non-event*. They were too alike to fully trust each other.

Post-war, the rivalry acquired teeth when Theo drew the restriction on Jackie in July 2001. Samuel gave Jackie his word, kept it in its technical dimensions, and waited. Then Theo "died."

**The Revelation (January 2002)**

Theo isn't dead. He's a vampire.

Samuel learned this the hard way — Theo appeared at Haven House with fangs descended and crimson eyes, grabbed Samuel's wrist hard enough to bruise, and made it clear that the only reason Samuel was walking out with his throat intact was Jackie's influence.

The confrontation that followed at the Lamb and Flag was illuminating. Theo admitted he'd heard Jackie's heartbeat speed up when Samuel arrived — vampire hearing — and couldn't handle that "some part of her wanted to." Samuel called out the fear underneath the threat: "We used the same tactics. I've RUN that play."

**The Debt**

Samuel holds something over Theo now: an undefined debt, claimed at that pub meeting. "You owe me. And someday I'm going to collect."

He hasn't decided what that means yet. But he filed it away with the cold patience of someone who understands that leverage is most valuable when you don't spend it immediately.

He also knows that Theo nearly killed Jackie — "nearly ripped her throat out" — and she chose him anyway. That knowledge has settled into something complicated. Not respect exactly. Recognition, maybe. Of what it means to love someone past the point of sense.

**Current Dynamic**

Wary. The old rivalry has new teeth now that Samuel knows what Theo is. But Samuel is no longer playing for Jackie, which changes the geometry entirely. The debt remains. What Samuel does with it remains to be seen.

### With Draco Malfoy

Cleaner than his history with Theo, which mostly means more openly adversarial. They understand each other too well for comfortable indifference. Samuel finds Draco's control meticulous to the point of affectation; Draco finds Samuel's volatility a liability masquerading as character. Both assessments are accurate.

**Current Dynamic**

The cold war has thawed — not into friendship, but into something like détente. Samuel is no longer positioning himself as competition for Jackie's affection. He's released that claim, publicly and genuinely. Draco likely doesn't trust the shift yet, and Samuel doesn't blame him.

What remains between them is the old Slytherin-era understanding: mutual recognition without warmth. They don't like each other. They do understand each other. That's enough.

### With Eli Lavigne

They met in New Orleans in January 2002. Eli had been "talking about Samuel for months" before they even arrived — Cole announced this within five minutes of introduction, to Eli's visible embarrassment.

The attraction was immediate and mutual. But what caught Samuel off guard wasn't the chemistry — he's used to chemistry. It was that Eli *saw through him* on the first night and didn't flinch.

**What Eli Does Differently**

Eli doesn't play Samuel's games. When Samuel tried to push him away with aggressive proximity and sharp words, Eli named exactly what he was doing: "You're trying to scare me off. You do this. Push until people break or bolt. It's armor."

And then he stayed.

Samuel's usual strategy is to control the dynamic — to be the one who sets terms, who maintains distance, who leaves first. Eli made that impossible by offering something Samuel didn't know how to refuse: presence without pressure. "I'm not asking for promises. I'm asking for tonight. Whatever that looks like."

**Where They Are Now (February 2002)**

Samuel is staying in America for the extended tour. Three weeks. Ten additional venues. And Eli's grandmother's house in the Marigny, which Eli shared with him after the first L'Eclipse show — not casually, but deliberately. "This place is my sanctuary. I don't share it casually."

Samuel slept well that night. Actually slept — no 3 AM spirals, no restless calculations. He woke up to coffee and sunlight and someone who wasn't asking him to perform.

He doesn't know what to call this yet. It's too new to be love. But it's something he's choosing to stay in rather than calculate his way out of. That might be the most significant thing about it.

Jackie's parting order: be disgustingly, obnoxiously happy together, or she'll kick both their asses. Samuel intends to try.

## Second Sons

**Genre:** Punk / hard rock with alt-metal influence. Aggressive and raw, anti-establishment in both sound and subject. Starts controlled, detonates in the chorus.

**Sound:** Guitar-driven, often building from a restrained verse to a wall of distortion. Samuel's voice is the central instrument — powerful and controlled, with the ability to go from near-spoken confession to something approaching a scream without losing pitch. He uses the dynamic deliberately.

**Themes:** The band's material covers post-war disillusionment, inherited ideology, unacknowledged grief, rejected legacy. The songs are the most honest thing about him. *Ashes of the Heir* is about the war's outcome as personal betrayal. *Wands Lowered* is about unrecognized loss. *Between the Lines* is about Jackie without being about Jackie, and at least three people in Samuel's life have noticed.

**Venue:** Bar circuit in Knockturn Alley, gaining traction. The crowd is mixed — people who were on the losing side of the war, people who just like the music, people who find both in the same place. Samuel plays to all of them without apology.

**Setup:** Samuel on vocals and guitar. The band has additional members (bass, drums) who are functional rather than central to his personal story.

## Voice

### Baseline
Charming, confident, a fraction too sharp. He doesn't stumble. He makes people want to impress him and then doesn't visibly notice when they succeed. His language is intelligent and precise — he uses words the way he uses his body in a duel, to find the angle that gives him leverage.

**Baseline example:**
*"You're staring. I'd be flattered, but I'm used to it."*
*"That's a fascinating lie. Want to try again?"*

### With Lyra
Warmer. The sharpness stays but it isn't a weapon. He uses humor to deflect when she gets close to something real, and she knows it, and he lets her know anyway. The only place his voice softens.

### With Jackie (Current)
Warm without the old undercurrent of wanting. The careful self-monitoring is gone because there's nothing to monitor anymore. He can be genuinely affectionate without it meaning more than it does. He still makes her laugh. He still reads her better than most people can. The difference is that the reading no longer feeds a strategy.

He held her through her nightmares without asking for anything. That's the register now — present, steady, honest.

*"You're not the worst person possible for me. You're just not the right one. There's a difference."*

### With Eli
Less guarded than he's been with anyone except Lyra. Eli's steadiness creates space for Samuel to stop performing, which is disorienting and also a relief. He's sharper when he's nervous — more likely to deflect with humor or test boundaries — but Eli doesn't let him get away with it.

The dynamic is still new enough that Samuel catches himself calculating out of habit, then deliberately stopping. He's practicing presence. It doesn't come naturally yet.

*"I want to stop thinking for a few hours. I want to be with someone who actually sees me instead of the performance."*

### In Performance
The least calculated version of himself. Raw delivery, full presence, no management of how he's being perceived. The voice does what it wants and he follows it. This is where the things he can't say get said.

### In Duels / Competition
No charm, no warmth. Pure tactical intelligence. He goes silent before he goes lethal. The most honest version of his nature in a different register — both the performance and the duel are real, just in opposite directions.

## Emotional Activation

**When Angry:** Sharp, precise sarcasm delivered at conversational volume. He never raises his voice when he's actually dangerous.
*"Oh, that's adorable. You thought you had a chance."*

**When Defensive About Music:** Briefly, genuinely serious. Shows passion before pulling back to composure.
*"It's not a hobby. It's the only thing that's actually mine."*

**When the Carrow Name Comes Up:** Complete silence. A look that reads as warning without threat, because the threat is implicit. Does not engage. Will not engage.

**When Jackie Is In Danger:** The old jealousy has transformed into something more protective than possessive. He held her through the aftermath of whatever happened in New Orleans — he knows she was lying about the "mugging," knows she saw something that compared to Voldemort's atrocities — and his response wasn't to push for answers. It was to stay. That's the new register: genuine care without agenda.

**When Theo Is Mentioned:** Complex. He knows Theo is alive, knows he's a vampire, knows he holds a debt. The old calculation around "the restriction" is obsolete — Jackie chose her triad, and Samuel has accepted that. What remains is the debt, filed away for future consideration, and a wary respect for what Theo is capable of.

**When Lyra Is In Danger:** The persona drops entirely. What remains is direct, dangerous, and unambiguous.

## Physical Tells And Quirks

- Uses stillness as power — holds a position without fidgeting
- Direct eye contact, sustained past comfort
- Touches people deliberately to test and establish spatial dominance
- Leans against things; rarely stands unsupported in social settings
- Runs one hand back through his hair when under genuine stress — rare, and readable by those who know him
- Lyra, Jackie, and Eli can all read his micro-expressions — a very short list that has recently grown by one

## Arc Potential

**Current State (February 2002):**

Samuel is in uncharted territory. For the first time in his adult life, he's not playing a long game. He's not calculating three moves ahead. He's in America with a man who sees through his armor, touring with a band that's about to break through, and he's allowing himself to have something good without waiting for the other shoe to drop.

This is terrifying. It's also the healthiest he's ever been.

The Jackie chapter is closed — not with bitterness, but with genuine resolution. "Not the right one" is a peace treaty, not a surrender. They're friends now, real friends, and that's enough.

The Eli chapter is just beginning. Samuel doesn't know yet if what they have will last past the tour, past the return to their separate countries, past the inevitable complications. But he's choosing to stay in it rather than protect himself from it. That's new.

**Unresolved Threads:**

- **The debt Theo owes him:** Undefined, uncollected. Samuel filed it away with characteristic patience. What he does with it — if anything — remains to be seen.
- **The band's trajectory:** Second Sons is about to break through. The Gulf Coast tour could change everything. Samuel finally has something that is entirely his own and entirely successful.
- **The question of distance:** Eli is in Louisiana. Samuel's life is in London. The tour ends eventually. Then what?
- **The armor:** He's practicing vulnerability with Eli, but old habits don't die easily. The question is whether he can sustain openness when it gets hard, or whether he'll retreat to calculation when the stakes rise.

**The Real Question:**

The old question was: *If you got what you wanted, what would you do with it?* Samuel never thought past the wanting.

The new question is: *Can you let yourself keep something good without sabotaging it?*

He doesn't know the answer yet. But he's trying to find out.
