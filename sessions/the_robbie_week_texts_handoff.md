## **ROBBIE — TEXTING PROFILE (sufficient for this phase; full sheet built later, before the flat)**

### **The facts (canon)**

* **Robert "Robbie" Kowalski.** From Swansea. 27 years old.  
* Criminal (established by Ruby in-scene: "He's not like Connor Harlow. He's dangerous… he WILL hurt you if he sees you as a threat"). Smuggling cigarettes, dealing on the side. Usually between jobs — that didn't change since Ruby left him a year ago.  
* The abuse: cigarette burns, knife, the A\&E incident that ended it. Told everyone — including Crissy — it was an accident. Never violent toward/in front of Crissy.  
* Never found anyone after Ruby. Asks Crissy about her. Still "miserable about what happened" (Crissy's framing).  
* Was confronted at his own door by Gemma post-escape (Gareth in the car, engine running). No police — Ruby forbade it.  
* Got Ruby's NEW number via a girl who both Ruby and Crissy knew from school.  
* Knows (via Crissy, post-June 29): Ruby was in Morriston; there's a new man — posh, southern, English; they were embracing on the grass outside the shop; Ruby fled Crissy and then fell apart in his arms.

### **His self-narrative (the engine — write every text FROM inside this)**

He has CHANGED. It was anger issues — he's "done the work" (he has done nothing, or a few months of something abandoned). Ruby was part of the problem; it takes two. The A\&E was an accident that got exaggerated by her family. She left without giving him a chance to fix it, which was cruel. He never moved on because what they had was REAL. She's probably miserable in London. The new posh boyfriend is a phase / a rebound / someone filling her head. **He is the romantic lead of this story.** Everyone else is an obstacle between him and the reunion scene he has already written.

### **Texting register (HOW he writes)**

* Lowercase mostly, sparse punctuation, no emoji (or extremely rare). Reads casual, unbothered — engineered casual.  
* Opens REASONABLE. The first texts should pass a stranger's sniff test: *"heard you were back. wouldve been nice to know. im not angry, just thought we were better than that."*  
* **Guilt mechanics:** frames her boundaries as wounds he's bravely enduring. "i'm not asking for anything." (He is.) "just five minutes." (It's never five minutes.) "you owe me that much at least." (She owes him nothing.)  
* **The work:** vague, unverifiable self-improvement claims. "been seeing someone about the anger." "different person now." Never a detail, because there isn't one.  
* **The rewrite:** refers to the abuse only in passive, minimized shapes — "what happened," "that night," "the accident," "when things got bad." NEVER names what he did. If pressed, pivots to his pain: "you think i dont live with it too?"  
* **Escalation curve (Tue–Thu):** longer messages, faster follow-ups when she doesn't answer, double- and triple-texting, hurt curdling to entitlement: "so thats it, im just blocked out of your life like 4 years meant nothing." References to the new man arrive obliquely first ("crissy says you looked well. both of you."), then pointed.  
* **After the block:** silence to her — but the block is when he starts MOVING. Off-page.  
* He calls her Princess (main nickname), or Little Red. Kochanie, when he's done something and wants to apologize.  
* Never threatens explicitly in writing. The menace is in frequency, persistence, knowledge ("saw your mam's still got the corsa"), and the closing distance — never in stated intent. He thinks he's being patient. That IS the threat.

### **What Sable must hold**

* Make him PLAUSIBLE. A stranger shown text one should say "give him a chance." The horror is in the accumulation, not any single message.  
* The reader (and Draco, later) should be able to feel exactly why a 19-year-old Ruby once loved him — the charm is real, just load-bearing for something rotten.  
* NEVER let him become a cartoon. No mustache-twirling. He believes every word.
