## I. Haven House — ~6:50 p.m.

Draco had knotted the tie twice and taken it off both times. The third attempt sat wrong against his throat — too formal by half — so he pulled it loose and left the collar open, because *open* was the register Ruby lived in. Band tees, worn denim, nothing that announced itself. He had spent twenty-two years learning to dress like a verdict. Tonight he wanted to look like a man, and only that.

The signet ring turned once around his finger before he caught it doing it.

"You've changed your shirt three times." Theo filled the doorway, shoulder to the frame, the unlit lighter walking across his knuckles. "She decided she liked you weeks ago. You married into the hard sells, Draco. Ruby was never going to be one of them."

"She hasn't met Jackie." Draco said it to the mirror. "Or you."

"She's texted me about a book where the love interest has wings and a personality disorder." The lighter flipped, caught. "We're well past introductions. I'm only here to collect a face to go with the crimes against punctuation."

Luna had poured herself into the warm hollow of the unmade bed, gold eyes tracking the lighter like a thing she intended to kill later. Draco found his cufflinks by touch.

"Say it back to me," he said. "The condition."

Theo sighed, performance-weary. "Ill since I was a boy. A thing the doctors could never put one name to. I don't eat in front of people because it embarrasses me, and you're all very kind not to make it a thing." A beat, dry as bone. "Basically a vampire."

"Don't say that last part at the table."

"I say that last part *because* it disarms. A man who jokes about a thing can't possibly be hiding it." The lighter went still in his fist. "It's the truest lie I own. Let me keep it."

Down the landing: a door, the click of heels, then quiet — Jackie, somewhere between the bedroom and the stairs, putting herself together. Theo turned his head toward the sound out of old habit, a body that no longer needed to breathe still listening for the one heartbeat in the house that had ever mattered to the thing inside him.

He knew what tonight cost her. She'd told him weeks ago, in the dark after a Malice session, in the flat voice she used when she'd decided to be honest before she could talk herself out of it. He hadn't argued her down then and he wouldn't now. He only held it where he held everything — *she's coming anyway* — and let that be answer enough. The Heliophobus already sat on his skin, her work, the reason the low gold light through the window was only light and not a sentence passed on him.

Draco fixed the second cufflink. In the glass his own face looked back at him — composed, unreadable, the face he'd worn through a war and a trial and his father's sentencing. He hated that it was the same one. He wanted Ruby to get a different face than the world had been handed.

"She's going to see all of it at once tonight," he said, quieter. "Every part of me, in one room. I've kept it in pieces on purpose — her over here, this over there. After tonight it's a single thing."

Theo crossed to him, met his eyes in the mirror, and set a cool hand briefly at the back of his neck — there, then gone. "Then let her see the whole of it. You've been good in every piece I've watched you carry. You'll be good in the sum."

The ring went still under Draco's thumb.

"Shifts," Theo said, lighter for it. "You first — you promised the girl. Go and lead."

## III. The approach — 7:31 p.m.

They came up on foot from the Piccadilly end, the three of them — Apparition and a Michelin doorway didn't mix, and the walk gave Draco somewhere to put his hands. Theo walked easy in the low light, Jackie's work doing its quiet job under his skin. Draco saw her before she saw them.

She was doing the bright smile. He knew the bright smile — he'd catalogued it across a beach and a farmhouse and her mother's kitchen — and he knew the other one too, the real one, that arrived without her permission and pulled higher on the left. This was not that one. She had just put her phone away. Her shoulders sat a half-inch too high, and when he came close enough to read it, her breath was running in the counted rhythm she used when the floor had shifted under her and she'd decided to keep standing on it alone.

He didn't know what had moved it. He filed the fact away the way she'd taught him without ever meaning to teach him — *for later, for care, never for use* — and kept his face easy.

"You're early," he said, stopping in front of her, close enough that the skirt's hem brushed his trouser leg. "You're always early when you're nervous. You did it in Swansea, too."

Behind him Theo hung back the promised half-step, hands in his pockets, the lighter quiet for once. Jackie was at his shoulder.

Draco reached out and tucked the loose strand back toward the clip — the small, unshowy touch he was allowed in public, the only one on offer out here. "They came to meet you, not to weigh you," he said, low, only for her. "Hold on to that when Theo starts quoting your own book taste back at you across the table."

Then he stepped half-aside. He'd promised to lead, and leading meant the introductions in the right order — and the right order started with the man who'd been awake at four in the morning trading arguments with her about chapter endings.

"Ruby." Theo's voice came warm, unhurried, already amused. "So you're the one with opinions about the winged one. I've heard about chapter thirty for a solid month. I've got grievances of my own and nowhere civilised to put them."

And then there was Jackie —

## IV. The lift — ~7:33 p.m.

*(Co-written. Sable voices Theo & Draco; Ruby exterior-only. Jackie's two lines below — "halve it" and the "ask me anything" opener — were handed to Sable by Alice to place for this beat.)*

The lift was a brass cage barely built for four, and it put them closer than any of them had chosen.

Draco felt Ruby's shoulder come to rest against his arm as the grille rattled shut — and felt her, half a second later, think better of it and take the contact back, leaving a careful inch of air between them. She didn't know the rules yet. Didn't know if she was allowed to lean on him here, in front of his wife, so she'd decided not to risk it. It cost him not to close the gap himself. He left it where it was. Hers to close, when she believed she could.

"This lift," Theo announced from the back corner, "sounds like a hanging every time the door shuts. I've raised it with management. Nothing's been done."

"He hasn't raised anything." Jackie didn't turn round. "He likes it. He thinks it's atmospheric." Then she tipped her head a fraction toward Ruby — the first thing she'd aimed at her since the pavement that wasn't measured and careful. "Whatever they've told you about each other, halve it. They're both worse and softer than they let on. Took me years to get the ratio right."

And Draco watched Ruby watch *them* — watched her catalogue the easy shorthand of it, Theo's mouth twitching, Jackie's flat delivery, the two of them moving around each other like a sentence they'd finished a hundred times. He knew what she was seeing. She was seeing him be *known* — not performed at, not assessed across a dinner table, but known, by people who'd had years to learn him. He couldn't tell if it made her glad or lonely. With Ruby it was usually both at once.

Theo had clocked the other thing before the doors even opened.

It was small. In the press of the cage she'd angled herself away from him by a degree more than the space asked for, and when the grille shut them in together her breath had gone shallow and stayed there — and her face had done a small involuntary thing when he leaned to speak, a flinch that lived under thought. Not fear. He knew fear; fear had a smell and a flavour and this wasn't it. This was her body drawing back from his while her mind stood entirely uninformed — the way one animal reads another and goes still without deciding to.

He'd have written it off as nerves, except nerves smelled like sweat and adrenaline and this was neither. This was something below all of that, older, recoiling from *him* specifically — from the cold and the wrongness of him that the cover story papered over and a body, apparently, did not believe.

*Interesting,* said the thing in him, conversational, and Theo told it to be quiet. He filed the moment the way he filed everything now — neat, dated, unexplained — and gave her his easiest face. Whatever it was, it wasn't tonight's problem. Tonight he had a sweet Riesling to land and a girl of Draco's to win.

The doors folded back on colour and heat — crimson silk, brass gone soft-gold in the low light, coriander and woodsmoke off the tandoor. A round table in the second alcove, set for four, a pale glass already breathing at one place.

Ruby looked at it. Looked at Theo.

"Riesling," he said, folding himself into the chair at her left. "Sweet, not dry. You confessed to being a beer-and-mojito creature in one of your braver moments — this is the nearest the list would come to meeting you halfway." No plate appeared in front of him, and he didn't reach for one; the not-eating was old cover, worn smooth, nobody's business at a happy table.

Draco took the seat at her right and was aware, precisely, of the careful distance she kept on the cloth — her hands in her lap, his flat on his thigh, the small honest country of space between them that neither of them closed. *Later.* He'd become, against everything his life had trained into him, a patient man.

Jackie set her forearms on the table and looked at Ruby directly — the full heterochromatic weight of it, moss-green and glacier-blue, the look most people slid away from — and said it plain.

"This is strange for both of us. I'm not going to pretend it isn't." A beat, unhurried. "So ask me whatever you want. First. Before the food comes and we all start being polite at each other."

## V. The table — "ask me anything" — ~7:38 p.m.

*(Co-written. Alice voices Jackie & Ruby; Sable voices Theo & Draco. Alice's block is transcribed verbatim.)*

**— Alice (Jackie, Ruby; one Draco beat) —**

Ruby looked at Jackie, a little taken aback. She was expecting a high-society lady, someone wearing designer clothes, in immaculate makeup, performing niceties for the benefit of her husband who decided to bring his mistress to dinner.

What she got was a girl her age, who — somehow — looked and acted more like some of her teachers at school. Like she wanted to be kind but couldn't be bothered to perform the kindness. Like she was too tired to care.

She held Jackie's mismatched gaze for three seconds, then looked at her empty plate. She didn't touch the menu.

"Why did you agree to meet me?"

She could feel Draco's gaze on her right, and Theo's on her left — Jackie being seated on Draco's left side and hence across from her.

Jackie looked at the girl for a moment, and then smiled — not unkindly — and shrugged.

"He wouldn't stop yapping about you, like he was fifteen and scored the prettiest girl at school. So I wanted to see what the fuss was about."

"Jackie…" Draco's voice was quiet and pained, but he didn't dare speak up louder. It was her terms tonight. Hers and Theo's.

"Don't *Jackie* me, dear." She turned him down, opening the menu and looking briefly at the food positions. "You're the one that practically floats back home every Sunday. Last weekend I was almost sure I'd have to use—" she stopped, her eyes landing in Ruby and her mouth snagging on *a piercing spell to deflate you*.

"— a needle to get you down."

Ruby listened to them and felt, for the lack of a better term, perplexed. Jackie didn't even try to hide the fact that the dinner was a kind of assessment, and Ruby felt better knowing that than if she'd been assured she was accepted and included from the get go.

"Thank you." She said, drawing their attention back to her. "For giving me a chance. I do love him, you know?"

She glanced at Draco, out of the corner of her eye, still not daring to reach for him physically.

Jackie looked between them, nodded, and went back to reading her menu.

**— Sable (Draco, Theo) —**

Draco heard *I do love him, you know* and felt it go through him sideways — because those were *their* words. She'd given them to him first on the floor of her bathroom, knee to knee, both of them stripped of every defence, the only time in his life the sentence had ever been said to him without a price attached. Private. Theirs. And now she'd taken it out of that small sealed room and set it on the linen in front of Jackie like a coin she was prepared to lose — *here, this is what I've got, weigh it.*

He understood what it cost her. Ruby didn't spend that word lightly. To offer it up *here,* to the wife, as evidence in an assessment she might still fail — that wasn't sentiment. That was nerve.

*Brave girl. Reckless, brave girl.* He kept his hand flat on his thigh, an inch from the cliff-edge of the cloth, where it had been all night. He wanted badly to cover hers, to put his own coin down beside it so she wasn't out there alone — but the reach still wasn't his to make tonight, and she hadn't made it either, so the space between their hands stayed exactly as wide as her courage had left it.

"She does," he said instead, low, to the table more than anyone at it. "Love me. God knows why." Drier than he felt it by a mile — he couldn't have managed the truth straight, not with all three of them watching. He'd have cracked clean down the middle.

Theo watched the small catastrophe cross Draco's face and decided, charitably, to give the man somewhere to put it.

"You should know," he said to Ruby, lifting two fingers a half-inch and drawing a waiter out of the crimson dark, "that I haven't had the pleasure of you in person until tonight — but I've had to *live* with the evidence for a month." Warm, easy, aimed at her. "He comes home on a Sunday and he *floats.* I caught him humming over the washing-up last week. I've known the man since we were boys, and I'd have wagered everything I own he didn't know the words to a single song." A beat. "And we have met, after a fashion — you're the one who's kept me up till four in the morning arguing about Vincent and his cages. I knew your taste in monsters before I knew your face."

She turned to answer him, half a smile breaking loose — and mid-turn drew the breath back, her shoulders going with it, a small involuntary correction, her body deciding the distance was wrong a beat before her face had finished being amused. Half a second. Nobody at the table clocked it but him. *There it is. Twice.* He filed the second instance under the first, let the smile widen over the snag, and said nothing.

"Order something," he went on, nudging the menu toward her. "All of it, if you like. Draco's paying, and he can stand it." Then the faintest tilt of his head toward Jackie — *your floor, love* — handing the night back to the only person at the table who'd actually called it. "And let her finish. She's enjoying the assessment far more than she'll let you see."
