## Last exchanges (verbatim)

### Ruby (Jackie):
Jackie had already pulled away, and was looking up at him with a languid, satisfied smile. She licked her lips ostentatiously, before getting up — using his hip as leverage, because her leg went slightly numb — and reaching past him to the rack with soaps and shampoos.
"He's not going to gloat." She said, working the lavender soap between her hands.
"He's going to be happy. For me, and for you. Well, and maybe just a tad bit jealous that I got you all to myself." Jackie put the soap away and started slowly washing his chest and torso in careful, circular motions.

They got back downstairs with exactly three minutes to spare. Jackie changed the shorts to comfy black leggings and a tunic cinched with her trusty corset at the waist. Simple and unrestrictive, like the year of Auror training had drilled into her that she had to be always ready to fight. Or maybe it was just her life that did that — the curse, the breaking, Fenholt, Dante — they had no shortage of enemies, and just the three of them to fight them. She smiled at Theo, untying her hair, which was still slightly damp. The night was warm enough that she knew she'd dry before they got back.
"See? We made it in time." She grinned, utterly satisfied and unrepentant, knowing full-well that Theo had to have heard what exactly took them so long.

### Sable (Theo / Draco):
Theo had heard all of it.

He wasn't going to pretend otherwise — the walls of Haven House were thin to ordinary ears and glass to his, and he'd stood at the foot of the stairs through the whole of it: the water, the quiet, Draco's composure coming apart by degrees the way it only ever did when no one was meant to be listening. *She has the original.* He'd heard that, too. Had turned it over once, clean, and set it down without a scratch on it. Jealousy was a reflex he'd long since learned to disarm before it reached his face — and what was left underneath, once he had, was just the warm, absurd, bottomless fact of loving two people who were upstairs being good to each other.

So when Jackie came down untying her damp hair, flushed and unrepentant and *glowing,* and informed him they'd made it in time, Theo didn't gloat.

He looked at her from where he leaned against the front door, Luna a boneless drape over one shoulder, and let the crooked thing pull at the corner of his mouth.

"You made it in time," he agreed, grave as a judge, "by the generous standard of a woman who defines *in time* as 'eventually, and smug about it.'" He tipped his head, that fraction of a tilt that meant he was enjoying himself. "For the record, Menace — I'm not jealous that you kept him. I'm jealous of the *acoustics.* I had a much better seat than either of you appreciated."

Behind her, Draco arrived on the last stair with his composure rebuilt and his hair damp-tamed back into something almost regulation, and made the tactical error of believing the subject could be changed.

"We are *not* discussing the acoustics."

"We're absolutely discussing the acoustics," Theo said, without heat, without looking away from Jackie. "You held still the entire time, Malfoy. Disciplined. I was *proud.*" The deadpan didn't crack, but something moved behind it, low and real and aimed at both of them at once. "And then you weren't, and you said her name like it was the only word you'd ever learned. Good. She earned it." A beat. "She earns it daily. Try to keep up."

Draco opened his mouth, found nothing in it worth the cost, and closed it again — which was, in its way, the most eloquent thing he'd managed all evening. He came to stand at Jackie's other side instead, close, the lavender soap rising off both of them in the warm hall, and said only, quietly, to Theo: "Insufferable. I *told* her you'd be insufferable."

"You did," Theo allowed. "I'm honouring the prediction. It's the least I can do."

Then he straightened off the door, unhurried, and reached back to work the latch — and beyond it the evening had finally surrendered, the last copper gone out of the sky, the street going blue and then deeper, the lamps coming up one by one down the terrace. Night. His hour. The only stretch of the day the world ever handed back to him whole.

He stepped out first onto the top step and breathed in the warm dark out of old habit, though he didn't need the air, and tilted his face up to a city that only ever felt entirely his after the sun gave up on it.

"Come on, then," Theo said, and the dryness had gone soft at the edges, the way it did when he forgot to guard it. He held the door for the two of them, his family, freshly scrubbed and a little wrecked and *his.* "I promised you my city."

### Ruby (Jackie):
Jackie just rolled her eyes at their banter, sliding on her trainers. Her wand was hidden securely in the corset — she didn't take the holster today, knowing it would look too obvious in the Muggle London — and she reached to peel Luna off Theo's shoulder, placing the indignant creature on the floor.
"Guard the house, Luna. We're trusting you with it." She said solemnly, giving the Kneazle a gentle scratch, before straightening and following Theo out.
It was… eerie, seeing him outside. Some part of her immediately went on high alert, supplying that she should find him a cover, take him away, that maybe some hidden, trapped part of the sun had stayed in the blue spilling everywhere and that he would be *powerless* against it.
"So," Jackie started, mainly to distract her mind from the thoughts. "Your city. Not that we all live in it, but sure. Are we taking Muggle transport?"
She supposed Theo could've ran there in a few minutes. She and Draco had to either Apparate, or take the Tube.

### Sable (Theo / Draco):
Theo watched her set the indignant Kneazle down and brief it on its sentry duties, and didn't bother hiding his amusement. "Formidable," he said. "If the house is besieged by a single field mouse, Luna will have negotiated its surrender by morning."

But his attention had already gone where it always went — to her. To the way she'd come out onto the step beside him and *changed,* the soft satisfied thing from the shower folding away under something older and harder. He knew that stillness. The flick of her eyes to the rooflines, the parked cars, the mouths of the alleys. Scanning. Guarding. He could all but hear the Auror in her running the arithmetic of his exposure — *no cover, open street, get him under a roof* — though the sun was down for good now and nothing up there could touch him.

He let her sweep the street once, twice, and didn't interrupt it. There was nothing in the world that made him feel quite so absurdly safe as being looked at like something worth shielding.

"Muggle transport," he repeated, as if tasting it, drawing her gaze back down off the skyline — on purpose. "No. We walk."

"We *walk,*" Draco echoed, with the particular flatness of a man recalculating a distance he had not budgeted for. "Theo. It is the better part of three miles."

"It's exactly three miles, and that's the point." Theo came down the steps unhurried, hands in his pockets, and turned to take in the terrace — the lamps coming up, the blue going to ink over the chimney pots. "You don't *Apparate* into a thing you love. You don't take the Tube to it, either — though I'll allow it on the way back, if her feet give out and she's gracious about asking." A glance at Jackie, dry and warm at once. "The city isn't the hill, Menace. The hill's only where it ends. You asked why I call it mine — it's because I'm the one of us who's *only* ever allowed it like this. Empty. Lit. After everyone decent has gone indoors."

*He* stirred at that, lazy and approving, fond of the dark for entirely worse reasons. Theo let the agreement pass without comment and kept walking.

He stopped at the corner, though, and waited — for Draco's resigned, immaculate sigh, for the soft scuff of her trainers on the pavement — and when she drew level he didn't take her hand. He only fell in half a pace off her shoulder, where a man stood when he wanted to be able to put himself between her and whatever came next without making a performance of it.

"And before you ask," he added, lower, just for her, "yes. I see you doing it. The looking. The cover you keep trying to find me." The crooked thing again, gentler now. "Don't stop on my account. It's the most loved I've felt all week — and I've had a *very* good evening."

The street unspooled ahead of them, warm and dark and entirely theirs. At his side Draco was already saying something unforgivable about the fate of his shoes. Somewhere far off, the city exhaled, and Theo walked out into the one part of the day the world ever gave back to him whole.

### Ruby (Jackie):
Jackie joined him, the uneasiness lifting off of her with every step that he was outside and wasn't showing any sign of discomfort
"Don't flatter yourself. I know you're safe, it's just…"
She and Draco went out to work, to meet people, to buy groceries and go to Diagon Alley, to do things during the day, all the time.
And there Theo was, back at Haven House, like a portelgeist haunting the building. But she didn't want to ruin the evening by reminding him of that, so she landed on, "We don't do this often enough, and so it feels weird."

Jackie looked at Theo, out of the corner of her eyes as they crossed a street and weaved between Muggles walking every which way. It was late, but the streets weren't exactly empty. It being a warm, early July evening, there were groups of tourists, teenagers and young adults. A thousand heartbeats murmuring like a living pulse of the city — which Jackie could have only tried to imagine, and Theo could actually hear.
"How often do you leave the house when we're asleep?"
She wasn't even sure why she'd asked that, after walking in silence for several minutes. But Theo moved like someone who knew the directions, someone with purpose, like he actually knew the city like the back of his hand.

### Sable (Theo / Draco):
Theo didn't answer straight away. He let three Muggles weave past — a girl laughing into her phone, two boys arguing a football result — and watched her not-quite-look at him, and decided the question deserved the honest weight of it.

"Most nights," he said. "All of them, lately, if I'm telling the truth — and I generally am with you, because you've always been able to tell when I'm not." He kept his eyes ahead, on the long blue throat of the street. "You two go down around midnight. I give it an hour, in case anyone needs water or a nightmare talked down. And then I go out."

He named it without drama, the way he named blades and sunrises. The river at three, flat and black and carrying the lights. The markets at four, men stacking crates who'd long since stopped finding him strange. The foxes. The bridges with no one on them. A whole second city that only existed in the hours his family slept through — learned street by street because there was nothing else to do with a body that never tired and a mind that never switched off.

"I'm always home before you wake. I never mention it. It seemed—" the crooked thing, smaller now, "—ungracious. Complaining about the one thing I'm freely given."

Beside them Draco had gone quiet in a particular way, and Theo clocked it without turning. *He didn't know it was every night.* Filed that, too — gently. Some things you let a person learn on a warm pavement instead of telling them in a kitchen.

"So that's your answer, Menace. Often. Always. Alone." He finally looked at her, and the deadpan had nothing under it now but the plain fact of being glad. "Which is the whole reason I wanted you out here tonight. The city's better than I ever let on — and I was tired of being the only one who knew it."

The lamps stretched ahead of them, gold on gold, all the way down toward a hill they were in no hurry to reach.
