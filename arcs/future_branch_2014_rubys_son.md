## Established Future Premise: Jackie

- The June 1999 ritual that removed Jackie's hereditary curse had an unknown reproductive consequence. McGonagall warned Draco and Theo afterward that an old, untested ritual could have long-term effects no one could predict.
- Jackie **can conceive**, but cannot carry beyond roughly the first month or two. The working magical mechanism is that her body cannot sustain the pregnancy once the child begins establishing an independent magical core.
- The thematic mechanism: the ritual successfully ended the Taylor bloodline's transmission of the curse, but could not distinguish **ending the curse** from **ending the line**.
- Jackie eventually becomes pregnant and miscarries. By the Christmas visit to Samaya's she is freshly bereaved; motherhood surrounds her as ordinary household texture through Samaya, Pierre, and Edmund.
- Samaya helped research the ritual and supplied its book and ingredients. She helped save Jackie without knowing what the cure might cost, making her both beloved sister and an unbearable point in the causal chain.
- Jackie has not been established as someone whose deepest self-authored wish is motherhood. She wants to carry **Draco's** child partly because she loves him and wants the future he confessed he wanted. The loss is still the loss of bodily choice, of a possible future, and of a gift she meant to give—not proof that she always wanted a conventional maternal life.
- One pregnancy and one loss are sufficient to establish the rule. Repeated miscarriages are not required merely to prove the tragedy.
- During the resulting pain, Jackie may lash out at Draco with: *“Maybe you should try to get back in touch with Ruby or something. She'd give you an entire pack of pups.”* This is cruelty born from Jackie's attempt to dismantle the family before anyone can reject her. It is not a sincere offer or evidence that Ruby is interchangeable.

## Proposed 2014 Branch: Ruby's Son

### Spine Alice established

- Draco and Ruby had protected sex during their 2002 relationship. A condom failure went unnoticed; it was Draco's first time using one, making an unnoticed mistake plausible without requiring either character to behave recklessly on purpose.
- Ruby disappeared from Draco's life without knowing she was pregnant. She discovered the pregnancy roughly two or three months later.
- By then, Ruby had found a man in her werewolf pack who offered to accept “the pup” and raise him as his own. Ruby chose that stable family over returning to Draco and, as she understood it, ruining Draco's life.
- The boy is raised for approximately eleven years without Draco knowing he exists.
- In summer **2014**, the boy receives his Hogwarts letter. Ruby knows the magical world will make continued concealment impossible or dangerously fragile, so she contacts Draco.
- Her opening is deliberately understated. Alice's spontaneous wording was: *“Hi Draco. Listen, you probably don't remember me. We used to date for a little while ten years ago. Can we meet to talk?”* The elapsed-time wording needs adjusting for 2014, but the awkwardly casual register is locked.
- After intervening shenanigans and a meeting over coffee or another ordinary public setting, she tells him: *“My kid just got a letter from Hogwarts. So I suppose you'd find out sooner or later. Which is to say—you have a son.”*

## Emotional Architecture

- The son Draco wanted exists—but not with Jackie, who learned she could conceive and could not carry.
- Ruby's choice can be understandable without being absolved. She believed she was protecting Draco and securing a father for her child; she also withheld Draco's son and made an irreversible decision on his behalf for eleven years.
- **Objective story truth and character experience must remain distinct.** Ruby is not literally Jackie's fertile replacement, and Jackie is not a failed wife—but Jackie may experience the revelation as exactly that. She may believe Ruby gave Draco the child and future Jackie could not, that her own place has become conditional, or that the universe has proved her cruel “pack of pups” line correct. The narrative must let that belief hurt without quietly endorsing it as fact.
- The pack father is the boy's real, loving parent. Draco's biological fatherhood does not erase the man who raised him; the existing father does not erase what Ruby withheld from Draco. None of the men must perceive this balance immediately: Draco may feel another man occupied eleven stolen years of his life, while the pack father may experience Draco as a biological claim arriving to destabilise the family he built.
- Draco's central wound is not merely that he “has an heir.” It is that an entire relationship with his child occurred without him. He may be furious with Ruby, jealous of the father who was present, possessive of the son he has only just met, and terrified that wanting any place in the boy's life confirms Jackie's belief that what he wanted from her was transferable to a woman whose body could provide it.
- Jackie's September 3 line about Ruby giving Draco “an entire pack of pups” becomes accidental foreshadowing, not causal magic and not punishment delivered by the narrative.
- Theo remains a husband and father-shaped member of the household, not a neutral mediator or spare adult. How the son understands Jackie and Theo must develop through relationship rather than titles assigned in advance.
- **The Jackie Rule remains absolute:** Alice governs Jackie's reactions, choices, speech, and interiority.

## Guardrails

- This branch does **not** retroactively make the July 2002 sex unprotected or careless; the pregnancy results from contraceptive failure.
- Do not turn Jackie's infertility or miscarriage into a device invented solely to intensify the secret-son reveal. It is an independently established future consequence with its own arc.
- Do not protect the characters from drawing painful, unfair, or contradictory conclusions. The guardrail applies to narrative framing—not to Jackie's fear of replacement, Draco's anger and jealousy, Ruby's self-justification, or the pack father's defensiveness.
- Do not make the biological reveal instantly transfer custody, affection, surname, or paternal authority to Draco.
- Do not vilify the pack father to simplify Draco's claim.
- Do not let blood status, Malfoy inheritance, or Hogwarts bureaucracy resolve the emotional question for the family.
- Do not promote this branch into active canon until Alice explicitly chooses to play or ratify it.
