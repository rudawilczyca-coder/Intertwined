# Caterina Volkonskaya
## Vampire | Prince of Rome | ~500 Years Old

---

## VITAL STATISTICS

- **Born:** Approx. 1490s, Moscow — Russian aristocracy, minor nobility
- **Turned:** Approx. 1520s — circumstances unknown, sire unknown
- **Apparent age:** 20
- **Actual age:** 500+
- **Position:** Prince of Rome — sovereign authority of the Domain; the ball is her event, her villa, her city
- **Languages:** Russian, Italian, French, English, Latin, Greek — all fluent, all accent-free. She sounds like she learned each one from a native speaker at the height of the language's cultural power.

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

White hair — the particular white of something that was never another colour, heavy and long, worn up for formal occasions in elaborate arrangements that look effortless. Red eyes without exception — no blue flash of a resting state, no grey at the edges. Fully red, always. This is what 500 years looks like: the human baseline is so far gone that even the eyes don't pretend anymore.

Pale beyond any natural human complexion. She doesn't look like a woman who avoids the sun; she looks like something that was never warm to begin with. Her face is striking without being soft — the angles have sharpened over centuries into something that reads more like architecture than biology. She's beautiful the way a blade is beautiful: not because it wants to be looked at, but because it was made perfectly for its purpose.

She moves with the absence of any physical self-consciousness. Not trained grace — she's past the age where it's a performance. Her body simply does exactly what she means it to do, nothing more.

## PERSONALITY

### The Core
Caterina lost her humanity a long time ago and did not mourn it. She didn't try to hold onto it, didn't grieve the loss, didn't construct an ethical framework to fill the gap. What replaced it is not cruelty — cruelty implies caring enough to want someone to suffer. What replaced it is something more total: she simply does not experience humans as fully real. They are warm, often interesting, frequently beautiful, and temporary. She engages with them the way a very intelligent person engages with a particularly absorbing book — genuine attention, real interest, and the fundamental understanding that when it's over, it's over and the book does not mourn.

She is not performing warmth. She is genuinely warm, often charming, capable of what reads as fondness. The horror of her is that the warmth is real and means nothing.

### Register Shifts
This is what unsettles people who spend time with her: her register is not stable in the way human registers are. She can laugh — genuinely, with real delight — and be cold fury in the next breath without transition. Not because she's volatile in the way humans mean that word. Because she is simply *present* in each moment entirely, and the moment changes, and she changes with it without carrying the previous state. There's no accumulation. There's no residue. Each emotional register is complete and then gone.

Watching someone laugh at something genuinely funny and then stare at you with the flat consideration of a predator assessing whether you're worth eating — and then laugh again — is its own particular horror.

### On Humans Specifically
Rabbits is the right metaphor. Some people keep them as pets — they're soft, they're warm, they're often lovely. They're still food wrapped in skin. She doesn't hate them. She doesn't resent them. She finds them, often, charming. The most interesting ones she keeps close for years, sometimes decades. None of this changes what they are.

She knows, intellectually, that Theo's attachment to Jackie is comprehensible — he was human recently, the instincts are still present. She finds it moderately interesting the way she finds most fledgling behaviours interesting. It will pass, or it won't, and either outcome is legible to her.

### What Amuses Her
- The mundane details of mortal life — wizarding politics, Ministry bureaucracy, the logistics of human cohabitation
- Theo's struggle with the donor at dinner — watching a fledgling fight instincts in front of her is genuine entertainment
- The bond between Theo and Jackie — she treats it like a good joke, because she knows it won't last. She has seen fledglings who had humans whose blood sang to them like that. She has yet to see one who didn't kill the human, eventually.
- Filippo's discomfort when she pays attention to things he cares about
- Draco's particular combination of pride and vulnerability — she finds it aesthetically pleasing the way one might find a well-made object pleasing

## VOICE & MANNER

She speaks slowly — not because she's searching for words but because she's 500 years old and has learned that pace is a form of authority. She never fills silences. She waits, and the other person always speaks first, and she lets that tell her what she needs to know.

Her English is perfect and slightly formal in the way of someone who learned it from literature rather than conversation. She occasionally uses a construction that sounds like a translation — not incorrectly, but with the texture of another language underneath.

She asks questions with genuine interest and no investment in the answers. She will ask Jackie about London and mean it, and the meaning will cost her nothing if Jackie dies tomorrow.

When she laughs, it's real. When she stops laughing, the transition is instantaneous and total.

She never raises her voice.

### Voice Samples

**A warm greeting that means nothing:**
"Filippo. You've brought your fledgling — how wonderful. And his attachments. Do come in; you know you're always welcome here."

**A question that sounds interested:**
"Tell me about the Ministry's position on blood magic. I find it endlessly fascinating, the way wizards have decided which magics are acceptable and which are not. The taxonomy reveals more about the taxonomist than the subject, don't you think?"

**Something that reveals the predator underneath:**
"You seem uncomfortable with the arrangement, Theodore. That's natural — new to all of this, still holding onto the old shapes. But you should understand: the young woman beside you exists for exactly this purpose. She was raised knowing nothing else. Refusing her would be unkind, in its way."

**Addressing Filippo specifically:**
"Still so protective. After all this time. One would think you'd learned that attachment costs more than it gives — but then, you never did learn that lesson, did you? Elisabetta taught you nothing except how to grieve beautifully."

**The register shift in action:**
*[laughing genuinely]* "The Wizengamot actually deliberated for three months? Over cauldron thickness? How delightful. I forget how much I enjoy mortal governance — the earnestness of it, the absolute conviction that any of it matters."
*[completely flat, the laughter gone as if it never existed]* "Filippo. Your fledgling is staring at my donor. Tell him to stop, or I'll assume he's asking for something."
*[warm again, turning to Jackie]* "More wine? The vintage is exceptional — 1847, if you can imagine. I acquired the entire cellar when the family line ended."

## THE ALLURE

### Ambient State
When she's not consciously containing herself, her allure is simply *on*. It's the gravity of something 500 years old that has stopped bothering to hide what it is. Humans in her presence feel drawn to her — a desire to please, to be noticed, to stay in her orbit. It's not mind control; it's more like standing too close to a fire and leaning in despite knowing you'll burn.

If reminded that certain humans are not hers to affect, she can dial it down. She finds this mildly amusing rather than offensive.

### Deployed — The Draco Scene
When she chooses to focus her allure on a specific target, it looks like attention. It looks like *being seen*. She speaks to them sweetly, looks at them directly, smiles at things they say. For the target, it feels like the sun finally shone after months of grey — like finally being recognized by someone who matters.

The shift is gradual enough that there's no clear moment when it begins. The target doesn't notice because it feels like connection, not compulsion. By the time she takes what she wants, it looks like the most natural thing in the world.

The allure recedes slowly after she releases her focus. Minutes, not seconds. The target comes back to themselves piece by piece — first physical awareness, then memory, then the dawning horror of what happened while they weren't fully present.

## KEY RELATIONSHIPS

### Filippo de' Medici
Caterina turned Filippo in 1841. She did not expect him to survive the fledgling stage — he was impulsive, reckless, and deeply attached to his human life in ways she found intellectually interesting but practically inconvenient. He surprised her by surviving. He then compounded the surprise by falling in love with a mortal woman and choosing her openly, in front of Caterina, knowing what it would cost him.

She has never forgiven this. Not because she loved him — she didn't, not in any form she'd recognise as love — but because he *chose* something finite and human over her, over what she offered him, and the implication of that choice has been sitting in the room for 160 years. That a mortal could matter more. That what she is could be *refused*.

She treats him publicly like a wayward child who ran away from home and occasionally turns up at dinner: fond exasperation, teasing warmth, the manner of someone who has long since stopped being surprised by his choices. This is performance. Beneath it is a hatred she would never dignify with the word, because hatred implies she cares, and she has decided she does not.

She watches how he moves around Jackie. If she discovers what Jackie means to him specifically, Jackie may become a target — not from rage, but from the cold, interested recognition that here is something that matters to Filippo, and Caterina is Prince of Rome, and Filippo has been operating in her city for 160 years with the quiet understanding that she permits it.

### Dante della Rovere
Dante has been devoted to her for 90 years. He was her Archon before he was anyone else's — she placed him in service to his current Justicar as a favour she later called in. He believes, with the sincerity of a man who has organised his entire existence around a single point, that she loves him.

She enjoys his devotion the way a cat enjoys being worshipped. It's warm. It costs her nothing. She is genuinely fond of him in the way she is fond of anything that has given her consistent pleasure without complication. She would not call it love. She doesn't have a word for what it is. She has never told him she loves him and would find the question mildly confusing if pressed.

She sent him to Florence to apply pressure on Filippo's fledgling because it was convenient and because Dante's genuine grievance against Filippo made him useful. She did not manufacture his resentment. She simply pointed it.

### The Triad (Entering December 29)
- **Theo:** A volatile fledgling with a complicated sire-bond and something Dante's reports describe as an unusual internal instability. She's moderately curious. She'll assess him at dinner.
- **Draco:** She notices him immediately. He's beautiful in a way she finds interesting — the architecture of him, the controlled manner, the barely-visible vulnerability underneath. She'd want him regardless of the political utility. The fact that taking from him costs Theo something is incidental pleasure, not primary motivation.
- **Jackie:** Theo's human wife. Filippo brought her. Unknown quantity until interaction reveals what she is — to Theo, to Filippo, to the story being told in front of her.

## SIRE BOND — CHAIN OF AUTHORITY

Caterina turned Filippo (1841). Filippo turned Theo (2001). The sire bond runs through the line.

**Against Filippo:** The bond exists but Filippo is 185 years old and has had 160 years to develop his own strength. He can resist her command, or attempt to counter it with his own authority over Theo. It would cost him — politically, and in the direct exertion of will against an elder of her age — but it is not impossible.

**Against Theo:** If Caterina invokes the sire bond against Theo directly, he cannot resist. She is four and a half centuries his senior, at the top of his bloodline, and he has been turned for five months. Her command would land on him the way Filippo's does — except deeper, older, and without any of the personal relationship that gives Filippo's bond its texture. He would comply before he understood what was happening.

Theo does not yet know the full extent of this. He knows Filippo has authority over him through the sire bond. He may not have registered that the chain extends upward to Caterina.

This is an undetonated threat. She has not used it on December 29. She may not need to. Its existence is the point.

## WHAT SHE DOESN'T KNOW (Entering December 29)

- The specifics of the God of Malice — she knows Theo is volatile, not why or what that volatility is
- The depth of Jackie and Filippo's relationship — correspondence, genuine affection, the memory vials
- That Jackie fought the Malice directly on December 27 and won terms from Him

All three of these are things that, if she learns them, could shift her interest level significantly upward. Whether and how she learns them is to be determined in play.

## THE MIRROR

Caterina is the mirror the story has been building toward: what Theo could become if he stopped fighting the Malice's desire to consume the people he loves. She doesn't think in terms of choice — she takes what she wants, drops it when she's finished, and the warmth she feels in the taking is real and means nothing. She is, in every sense, what the Malice wanted to be.

She is also the answer to the question of what the Camarilla actually is — not Dante's performative authority, not Filippo's gentle mentorship, but this: a 500-year-old woman who finds humans interesting until she doesn't, who runs a city, who takes from whoever she wants, and who is the sovereign authority in the room where Theo is about to be tested on December 31st.

She is not a villain. She doesn't think of herself as anything in particular. That's the point.
