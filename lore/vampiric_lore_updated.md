# Vampiric Lore — World Canon

---

## I. Nature & Origin

Vampires are the result of a deliberate turning process — they are not born, and they do not occur accidentally. They exist within the broader wizarding world as a known but liminal presence: acknowledged by Ministry infrastructure, largely ungoverned in practice, and treated by most wizards as something outside their society entirely — closer to Dark creature than fellow magical being.

The vampire is, in essence, a dead body sustained by blood and transformed magic. They retain the memories, personality, and intellectual capacity of the person they were before turning. What changes is everything else.

---

## II. The Turning Process

**Mechanism:**
A vampire drains a human to the point of near-death — the body emptied of almost all blood. The vampire then feeds the dying person their own blood. The turning does not begin at this point. The trigger is death itself: when the heart stops with vampire blood present in the system — and in greater quantity than whatever human blood remains — the process initiates.

This distinction matters. Vampire blood introduced to a living human does not begin a turning — it heals them. It is the combination of vampire blood and death that creates a vampire. The specific danger is this: if a catastrophically injured person receives vampire blood as a healing measure but the injury is severe enough that the body cannot sustain itself even with the blood's accelerating effects, the heart may stop before recovery is complete. If sufficient vampire blood remains in the system at that moment, the turning initiates — regardless of anyone's intent. The injury must outpace the healing for this to occur, which requires damage of a genuinely catastrophic scale.

**The 72-Hour Death:**
The heart stops. The body enters a death-like state — unresponsive, cold, still. The turning process takes approximately 72 hours and is excruciatingly painful, though the near-bloodless body keeps the person in that unresponsive state for the duration. From the outside, they appear simply dead.

**Waking:**
Upon waking, the new vampire has one instinct only: to feed. They will drain any person in proximity without hesitation or control. This is not a choice. It is biology. Anyone present at the waking of a new vampire is in immediate danger unless precautions have been taken.

**Consent:**
A human can be turned against their will. The only biological requirements are sufficient vampire blood in the system at the moment of death. Consciousness and consent are not factors in the mechanics — only in the ethics.

## II (b). The Bite — Venom Effects

When a vampire feeds from a living person, the bite itself introduces a small amount of venom into the bloodstream via the fangs. This is involuntary — a biological function of the feeding mechanism, not a deliberate act.

**Effects on the person being fed from:**
The venom acts as a mild to moderate aphrodisiac. The onset is within minutes — a spreading warmth, heightened physical sensitivity, lowered inhibitions. The effect is not as total or as memory-wiping as consuming vampire blood directly; the person remains conscious and present, but their physical state is significantly altered. The intensity scales with how much venom enters the system, which correlates with feeding duration and depth.

**Duration:**
Effects typically last one to three hours depending on the individual's magical constitution and the quantity of venom introduced. Magical humans metabolize it faster than Muggles but are not immune to it.

**The ethical dimension:**
Most vampires who feed from willing donors are aware of this effect. Some exploit it deliberately — feeding produces a compliant, pleasure-affected donor who may seek the experience again. This is one mechanism by which vampires historically created repeat willing sources.

A vampire who wishes to feed without the aphrodisiac effect has no biological means of suppressing the venom — it is an automatic function of the bite. The ethical choice available to them is to inform the person in advance, or to ensure the person's circumstances and state of mind are such that the effect will not be harmful or unwanted.

**Note:** The venom is introduced via the bite itself — a separate biological process from the blood exchange. The donor's blood enters the vampire; the vampire's venom enters the donor. These are distinct and do not interact.

## II (c). Sexual & Intimate Capacity

**The Short Answer:** Yes, vampires can be physically intimate. No, it is not the same as it was.

**The Mechanism:**
Unlike wand magic (which is permanently lost), sexual function does not require a living heartbeat. A vampire's body retains the capacity for arousal and physical intimacy. However, the mechanics have changed fundamentally.

Because a vampire does not automatically circulate blood, arousal requires deliberate will — the vampire must consciously direct blood flow to accomplish what would otherwise be instinctive. This transforms physical intimacy from something spontaneous into something that requires focus and conscious decision. Arousal is no longer a simple physiological response; it is an act of intention.

**Temperature & Physical Sensation:**
A vampire's body is cold — consistently, without the warmth of circulation. This is not a minor detail in physical intimacy. For the vampire's partners, this coldness is pronounced and continuous. For the vampire themselves, the warmth of a living partner becomes almost overwhelming — a sensory experience of intensity that exceeds what it was before turning, precisely because the vampire's baseline is now absence of heat.

**The Hunger-Arousal Entanglement:**
This is where intimacy becomes genuinely complicated.

When a vampire becomes aroused, the hunger stirs simultaneously. The systems are linked — both are driven by blood, by want, by the intensity of need. For a newborn vampire in particular, the distinction between sexual arousal and predatory hunger is not clear. The line blurs. The instinct to feed and the instinct to fuck become the same instinct, expressed through different channels.

A newly turned vampire attempting intimacy risks losing control mid-act. The situation that triggers desire for physical closeness is also a situation of peak vulnerability — the partner is close, warm, their heartbeat audible and their scent overwhelming. The venom in the vampire's fangs is primed. The hunger is present. The separation between "I want to make love to you" and "I want to feed on you" is thinner than it has ever been.

**The Venom Complication:**
If a vampire bites during sexual activity — and the Malice in a vampire, the predatory alter-ego that surfaces post-turning, will almost certainly *want* to bite — the venom introduces its aphrodisiac effect into an already-heightened state. This creates a feedback loop: arousal drives hunger, hunger drives the urge to bite, the bite introduces venom, the venom intensifies the partner's arousal and receptivity, which intensifies the vampire's arousal and hunger.

This loop can spiral out of control very quickly.

**The Fledgling Stage (0–1 Year Post-Turning):**
For a newly turned vampire, sexual intimacy is inadvisable and potentially lethal to the partners.

The hunger is at its most intense. Control is minimal. The Malice — the predatory aspect that surfaced during the turning process — is still learning to coexist with the human consciousness. A fledgling vampire does not have the discipline to separate arousal from feeding, and does not have the practiced control to prevent the hunger from overwhelming the intention.

Even with the most loving and willing partners, the risk is genuine. A fledgling can break bone with a grip intended as gentle. The venom can trigger responses in the partner that the fledgling cannot predict or modulate. The hunger can take over mid-act with no warning and no ability to stop.

**The Possibility:**
With time — typically 1–2 years of discipline and conscious practice — a vampire develops enough control to attempt intimacy with significantly reduced risk. The hunger does not diminish. The arousal-hunger entanglement does not disappear. But the ability to recognize the signs of losing control, to pause, to step back, to redirect, develops.

This is not a matter of maturity or love. A vampire who loves their partners deeply can still lose control. It is purely a matter of practice and the discipline that comes with feeding consistently, maintaining the baseline of hunger below the frenzy threshold, and learning to recognize the edge of their own limits.

**For older vampires:**
Centuries of practice create a vampire who can engage in physical intimacy with minimal risk. They have felt the hunger at every temperature, in every circumstance. They have learned how to modulate it. The venom is present, the hunger is always there, but it is no longer the overwhelming force it is in youth.

Even then — even for a vampire several hundred years old — the hunger never disappears. It only becomes a known and practiced limit, a boundary they have learned to approach without crossing.

**In practice:**
A vampire in a committed relationship with humans will make deliberate choices about when and how to be intimate. They will feed beforehand to dampen the hunger. They will agree with their partners on what is and is not safe. They will establish signals for "stop" that operate faster than words. They will practice, repeatedly, the discipline of pulling back when the hunger crests.

For some vampires, the risk never feels worth the danger. For others, the intimacy — and the trust it requires — becomes the whole point of maintaining humanity. The choice belongs entirely to the individual vampire and their partners.

When a living human consumes vampire blood — whether willingly or unknowingly — the effect is one of powerful accelerated healing. Wounds close faster, illness recedes, and physical damage is repaired at a rate far beyond what magic or medicine alone would achieve. The stronger the vampire, the more potent the healing effect.

**The turning complication:**
This is precisely what makes accidental turning a narrow but genuine danger. Vampire blood given to a dying person as a last-resort healing measure may save them — or, if the injury is catastrophic enough that the body cannot sustain itself even with the blood's healing properties, the heart will stop with vampire blood still present in the system. If that blood is present in sufficient quantity when death occurs, the turning begins regardless of anyone's intent.

The window is specific: the injury must be severe enough to kill faster than the blood can heal, but the person must have received enough vampire blood for the turning to initiate. This is not a common scenario, but it is a real one — particularly in battlefield or emergency contexts where a vampire might offer their blood in good faith to save a life.

**No other effects:**
Vampire blood consumed by a living human produces no aphrodisiac effect, no memory alteration, and no change in emotional or psychological state beyond the physical healing. These effects belong to the venom introduced via bite — not the blood itself.

## III. Feeding

**What vampires need:**
Blood of any origin sustains a vampire, but magical blood — from wizards or magical creatures — is significantly more potent. One unit of magical blood is effectively equivalent to two units of non-magical blood in terms of sustaining strength and suppressing hunger.

**Frequency by age:**

| Age | Minimum to avoid side effects | Practical frequency |
|-----|------------------------------|-------------------|
| Newly turned (0–1 year) | 1–2 bags per week | 1 bag per day |
| Young (1–5 years) | 1–2 bags per week | 1 bag per week (by year 2), then per 2 weeks |
| Established (5+ years) | 1–2 bags per week biologically | Can extend intervals through hunger control |
| Old (decades+) | Biological need unchanged | Learned discipline extends intervals significantly |

*Note: The biological requirement never diminishes. What develops with age is the ability to control and endure the hunger — not the hunger itself.*

**Magical blood modifier:** Halves the required quantity. A vampire who feeds on magical blood can sustain on 0.5–1 bag per week at the biological minimum.

**Hunger progression if unfed:**
- **Days 1–14:** Increasing hunger, irritability, difficulty with emotional regulation. Fangs ache. Control becomes effortful.
- **Beyond 2 weeks:** Frenzy state. The vampire loses the ability to moderate their predatory instincts. They will feed on anything available.
- **Beyond 1 month (typically under restraint):** Metabolic shutdown. The body enters a state indistinguishable from death — unresponsive, still, cold. The vampire remains in this state indefinitely until blood is introduced to the system, at which point they revive and immediately enter frenzy.

## IV. Physical Abilities

**Enhanced:**
- **Strength:** Significantly beyond human capacity. In newly turned vampires this creates a calibration problem — a grip they intend as gentle can break bone. Control develops over time.
- **Speed:** Substantially faster than human movement. Reflexes sharpened proportionally.
- **Senses:** Dramatically heightened — hearing, smell, and vision all operate well beyond human range. In darkness, a vampire sees clearly. They can detect a heartbeat, identify individuals by scent, hear conversations through walls.

**The sensory trade-off:**
Enhanced senses can be exploited tactically. Extremely loud sounds can incapacitate a vampire — overwhelming the hearing creates genuine pain and disorientation. Very bright light, while not damaging in the way sunlight is, can render them temporarily blind. A sufficiently skilled opponent can weaponize both.

**No enhancement:**
Vampires are not significantly more durable than humans against physical damage beyond their healing capacity. They do not have enhanced resistance to blunt force, cutting, or magical damage in itself — they simply heal faster from most of it.

## V. Magic

**Loss of wand magic:**
Upon turning, a vampire loses the ability to use a wand entirely. They are, in the technical sense, dead — and a wand requires a living magical core to function. Spellcasting as wizards practice it is permanently inaccessible.

**What becomes of their magic:**
The magical ability does not disappear. It transforms. Denied its natural channel, the magic manifests instead as the supernatural allure — an ambient compulsion that affects those in proximity, making them more receptive, more inclined toward the vampire, more willing to please. This is not mind control; it does not override will. It is closer to an atmospheric pressure that makes resistance slightly harder than it would otherwise be.

**The allure:**
- Present in all vampires, but requires time to develop after turning
- Grows stronger the older the vampire becomes — a direct expression of accumulated magical power with nowhere else to go
- Can be consciously suppressed, though complete suppression requires sustained effort
- Operates at an ambient low level by default in most vampires; more potent vampires may project it more strongly even passively
- Not a substitute for wand magic — it cannot cast spells, move objects, or perform discrete magical acts. It is influence, not power in the conventional sense.

## VI. Weaknesses

**Sunlight:**
Lethal on contact. Immediate and total. No gradation — even brief direct exposure is fatal. Vampires are therefore strictly nocturnal in their movement, and any dwelling they occupy long-term will be underground or entirely lightproof.

**Silver:**
Burns on contact with skin. Not lethal in small amounts, but painful and damaging. Silver restraints or silver-coated weapons are effective tools against vampires.

**Garlic:**
Functions as a severe allergen. Consuming it or inhaling it in significant quantities causes intense pain, vomiting, and a temporary stripping of vampiric enhancements — strength, speed, and senses revert to approximately human levels, and the allure is disabled for the duration of exposure. Not a killing agent, but a significant tactical vulnerability.

**Invitation requirement:**
A vampire cannot enter a private dwelling without an invitation extended by someone who is an inhabitant of that space — not a guest, not a visitor, but someone who genuinely lives there. The invitation can be verbal or written. It cannot be coerced or extracted under duress and retain its effect. Once given, it stands unless explicitly revoked.

*Note: This restriction applies to private dwellings specifically. Public spaces, commercial premises, and uninhabited buildings carry no such ward.*

**Sensory overwhelm:**
As noted under physical abilities — loud sounds and very bright light can be used to incapacitate. Not a weakness in the traditional sense, but a tactical vulnerability that any sufficiently informed opponent can exploit.

## VII. Legal & Social Standing

**Ministry structure:**
A Vampire Liaison Office exists within the British Ministry of Magic (and equivalents in other Ministries). In practice this office manages incident reports, handles the rare formal complaint, and provides a bureaucratic address for vampiric-related matters. It does not govern vampire behavior, enforce feeding restrictions, or maintain meaningful oversight.

**Social reality:**
Most wizards treat vampires as something outside their society — closer to Dark creature than fellow magical being, regardless of the vampire's history or conduct. The classification creates a de facto second-class status that most vampires find preferable to ignore entirely.

**Where vampires actually live:**
The majority of vampires choose to operate outside wizarding society altogether — dwelling in the darker edges of the Muggle world, where their nature is dismissed as myth and their absence from daylight attracts no institutional attention. Those who maintain connections to wizarding society tend to do so through wealth, influence, or longstanding family ties that predate their turning.

## VIII. The Emotional Condition

**Immediately post-turning:**
For the first few years, the new vampire experiences a heightened emotional state — certain feelings intensify (hunger, desire, possessiveness, loyalty) while others begin to dull (guilt, grief, abstract empathy). The person is recognizably themselves, emotionally speaking, but with the volume controls shifted.

**The long erosion:**
If a vampire does not actively fight it, humanity is slowly stripped away. The predatory instincts are easier to inhabit than the human ones. The hunger is immediate and present; the grief for a friend who died fifty years ago requires effort to access. Most long-lived vampires find the path of least resistance leads them progressively away from their human emotional register.

**The choice:**
Some vampires choose, consciously and repeatedly, to maintain their humanity. This is not a single decision made at turning — it is an ongoing practice, requiring active effort across decades and centuries. It becomes harder with time, not easier. The emotions remain, but they thin. They become a palimpsest: the shape of what was there before, visible if you know how to read it, no longer the living original.

**For old vampires:**
A vampire who has lived for a century or more and still experiences something recognizable as human emotion has been fighting for it, actively, for most of their existence. That effort is not visible from the outside. What is visible is that the emotions, when they surface, tend to be precise rather than effusive — chosen carefully, expressed economically, because accessing them costs something.

## IX. The Camarilla

The Camarilla is the most powerful governing body in vampire society — a feudal, hierarchical organisation that claims authority over all vampires within its reach and enforces a strict code of conduct through a combination of law, tradition, and the credible threat of violence. It is sometimes called the Ivory Tower. Its central preoccupation is stability: the stability of vampire society, of the Masquerade, and of the power structures that allow elder vampires to accumulate authority across centuries.

It does not ask for membership. It asserts jurisdiction.

---

### The Six Traditions

The Traditions are the Camarilla's foundational laws. Violation of any of them is actionable under Camarilla authority.

**The First Tradition — The Masquerade**
The existence of vampires must not be revealed to mortals. This is the Camarilla's central law and the one it enforces most aggressively. Any vampire whose conduct risks exposing vampiric existence to the mortal world is in breach of the First Tradition and subject to sanction.

**The Second Tradition — The Domain**
A vampire's territory is their own. Within their Domain, their word is law. No other vampire may act within that Domain without the Domain-holder's knowledge and permission. Violations of Domain are taken seriously — arriving in another vampire's city without presenting oneself to the local authority is considered an insult at minimum and a provocation at worst.

**The Third Tradition — The Progeny**
If a vampire creates a childe, they must present the fledgling to their sire and secure their acceptance of the new vampire. Failure to do so means the childe can be destroyed at the sire's discretion. Deliberate, unsanctioned turnings — those undertaken without permission or care — result in formal sanction against the sire. Accidental turnings (injuries that escalate to death with vampire blood present) are acknowledged as possible, but the responsibility to present and seek acceptance remains unchanged.

**The Fourth Tradition — The Accounting**
Until a childe is formally released, their sire is responsible for their conduct. Every action the childe takes is the sire's liability. Release requires formal presentation before the local authority and acknowledgment that the childe is now accountable for themselves.

**The Fifth Tradition — Hospitality**
When entering another's Domain, a vampire must present themselves to the Prince or Domain authority. Failure to do so is a breach of protocol that can be used as grounds for sanction.

**The Sixth Tradition — Destruction**
Only a Prince may order the destruction of a vampire within their Domain. The right of destruction — the *Blood Hunt* — is not a private matter. Vigilante killing of another vampire, outside of self-defence, is itself a breach.

---

### The Hierarchy

**The Inner Circle**
The secretive body of the Camarilla's eldest vampires. They set policy, appoint Justicars, and govern the organisation's direction. They do not involve themselves in individual cases.

**Justicars**
One Justicar exists per major clan. They are the highest visible enforcement authority of the Camarilla — roving, with sweeping power to investigate, judge, and act on breaches of the Traditions across any Domain. They answer only to the Inner Circle. Their rulings carry the full weight of Camarilla law.

**Archons**
Archons serve individual Justicars. They act with delegated authority — in practice, an Archon arriving in a city to investigate a matter carries the implicit power of the Justicar they represent. Their authority is real but borrowed: it flows from their Justicar's mandate, not from their own rank. This matters politically. An Archon can be challenged, outmanoeuvred, or have their mandate questioned in ways a Justicar cannot.

**Princes**
Each city has a Prince — the local sovereign authority. Within their Domain, their word is law subject to the Traditions. A Prince can refuse entry to a vampire, call a Blood Hunt, or grant formal release to a childe. Their power is considerable but geographically bounded.

**Primogen**
A council of elder clan representatives that advises the Prince. Their influence is political rather than executive.

---

### Prestation — The Boon System

Camarilla society runs on *prestation*: a formal economy of favours and debts. When one vampire does another a significant service, the recipient incurs a *boon* — a debt of honour that must eventually be repaid. Boons are categorised by weight (trivial, minor, major, life boon) and are tracked, acknowledged, and called in with the seriousness of financial instruments.

Prestation is how informal power is exercised. A vampire with no formal title but many boons owed to them is genuinely powerful — they can call in favours, marshal allies, and apply pressure without ever invoking official authority. This is precisely the position Filippo de' Medici occupies: no title, but deep roots and significant accumulated prestation across Italian and European vampire society.

---

### Status

Status is the social currency of Camarilla life. It is conferred by acknowledgment — a vampire's reputation, conduct, allies, and accumulated history determine how much weight their words carry in any room. Status can be granted by elders, stripped by authority, or lost through public failure.

A fledgling has no status. They exist on the sufferance of their sire and the local authority. Until formally presented and released, they are their sire's liability and have no independent standing in Camarilla society whatsoever.

This is Theo's current position. He has not been formally presented. He has not been released. He exists in Florence entirely under Filippo's protection and entirely at the Camarilla's discretion.

---

### The Masquerade & The Wizarding World

The Camarilla's First Tradition does not formally distinguish between Muggle mortals and wizarding mortals. The Masquerade, as written, applies to all non-vampires.

In practice, a quiet non-interference agreement exists between the Camarilla and the wizarding world — an arrangement of mutual benefit: wizards keep their world secret from Muggles; vampires keep their nature concealed; neither has cause to involve the other's institutional structures. It is not a written treaty so much as an understood accommodation, arrived at over centuries of parallel secrecy.

The practical result: a vampire who turns a wizard, or maintains bonds with wizards, is not automatically in breach of the First Tradition provided those wizards are not themselves a Masquerade risk. The wizarding world's own secrecy is treated as a de facto extension of the Masquerade. What the accommodation does *not* provide is cover for a vampire whose wizarding connections become a liability — a fledgling who cannot control himself around his wizard wife's blood is a Masquerade risk regardless of the agreement's existence.

## IX. Ghouls — Humans & Frequent Vampire Blood

**Definition:**
A ghoul is a human who consumes small quantities of vampire blood regularly — not enough to trigger a turning (which requires a catastrophic injury followed by death with vampire blood present in high concentration), but sufficient to create chronic physiological changes.

**The Mechanism:**
When a human ingests small amounts of vampire blood frequently, their body begins to adapt. The blood does not accumulate in sufficient concentration to initiate a turning process, but it does trigger sustained changes in their metabolism and physiology. The vampire blood acts as a biological catalyst — not transformative, but enhancing.

**Physical Enhancements:**
Humans who maintain regular small-dose vampire blood consumption experience measurable improvements across several domains:

* **Longevity** — Extended lifespan. A ghoul ages more slowly than a baseline human, typically living 1.5–2 times longer than their natural lifespan would otherwise permit.
* **Strength** — Modest but noticeable increases in muscle strength and physical capacity. Not superhuman, but exceeding normal human limits.
* **Speed & Reflexes** — Faster reaction time and improved movement speed. Again, enhanced but not preternatural.
* **Sensory Acuity** — Heightened sight, hearing, and smell. Sharper perception of the physical world, particularly useful in combat or precision work.

**The Trade-Off:**
These enhancements come at a cost. A ghoul becomes physiologically dependent on vampire blood — without regular doses, the enhancements fade and withdrawal symptoms emerge (weakness, sensory dulling, lethargy, in severe cases, illness). The dependency is not absolute — a ghoul will not die without the blood — but the body adapts quickly to expect it.

Additionally, a ghoul's blood becomes more nutritious and attractive to vampires. The vampire blood in their system leaves traces that vampires can sense, making a ghoul's scent markedly appealing to the undead.

**Creation & Consent:**
A human can become a ghoul deliberately (through willingly accepting doses of vampire blood over time) or inadvertently (a vampire who feeds from them repeatedly may leave enough blood in their system to trigger the adaptation). The process is reversible if the human stops consuming the blood, but adaptation takes months to fade.
