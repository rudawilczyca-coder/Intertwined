# Auror Training Program (2001–2002)

## Program Overview

**Duration:** 12 months (July to June)
**Location:** Ministry of Magic, Level 2 — Auror Headquarters; various field locations
**Schedule:** Monday–Friday, 07:00–15:00
**Supervising Auror:** Harry Potter (working Auror; not Head Auror)

The Auror Training Program admits a small cohort each year following a rigorous three-part tryout (Physical, Magical, Psychological — tests randomized annually). Entry requires Outstanding NEWTs in Defence Against the Dark Arts, Potions, Charms, and Transfiguration.

**Post-War Context:** Following the Battle of Hogwarts (May 1998), Harry Potter, Hermione Granger, and Ron Weasley were awarded honorary NEWTs — their war contributions were credited as equivalent to the highest examination scores, exempting them from repeating their seventh year. Potter entered the Auror Office immediately and rose through the ranks quickly, but as of 2001 he holds a senior working Auror position, not the department head role. Other students — particularly those from Death Eater-adjacent families — were required to return for a repeat seventh year (1998–1999) before sitting their NEWTs normally.

Potter's dual role as active field Auror and training program supervisor is unusual. He personally oversees the cohort's development and takes one trainee per year as his Shadow Program partner. Assignments are officially passed down from department administration, but it's an open secret that Potter has a say in who rides with him.

## Weekly Structure by Phase

Training runs Monday–Friday, 07:00–15:00. Weekends off. Physical training is front-loaded heavily in Phase 1 and tapers into maintenance as the program progresses. The classroom-to-field ratio inverts across the year.

**Phase 1 Schedule (Months 1–3):**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 07:00–08:00 | Morning PT (runs, calisthenics, stamina drills, flexibility) |
| 08:00–09:30 | Hand-to-hand combat training / wand combat drills (alternating days) |
| 09:30–09:45 | Break |
| 09:45–12:00 | Classroom instruction (law, theory, report writing, Muggle awareness) |
| 12:00–12:45 | Lunch |
| 12:45–15:00 | Classroom instruction continued / study / written assignments |

Physical conditioning is at its peak here — nearly half the training day. Trainees build the fitness base they'll maintain for the rest of the program. Combat training alternates between hand-to-hand (boxing fundamentals, grappling, restraint techniques) and wand combat (dueling drills, shield work, non-verbal casting under pressure).

**Phase 2 Schedule (Months 4–6):**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 07:00–07:45 | Morning PT (maintaining Phase 1 fitness gains; varied — runs, circuits, combat refreshers) |
| 07:45–08:30 | Wand combat drills (dueling, tactical spellwork, team-based combat scenarios) |
| 08:30–08:45 | Break |
| 08:45–12:00 | Controlled practicals (forensics, interrogation sims, tactical Apparition, artifact handling) |
| 12:00–12:45 | Lunch |
| 12:45–15:00 | Practicals continued / case simulations / Red Tape exercises |

PT scales back to maintenance. The combat block tightens — less foundational drilling, more applied scenarios. The bulk of the day shifts to hands-on practicals in Ministry-controlled environments.

**Phase 3 Schedule (Months 7–9):**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 07:00–07:30 | Morning PT (varies by day — combat sparring, runs, bodyweight circuits, flexibility) |
| 07:30–08:15 | Wand combat drills |
| 08:15–08:30 | Break / transition to field |
| 08:30–15:00 | Field work with assigned Senior Auror (includes lunch in the field) |

Physical training is pure maintenance — 30 minutes, rotating focus. Wand combat stays sharp but brief. The rest of the day belongs to the ride-along. Trainees report to the Ministry, train, then deploy with their Senior Auror for the remainder of the shift. Paperwork from field work is completed during the afternoon block or — more realistically — on the trainee's own time.

**Phase 4 Schedule (Months 10–12):**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 07:00–07:30 | Morning PT (maintenance) |
| 07:30–08:15 | Wand combat drills |
| 08:15 onward | Independent casework / leading operations / Wizengamot preparation |

Similar to Phase 3 but with greater independence. Trainees manage their own schedules within the day's framework — cold case investigation, raid planning, trial preparation. Senior Aurors are available for consultation but are no longer directing the trainee's day.

*Note: The Final Field Trial in June replaces the normal schedule entirely for its duration.*

## Phase 1: Foundation and Bureaucracy (Months 1–3: July–September)

*The reality check. Trainees come in expecting to duel Dark Wizards on day one. Instead, they're handed a stack of parchment and a quill.*

**Core Modules:**

- **Magical Law & Wizengamot Procedure:** Trainees memorize the Decree for the Reasonable Restriction of Underage Sorcery, the Statute of Secrecy, search-and-seizure laws, inter-departmental jurisdiction boundaries, and the legal framework for arrests and detentions.

- **Use-of-Force Protocol & Post-War Ethics:** Developed after the fall of the Voldemort regime, which had legalized Unforgivable Curses against suspects. Covers proportional response doctrine, lethal force authorization thresholds, documentation requirements for every spell cast during an operation, and the legal consequences of excessive force. A direct institutional overcorrection — taken very seriously by program leadership.

- **Incident Report Writing:** Trainees are shown Pensieve memories of complex magical crimes and given one hour to draft legally watertight reports. Accuracy, completeness, and clarity are graded ruthlessly. A sloppy report can collapse an entire prosecution.

- **Advanced Magical Theory:** Breaking down why certain curses bypass standard shield charms, the mechanics of spell deflection, ward analysis, and magical signature identification theory.

- **Muggle Awareness & Integration:** Practical training for operating in Muggle environments. Using Muggle transport, navigating Muggle London, blending into Muggle crowds, understanding Muggle technology at a functional level. Muggleborn trainees have a significant advantage here; purebloods often struggle.

---

## Phase 2: Controlled Practicals and Forensics (Months 4–6: October–December)

*Trainees leave the classroom but only enter Ministry-controlled simulated environments. The focus shifts to investigation, forensics, and safe extraction.*

**Core Modules:**

- **Magical Forensics & Trace Analysis:** Isolating magical signatures at crime scenes. Identifying which wand core cast a specific curse. Safely bottling lingering dark magical residue for evidence. Chain-of-custody protocols for magical evidence.

- **Dark Artifact Handling:** Practical sessions with cursed objects in controlled environments. Safe containment, transport, and cataloguing procedures. Identifying common curse types by visual and magical signature. Not just theory — trainees handle real (defused) cursed items under supervision.

- **Field Potions & Emergency Medicine:** Basic healing potions and their field applications. Antidote identification. Recognizing symptoms of Veritaserum, Amortentia, common poisons, and dark potion exposure. Stabilization techniques when St. Mungo's isn't immediately reachable. Trainees with strong Potions backgrounds tend to excel here.

- **Interrogation & De-escalation:** Roleplaying scenarios with trained Ministry actors. Talking down a panicked civilian who accidentally caused magical harm. Breaking a stubborn smuggler without resorting to illegal Legilimency. Reading body language, managing emotional volatility, and knowing when to push versus when to back off.

- **The Red Tape Simulation:** Trainees are given a mock case to solve while instructors actively throw bureaucratic obstacles at them. Applying for inter-departmental warrants to search a suspect's Gringotts vault. Requesting backup from the Magical Law Enforcement Patrol through proper channels. Filing cross-jurisdictional paperwork. Teaches that real Auror work lives and dies on administrative competence.

- **Tactical Apparition:** Apparating safely into hostile or unknown environments. Apparating while carrying a wounded partner. Silent Apparition techniques for stealth operations. Anti-Apparition ward detection and countermeasures.

- **Undercover Fundamentals:** Disguise basics — Polyjuice protocol, glamour charms, Muggle costume and behavior. Maintaining a cover identity under questioning. Operating without backup or communication. Deep undercover work is advanced Auror specialty, but all trainees get the foundations.

## Phase 3: The Shadow Program (Months 7–9: January–March)

*Trainees are paired with Senior Aurors for active field duty. They are observers and backup first, gradually taking on more responsibility as the phase progresses.*

**Senior Auror Assignments:**
Each trainee is assigned to a Senior Auror for the full three-month phase. Assignments are officially made by department administration based on specialization fit, but senior Aurors with enough standing can influence who they receive. Potter's trainee selection is the most visible example — everyone knows he picks his own.

**Jackie's assignment to Potter** is the cohort's most talked-about pairing. From the outside it reads as favoritism — the Slytherin girl married to a Nott, involved with a Malfoy, getting the golden assignment. From Potter's side, it's a judgment call based on her abilities. Either way, it makes her a target.

**When a trainee is temporarily unavailable** (illness, leave, personal emergency), a substitute from the cohort may volunteer to cover their Senior Auror's paperwork and ride-along duties. Drake Lightwood volunteered to sub for Jackie during her week-long absence in late January 2002 and left the filing in such disarray that Jackie spent her first days back working overtime to repair it.

**Core Activities:**

- **The Ride-Along:** Trainees trail their assigned Auror on daily duties — breaking up brawls in Knockturn Alley, investigating cursed artifacts at Borgin and Burkes, responding to dark magic disturbances, executing warrants, interviewing witnesses. Trainees observe first, then gradually participate under direct supervision.

- **The Paperwork Penalty:** The trainee is responsible for filing every piece of paperwork generated by their Senior Auror's cases. If the Senior Auror casts a spell in a Muggle area, the trainee fills out the Obliviator requisition forms. If an arrest is made, the trainee processes the booking documentation. Teaches accountability and the administrative backbone of casework.

- **Surveillance Operations:** Extended stakeout duty. Sitting in a Disillusioned tent for 14 hours watching a suspected smuggling drop point. Teaches patience, observation skills, and the unglamorous reality of investigative work.

- **Live Backup Protocols:** Trainees learn their role in active operations — securing perimeters, managing civilian crowds, providing suppressing cover if their Senior Auror is engaged. By the end of Phase 3, trainees may participate in low-risk elements of live operations at their Auror's discretion.

## Phase 4: The Crucible and Independence (Months 10–12: April–June)

*The training wheels come off. The trainee takes the lead; the Senior Auror steps back to evaluate.*

**Core Activities (April–May):**

- **The Cold Case Assignment:** Each trainee is handed a low-priority unsolved case from the Ministry archives. They must independently track down new leads, re-interview witnesses, and attempt to close the case using their full skillset. This is individual work — no partner, no safety net.

- **Leading a Raid:** The trainee acts as lead tactical officer for a low-risk warrant execution (e.g., raiding an illegal potion-brewing operation). They must brief the squad, plan and execute the breach, and process arrests. Senior Aurors observe but do not intervene unless safety demands it.

- **The Wizengamot Trial:** Trainees take the stand in a simulated (or occasionally real, minor) Wizengamot proceeding. They must defend their arrest reports and evidence handling under aggressive cross-examination. Tests composure, legal knowledge, and the ability to withstand pressure from defense counsel.

- **Specialization Declaration:** By late Phase 4, trainees indicate their preferred division for post-graduation placement — general enforcement, dark artifact recovery, organized crime, counter-intelligence, protective detail, etc. Preferences are considered alongside performance evaluations and departmental need.

## The Final Field Trial (June — 3 Weeks Maximum)

*The capstone examination. The cohort is split into two competing three-person squads and deployed into a contained, warded environment. Same conspiracy, different starting positions. First squad to crack it wins. Three weeks is the ceiling, not the expectation.*

**Overview:**
Senior Aurors spend weeks preparing the Trial location in advance — staging evidence, planting enchanted "witnesses" (Ministry actors under glamours or sophisticated behavioral enchantments), constructing environmental hazards, and designing a fully realized dark magic conspiracy for the trainees to unravel. The Trial is the closest thing to real field conditions the program can simulate.

**The location changes every year.** Past sites have included abandoned wizarding villages, decommissioned Ministry outposts, uninhabited islands, and stretches of warded wilderness. The area is fully enclosed by anti-Apparition and containment wards. Trainees cannot leave; senior Aurors monitor from observation points outside the perimeter.

**Squad Assignments:**
The cohort is divided into two squads of three by program leadership. Assignments are deliberate — designed to test dynamics, not reward friendships. Obvious alliances may be split; friction pairings may be forced. Trainees are not consulted.

Both squads are dropped into the same warded zone at different entry points. Each receives a different initial evidence packet — meaning they're investigating the same conspiracy from different angles. They share the environment but not a base camp.

**What each squad receives on deployment:**
- Their wands
- Basic survival supplies (shelter, limited rations)
- A briefing packet with starting intel specific to their squad's entry angle
- One emergency extraction signal per person (single-use; triggering it is an automatic failure for that individual)

**The Mechanic — Accusation and Takedown:**
The Trial operates on a solve-and-execute model. When a squad believes they've identified the culprit, the method, and the conspiracy behind it, they present a formal accusation at a designated checkpoint within the zone (an enchanted location, a handler NPC, or a sealed tribunal object — varies by year). They must name the target, present physical evidence, and lay out the conspiracy's structure.

- **Correct accusation:** The squad receives the target's location and must execute a final takedown operation — a raid, arrest, or extraction against a senior Auror playing the role of the dark wizard. They do not go easy. Successful takedown = extraction and completion.
- **Incorrect accusation:** Penalty. Consequences vary by year but are always significant — confiscated supplies, a 24-hour lockout from the accusation checkpoint, a new "complication" unleashed into the zone. Wrong guesses cost time, resources, and momentum.

**Winning and Finishing:**
The first squad to correctly accuse and successfully execute the takedown wins. They are extracted from the zone and the Trial is over for them. A sharp, efficient squad can finish well under the three-week window.

The second squad remains in the field. They can still pass the Trial by completing it themselves — solving the case and executing their own takedown — but they do so knowing the other squad already won. Finishing second isn't failure, but it isn't glory either. If the three-week window expires without a solve, the Trial ends and the remaining squad is extracted regardless.

**Interaction Between Squads:**
The program does not dictate how squads should handle encountering each other in the field. They share the same warded zone, the same "witnesses," the same crime scenes. Whether squads cooperate, share information, compete silently, deliberately mislead each other, or avoid contact entirely is left to the trainees. This is part of the test.

**Environmental Pressure:**
The zone is not neutral. Weather enchantments, released creatures, difficult terrain, supply caches that must be located, and hazards that escalate over time all create pressure alongside the investigative work. The conspiracy also "fights back" as squads get closer to the truth — simulated ambushes, traps on evidence locations, "witnesses" turning hostile, planted misinformation. The deeper in you dig, the harder the zone pushes back.

**What changes year to year:**
- Location and terrain
- The case (dark artifact smuggling ring, underground blood magic cult, werewolf trafficking operation, cursed object serial attacker, etc.)
- Environmental hazards and creature types
- Number and behavior of planted NPCs
- The penalty for incorrect accusations
- The twist (there is always at least one)

**What stays constant:**
- Three-week maximum, no outside contact
- Two squads of three, competing
- Accusation-and-takedown solve mechanic
- Senior Auror monitoring and safety oversight
- Formal debrief and individual evaluation post-extraction (for both squads, regardless of who won)

**Evaluation:**
Both squads are evaluated individually regardless of who won. Trainees are assessed on investigative reasoning, tactical decision-making, leadership, teamwork, magical proficiency, physical endurance, composure under stress, and adaptability. The winning squad's assessments reflect their efficiency; the second squad is evaluated on their process and whether they ultimately completed the Trial. The Final Field Trial is weighted heavily in the overall program evaluation. Trainees can pass the year but fail the Trial — which typically results in a conditional pass requiring additional supervised fieldwork before full certification.

---

## Retention & Washout

The tryout process is where most candidates are eliminated. Once admitted to the program, trainees are generally not cut unless they commit a serious disciplinary or ethical violation. However, trainees **can fail the final evaluation** — either the Field Trial specifically or the cumulative year assessment. Failure means no Auror certification; candidates may reapply to the program the following year, but must undergo tryouts again from scratch.

There is no formal ranking system within the cohort, but evaluations are individual and comparative performance is visible to instructors. Trainees always know, roughly, where they stand relative to each other.
