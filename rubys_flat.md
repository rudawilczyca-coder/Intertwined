# Ruby's Flat — Spatial Layout & Room Guide

*One-bedroom flat, approximately 40–45 sqm. London.*

---

## Layout at a Glance

The front door opens into a short entry corridor — barely three steps. Bathroom door immediately on the left, utility cupboard (washer/dryer) tucked into the right wall. The corridor feeds directly into the living room without a door, just a threshold where the flooring shifts from tile to warm hardwood.

The living room is the hub. Everything connects through it. Bedroom door on the left wall. Kitchen through an open archway on the right. Glass doors on the far wall lead out to the balcony. Standing at the sofa, you can see into the kitchen, hear someone in the bathroom, and smell whatever's blooming on the balcony. The flat is small enough that sound carries everywhere — a kettle clicking off, a page turning, rain on the balcony railing.

---

## The Rooms

### Entry
Narrow. Coat hooks on one wall, shoes kicked underneath. A small mirror. The kind of space you move through, not linger in.

### Living Room — 15.5 sqm
The bookshelf dominates: a full wall of dark shelving, floor to ceiling, books organized by color — reds bleeding into oranges into yellows into greens into blues into purples into blacks along the bottom. Framed photographs and small potted plants are tucked between the spines wherever there's room. A trailing vine has claimed the upper left corner.

The sofa is deep green velvet, well-loved, positioned to face the shelves and the television mounted on the opposite wall. A chunky knit throw lives permanently on one arm. The coffee table is rustic — reclaimed wood, low to the ground, perpetually holding a mug, a candle, and whatever book is mid-read. An armchair sits at an angle near the window. A dog bed on the floor between the sofa and the shelves — grey, well-used, with a rope toy in it.

The light in here is warm and low. A brass floor lamp by the sofa. Afternoon sun through the window hits the coffee table and the rug — a thick, cream-colored thing that softens the hardwood.

### Kitchen — 7.7 sqm
Galley-style, entered through an open archway from the living room. Counter on both sides, narrow enough that two people would have to negotiate around each other. Gas hob, deep ceramic sink, open shelving holding mismatched mugs and jars. A small dining table with two chairs is wedged against the far wall — just enough room for breakfast, a laptop, or a bottle of wine and two glasses.

Plants on the windowsill here too. The kitchen smells like whatever was last cooked and faintly of herbs.

### Bedroom — 12.2 sqm
Dark and warm. A double bed with a dark wood slatted headboard, dressed in deep forest-green velvet — a heavy coverlet with gold embroidery (a fox among flowers and stars). Gold and green cushions piled against the headboard. Cream sheets underneath. The bed is made but lived-in, not military-precise.

A Tiffany-style lamp on the bedside table casts amber and green light. Fairy lights are strung along the wall above the headboard, tangled through trailing ivy that's been allowed to grow unchecked along a high shelf. The shelf holds books, small framed photographs, and dried flowers. More photographs pinned to the wall — snapshots, postcards, a mixture of Muggle and wizarding prints.

A reading chair by the window with a blanket draped over it — the kind of chair that collects things: a cardigan, a book left open, a scarf. A desk against one wall with a lamp, a laptop, and a controlled scatter of papers and notebooks. A dark patterned rug underfoot.

The room feels like a den. Enclosed, layered, deliberately dim.

### Bathroom — 4 sqm
Small. Shower stall (no tub), toilet, pedestal sink. Hexagonal floor tile. A mirror above the sink. Functional, clean, slightly cluttered with products along the sink edge. A candle on the shelf above the toilet. Towels on a hook behind the door.

### Utility Cupboard — 1.2 sqm
Washer/dryer stacked. Detergent. Ironing board folded against the wall. The door stays closed.

### Balcony
Narrow — barely wide enough for one person to stand comfortably, running the width of the living room's back wall. Accessed through glass doors from the living room.

Every inch is used. Lavender and herbs in terracotta pots crowd the railing. A small wooden bench with mismatched cushions (green, mustard, rust) and layered blankets — this is where she reads when the weather allows. Fairy lights wound through the railing. A mug left on the floor beside the bench. The view is London rooftops and trees — terraced houses, chimney pots, the canopy of a street-level garden visible below.

The balcony smells like lavender and city air. On warm evenings with the glass doors open, the scent drifts into the living room.

## Spatial Relationships (Writing Quick-Reference)

- **From the sofa** → you can see into the kitchen through the archway, hear the front door open, and feel the breeze if the balcony doors are open.
- **From the bed** → the bedroom door opens directly onto the living room. No hallway buffer. If someone's on the sofa, you'd see the back of their head.
- **From the kitchen** → you can talk to someone on the sofa without raising your voice. The dining table faces the archway.
- **From the balcony** → looking back through the glass doors, you see the full living room. The bookshelf wall is the backdrop.
- **Sound carries everywhere.** This flat has no secrets. A phone ringing in the bedroom is audible from the kitchen. The shower running is audible from the sofa.
- **Two people in the kitchen** requires negotiation — someone's always in someone else's way.
- **The bedroom is the only truly private space**, and only if the door is shut.
