## THE MORNING (mention-only — one breath of buildup)

Ordinary bad Thursday. Cramps before dawn; she books the day off; hot water bottle, worst-day supplies. Charlie collects Vex mid-morning — Jordie's whole body wagging, Vex smug, some small warm exchange on the landing. The flat goes quiet in the way she *chose*. **The craft note:** everything in the morning should read as comfort, and reread — after the knock — as the dismantling of her defenses one kindness at a time. The dog leaves because she loves her neighbor. The door opens because nobody expects the worst on a cramps-and-blanket day. Don't signpost it. Let it land on the reread.

## PRESSURE CURVE — BEATS

*(Ruby's actions/dialogue/interiority are Alice's, always. Beats below mark Robbie's pressure and Draco's track; her moves between them are hers alone. Building-entry socket: how Robbie got past the street door — held door, trade buzzer, a delivery lie — pick in the moment or leave unexplained; menace prefers the unexplained.)*

### Beat 1 — THE DOOR
The knock. Not pounding — *normal*, which is worse. She opens (Alice's call how far, chain or no chain). Robbie in the frame: bigger than the texts, drunk-sharp, smiling like this is a romcom reunion. Opening register is the text-voice made flesh — "just want to talk," "five minutes," "you owe me that much" — but LOUD now, the volume knob he could hide in writing turned up. The foot, the shoulder, the weight: he's inside before "no" finishes forming. Door forced is locked canon — however it plays, he does not get *invited*.

### Beat 2 — OCCUPATION
He walks the flat like he's already been living there in his head — touching things, narrating, sarcastic inventory ("nice gaff, princess, cyber money buy this?"). Territory display. **The knife enters here, casually** — not brandished, *fidgeted*: flicked open-closed while he talks, a dark mirror of Theo's lighter. It's not a threat yet. It's a fact he's letting her live with. He clocks the missing dog out loud. That's the premeditation reveal — he watched, he timed it, and he *tells her*, because to him surveillance is proof of devotion.

### Beat 3 — THE REWRITE, PERFORMED LIVE
The text-arc's greatest hits, now with a body attached: he's changed, he did the work, "what happened" (never named — pivots to his pain if pressed), she was part of the problem, nobody ever got him like she did. The blocking of his number = proof someone poisoned her. Crissy material surfaces: he knows about Wales, the shop, the collapse — *"heard you fell apart in the street. That's what leaving me did to you."* Everything reversed, everything his. He believes every word. **Never a cartoon.**

### Beat 4 — THE POSH FELLA
Her own shield turned blade. She told him Draco was nothing — a married man, a bit on the side — and he says it BACK to her, vulgar and delighted: so she's *that* now, some rich man's convenience, that's what she left him for? The married-man detail makes it filthier in his mouth and makes Draco *smaller* — safe to rage about, safe to threaten. Family enters as leverage, his mouth only: her mam's address, Mike's job, "one phone call, princess." (None of it played out; all of it said.)

### Beat 5 — HANDS
The gradient: blocking her path → the wrist → the phone ("who are you texting, WHO ARE YOU TEXTING") → shove or wall or grip, dealer's choice — **ceiling: violent, terrifying, no real injury.** The knife stays in his hand through it, still "casual." The gun, if in play (see sockets), prints under his jacket here — seen, never drawn pre-Draco. Somewhere in this beat Ruby's break for the bathroom happens ON HER TERMS (Alice's move entirely — the scaffold only guarantees the corridor of opportunity exists: he's drunk, he gestures big, he turns to kick something or sweep a shelf; a two-second window).

### Beat 6 — THE BATHROOM / THE TEXT (split-screen)
**Her side (Alice's):** lock, back to the door, phone. The text to Draco — whatever it says, it arrives.

**His side — DRACO'S TRACK (mine):** Haven House, ordinary Thursday evening, phone face-up since Monday because some part of him has been waiting all week and calling it nothing. The text lands. THE COLLISION, compressed to seconds: her grass-promise (*don't engage him, he'll hurt you*) against his own (*if he comes to your door, I won't stand against the wall*) — and the promises were never compatible; tonight is just when they finally touch. Cab: twenty minutes. The other way: seconds.

**The wand beat — render this with full weight:** he sets it down. On the hall table, deliberate, both hands, the way you'd disarm before walking into a cell. Not mercy — *math*. Wand in the flat = magic in front of her = her memory on a Ministry table. He will go into a room with an armed man carrying nothing but his hands and his name before he lets anyone take an hour of her out of her head. If Jackie or Theo is home to witness the leaving — one line, no explanation, no time (socket, not requirement).

The crack of Apparition in the back alley, gone before an echo. Statute broken #1 and he doesn't spend a single thought on it. Robbie hears/registers nothing — a sound like a car door.

*(Meanwhile Beat 5's energy continues at the bathroom door: Robbie at the wood, the register curdling fully — coaxing to vicious and back per breath. "Open the door, we're TALKING." The knife against the frame, idle. Working the handle.)*

### Beat 7 — THE DOOR, AGAIN (scaffold's last beat)
Stairs. The flat door — forced once already tonight, it isn't stopping anyone now. Draco through it, wandless, in whatever he was wearing at home. First fact: he hears Robbie before he sees him. Second fact: bathroom door shut = she's *behind* it = alive, barricaded, waiting. Third fact: the two men see each other.

**HARD STOP. The scaffold ends here — three people in the flat, one locked door, one knife, no resolution line.** From this breath forward it's live play: Robbie's next move is mine on the night, Draco's is mine, Ruby's are Alice's, and nobody — including us — gets to know the ending before it happens. Post-Draco ceiling applies: nothing that kills or permanently disfigures. Everything else is weather.

## ROBBIE — FLAT VOICE SPEC (spoken ≠ text voice)

The texts were *engineered* — lowercase, reasonable-passing, plausible-deniability menace. **In person the engineering is gone.** Drunk-sharp: LOUD, vulgar, fast, sarcastic — the brute the world is filled to the brim with, not a mastermind, not cold. Draco's register is cold fury; Robbie must be its opposite in every measurable way — heat, volume, profanity, self-pity with its shirt off. He laughs at his own lines. He swings coaxing→vicious inside a sentence and doesn't notice the swerve. Nicknames as leashes: *Princess*, Little Red. The rewrite grammar stays ("what happened," "that night," his pain as trump card) but shouted, sworn-through, interruptible by his own grievances. **Still never a cartoon: every vile thing he says, he believes. He is the romantic lead of his own story — drunk, armed, and heartbroken in a way that has never once included her as a person.**

*(No POV. Ever. He arrives; his interiority lives only in HOW the words and hands behave.)*
