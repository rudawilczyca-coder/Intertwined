When Jackie woke up, the first thing she felt was that she was hungry. She opened her eyes slowly. Her lap was empty — evidently Luna decided her work was done at some point and relocated to an unknown location in the House — and Draco's chest occupied most of her vision. Draco’s breathing, bit by bit, losing its vigilance.

The blood had done its work slowly. The raw pull beneath his bandages had settled into a deep ache, and the room no longer tilted when he turned his head. He was still weak. But three metres of carpet would no longer put him on the floor.

Progress.

“Luna’s in the library. Filippo is upstairs pretending he isn’t listening to the entire house.” One corner of Theo’s mouth moved. “Nothing has caught fire. I checked.”

He let his head rest against the chair again, watching Draco sleep with the wary concentration of someone who still expected the peace to be interrupted merely because it had lasted.

“The donor isn’t due until nine. Filippo wants another bag in me before then.” Theo glanced towards the two collapsed bags on the low table. “Apparently starving for three days doesn’t excuse poor manners with dinner.”

He set both hands on the arms of the chair and pushed himself upright. The movement held steady until the last few inches, when one hand clenched hard against the upholstery. Theo stayed there, half-risen, jaw set, until the weakness passed. Then he straightened.

“Don’t wake him. I’m going to the kitchen before Filippo comes down to supervise me drinking from plastic.”

He took one careful step towards the kitchen, moving quietly enough that Draco’s breathing did not change.

The Malice receded without argument. That frightened him more than the hunger would have.

Footsteps sounded overhead: measured, unhurried, too precise to belong to anyone who had just woken on a sofa.

Theo drank before Filippo could reach the stairs.

The glass stopped halfway to Theo's mouth.

Tuesday night. The first hours beneath Viola's roof, when hunger had still been an ache rather than the only fact in the room. When the Malice had thrown Himself against every boundary Theo possessed, then gone abruptly, unnaturally still.

Theo set the glass down. Blood lapped the rim and left a dark crescent on the crystal.

*You reached her.*

The answer came without triumph.

*I called.*

His chair scraped back. Theo was on his feet before weakness caught up with the decision, one hand closing hard around the table's edge, his attention never leaving Jackie. He wanted to cross the kitchen. The wanting itself held him where he stood.

“Yes.”

There was no useful way to soften it. Not when her sleeping mind had taken the touch of Him and given it the shape of the worst thing Theo had ever done to her.

“He reached through the bond. I didn't know He could. I didn't know He had.” Theo swallowed; hunger turned the small motion raw. “He wasn't sending you the nightmare. He was trying to tell you I had been taken, and distance—sleep—whatever remains between you and Him translated it into the only language your mind had for His voice.”

The explanation sounded clinical. The blood on the rim made it obscene.

“You did nothing wrong. You weren't meant to understand something none of us knew was possible.”

Measured footsteps reached the bottom of the stairs. Theo heard them and let them come.

“And I missed you too.” His fingers tightened once against the wood. “By Thursday, I had stopped pretending that wasn't the reason I wanted to come home.”

Filippo appeared in the kitchen doorway, damp hair combed back, shirtsleeves fastened with his usual precision. His gaze went first to Theo standing, then to the untouched glass, then to the distance Theo had kept between himself and Jackie.

“The Malice reached her through the bond,” Theo said. “From the court.”

Filippo's expression changed by less than most people's breathing.

“Then today you recover,” he said. “Tomorrow, we learn what else crossed with Him.”
