### Jackie

She looked at Draco, then at Leandro. She didn't draw any particular pleasure from making him suffer. She also fought The Malice and survived. She knew better than most how powerful vampires were.

Finally she groaned, got up, and walked to the man. She braced her hand on his stomach as she pulled the dagger out with the other on. It came out with a hiss and a squelch — the sound lound in the quiet room, making her stomach uneasy — and Jackie wiped the knife on the sleeve of his shirt before stepping back and sitting back down. She didn't put the dagger away. Instead she played with it for a while.

When Bastien asked his next question, she looked sideways at him. Jackie wished she could have a different answer for him, but in her hierarchy, keeping Theo safe was higher than beying a loyal cousin.

"Yes. That was supposed to be Theo. And yes, if not for him," she pointed at Leandro with the tip of her dagger, "you would've left for the chateaux tomorrow non the wiser."

### Bastien

"Were you afraid I'd tell someone, or did I simply not get a say in whether I could be trusted?"

### Jackie

"I didn't think you'd tell someone. Not consciously and not by choice. But who to tell was mostly Theodore's choice, and we understood why he wanted as little people to know as possible. It's really not personal."

### Bastien

"It feels personal from this side. But all right—his choice. Is he safe to be around? I mean Theodore, not the one tied to the chair."

### Jackie

She laughed dryly. "They are vampires. They are not safe by definition." She was quiet for a moment. "But yes, Theodore won't hurt me or Draco. Not because he's not capable of that, but because he chooses not to, every day."

### Bastien

"You and Draco. What about the rest of us? If he'd come tonight, would Élodie have been safe sitting beside him?"

### Jackie

"Oh for fucks sake, yes. He feeds from bags and willing donors. He's not a rabid animal." She left out the part the sentence should have ended on - not anymore.

### Bastien

"I didn't know that until you told me. You said vampires weren't safe; I'm asking about my sister." A pause. "Does Eli know Theodore's been using his face?"

### Jackie

She was quiet for a while.

"Yes. So I suppose that means he's included in the list of people who know. Sorry. See, that's why we need to keep it tight. The issue really is that Theo didn't technically have to fake his death. But if he didn't, he'd have to register with the Ministry and..." She bit exhaled hard.

"The Ministry treas vampires like a problem to be managed. He was better off staying off their radar."

### Bastien

"All right. I won't tell anyone." A pause. "But what happens when you go to London? Who stays here with him—and what do we tell Grand-mère if she asks?"

### Jackie

"Who who? Him?" She pointed the dagger at Leandro again. "He's coming with us. I'm not taking any chances. As far as Grandmère knows, Theo came, spent the evening as Eli and had a lot of fun. She liked Theo as a man and she accepted him as a vampire, but she'd be much less forgiving if she knew another vampire took his disguise and sat near her daughter and grandchildren. The last thing I need is involving her in that."

She closed her eyes for a moment.

"She'll find out anyway. Hopefully by that time the matter with Camarilla will be resolved and we'll send her an extensive apology letter from London."

### Bastien

"Send it after you've got Theodore home. She'll be furious, but at least she'll know he's safe." He rubbed his eyes. "All right. What can I do while we wait? I'd rather help than sit here asking questions you don't want to answer."

### Jackie

She looked outside through the enchanted window. It had to be past midnight, maybe even later.

"There's nothing you can do. Unlike him, we need to sleep. You can take the first watch with Draco and keep him at wand point."

She yawned. She was exhausted and the adrenaline started to drain out of her veins.

"I'll take a nap, then Draco will. Hopefully we'll get an answer from Florence before sunrise and we can go. Then you stay here and cover our asses."

She moved closer to him, and kissed his cheek.

"Thank you, cousin."

Then Jackie crossed the room, and laid down on the bed Draco was sitting on. She folded herself in a fetal position, laying her head close to Draco's knee. She put the dagger right in front of her on the bed.

"If he as much as looks at you sideways..." And then she closed her eyes.

### Draco

“I'll notice,” Bastien said. He touched the cheek she had kissed, then drew his wand and pulled a chair across from Leandro. Draco waited until he was settled before lowering his own hand. His wrist ached. He rested it on his thigh, keeping the wand pointed towards the prisoner, and drew the folded blanket from the foot of the bed over Jackie without covering the dagger.

For a while, neither of them spoke. Outside, someone was still attempting the Welsh anthem, though fewer people joined in each time he reached the chorus.

Pansy returned shortly after half past midnight. She slipped through the door with her shoes in one hand and told Draco the letters had gone, then stopped at the sight of Jackie beside his knee. Whatever else she had intended to say could wait. She put her shoes beneath the other bed and sat down.

The potion wore off before one.

Draco saw the change begin around Leandro's mouth and sat forward at once. Bastien raised his wand, although there was nothing to cast at. Eli's features shifted beneath their attention; his hair shortened into dense black curls, his cheeks changed, and the unfamiliar face that emerged had warm brown skin and amber-brown eyes. The ropes tightened unevenly around the altered shoulders. Draco renewed them before Leandro could test the slack.

“There,” Leandro said, looking at Bastien. “You may stop looking for your cousin.”

Bastien kept his wand where it was. “I had stopped. This helps.”

Without Eli's face, the bloodied shirt looked different too. Draco found he could look at the damaged side without remembering Theo accepting his hand in the stadium. He wasn't sure that made him a better person to keep watch, but it made the next hour easier.

Geneviève and Vivienne came back while Pansy was fetching water. Their voices reached the bedroom briefly when she opened the door again; Vivienne was complaining about the walk, and Geneviève told her to lower her voice before she woke the entire tent. Pansy shut the door carefully and stood against it until their footsteps had passed.

“They think we've all gone to bed,” she whispered.

Bastien looked at the ceiling. Nobody laughed.

By two, Draco had begun checking his watch whenever Leandro moved. By three, he was checking it when he didn't. Bastien offered twice to take over properly, and twice Draco said he would lie down in a minute. The water in his glass grew warm. His knee had gone stiff beneath Jackie's head, but he left it where it was.

The reply came at twenty past three. Pansy brought it in from the front of the tent, read the short note twice, and handed it to Draco.

Filippo had secured a Portkey. He needed someone at the north field.

“I'll go,” she said, already reaching for her shoes.

She was back less than half an hour later. This time she opened the bedroom door wide enough for someone to follow her.

Filippo entered in a dark coat, his black hair disturbed by the wind. He paused inside the doorway. His gaze moved from Bastien's wand to the ropes, then to the blood on Leandro's shirt. It stayed there for a moment before finding Draco and the bed.

“You came yourself,” Draco said. He hadn't meant to sound surprised.

“Yes.” Filippo took off his coat and laid it over the end of the other bed. “Your letter did not suggest that another letter would be sufficient.”

He came closer, stopping short of the silver lying on the blanket. There were questions Draco had spent hours rehearsing, but now that someone had arrived who might answer them, he couldn't choose which to ask first.

Filippo looked down towards Jackie. His voice softened.

“Cara. I am here.”

### Jackie

She came in and out of sleep several times, adjusting her body in small measures available on the single bed she shared with Draco. Once she grabbed the hilt of the dagger, without opening her eyes. She heard some parts of the words spoken around them, but she let them pass, her mind compartmentalizing to allow herself the rest she could take.

Filippo's voice brought her back in one instant. She sat on the bed, pushing her wild red curls back from her face.

"Filippo, you came."

Some tight, unbearable knot of fear and anxiousness that lodged itself in her chest from the moment they realized Eli was not Theo, eased by a fraction finally.

Then she looked at the chair, and she jerked, seeing a completely different man sitting in it. In theory she knew the potion would wear off. The difference was stark enough to startle her into reaching for the dagger before her mind caught up.

"Signor de'Medici," she said, her voice turning cold, her eyes fixed on Leandro's hazel ones. "Meet Leandro. He was so kind as to inform us that our husband is detained at London court. After he spent the entire evening pretending to be him."

### Draco

Filippo put a hand on Jackie's shoulder. "Of course I came." He left it there only long enough to say it, then turned towards the chair. Draco straightened his cramped leg, pins and needles running down into his foot. He had imagined this arrival so often over the last hour that the ordinary business of making room for another person seemed strangely inadequate. Pansy moved the water glass off the trunk. Bastien stayed seated with his wand raised.

"Leandro Velluti," Filippo said, considering him. "You have had a difficult evening."

"Your family has been inhospitable." Leandro shifted against the chair, then stopped when the movement pulled at his side. Filippo glanced at the wound but made no move to examine it. "They discovered a stranger where they expected Theodore. I would have advised you to expect worse." There was no pleasure in his voice. He drew the other chair closer and sat, leaving enough space that Leandro would have to make a conspicuous effort to reach him. "Before we discuss your treatment, I would like to know what you can actually tell me about his. Did you see him yourself?"

"Yes. Before I left London. He was alive." Leandro looked between Filippo and Draco. "I have already told them that."

"And now you are telling me." Filippo rested his hands loosely on his knees. "Have you received any news of him since?" This time Leandro took a moment before answering. "No." Draco pressed his heel into the carpet until the prickling in his foot became pain he could put somewhere. All those hours, and they still had no more than this man's account of a single moment before he left London. Filippo did not ask them to be reassured by it. "Then we shall need more recent information. What message did Viola entrust to you for me?"

"My instructions concerned Theodore's household." Leandro's mouth moved towards a smile without quite arriving. "Which has proved rather more resourceful than anticipated." Filippo waited. Under the Accounting." Filippo looked at him then. "I am." The answer was immediate, and Draco found himself angrier for how simple it sounded. "Then I would like to understand why we've spent the night getting our information from the man who took his place."

Filippo lowered his eyes to his hands for a moment. "So would I." When he looked up, he met Draco's gaze directly. "I received your letter. I came. I have not spoken to Viola, and I will not pretend that I know what has passed between them." Draco held his gaze, waiting for the qualification that would make this another conversation about patience. It did not come. "I intend to see Theodore," Filippo continued. "You need not persuade me of the urgency."

He turned his chair slightly towards the bed, keeping Leandro within sight. "But this man was sent here for something. Until I understand what he has learned, I cannot judge what Viola may already have gained from it." His attention settled on Jackie, the formality easing without the question becoming gentler. "Tell me about the evening, cara. What did he ask you while you believed he was Theodore?"

### Jackie

She was glad to put the responsibility of managing Leandro in someone else's hands. With a small prick of guild Jackie realized, she believed Draco and Bastien were capable enough to hold him, but not to know how to speak with him or what questions to ask him.

She took that time to wake up fully, accepting a glass of water from Draco. She drank the entire glass, then reached across Draco and put it on the bedside table.

"Bastien," she looked at her cousin, "Pansy, you can go now. You should get some sleep too."

"We'll be gone by morning. Grandmère expects us to leave before dawn anyway." She hugged Pansy and then Bastien.

"We'll keep in touch with you that way." She tried for a laugh. "This is just for safekeeping though, you'll send it back when you're sure we're safe."

"He asked how we are, then nothing in particular. If he wanted to extract information, he had ample opportunity to do so, and he did not. Which makes me think his only goal here is to sow discord and confuse us."

"He can't be that valuable to this Prince Viola, if she sent him off on a mission like that."

### Draco

Pansy closed her fingers around the ring before looking at Draco. He had watched it leave Jackie's hand with a small, unreasonable resistance that he kept to himself. ### Jackie

The packing was a quiet and fast affair. Having just arrived, Jackie and Draco's things were all still packed in their trunks. She put on her corset and a worn-down hoodie over her dress. It made her look a little unruly, but nights in August could be cold in London.

### Draco

Draco wiped his scraped palm against his trousers and moved to the foot of the stair, keeping the open doorway in sight while he waited for the others. ### Jackie

She had her wand tucked safely in the holster when they touched the Portkey. She knew they would be deposited in the Underground. She had to brace her hands on her knees for a second, but straightened as soon as Draco lit up the passage with his wand.
