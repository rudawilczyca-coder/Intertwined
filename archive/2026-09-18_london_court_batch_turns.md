### Theodore

For the first instant, Theo did exactly what he had promised. He did not move.

Jackie's heart struck against his cheek, too fast and fiercely alive. Beneath it ran the blood he could smell through skin and cloth, close enough that the hunger lifted its head again despite the dead blood cooling in his stomach. His mouth remained closed. His hands stayed open against the mattress while she called him an idiot. Holding still was the only proof he had offered her, and he would not break it the moment she trusted him enough to cross the room.

Then she shook.

Theo's arms came up slowly. One bandaged hand closed in the fabric at the back of her hoodie. The other settled against her ribs without pulling; she had already brought him against her. His grip had steadied for Nell. It trembled now for an entirely different reason.

“​Yes,” he said against her. His voice caught in the cloth and came out rough. “I am.”

The Malice received what Jackie opened to Him. Theo could not feel what she gave Him; death had left him on the wrong side of that door. He felt only what it did inside their shared mind. The Malice went utterly still. No answer forced its way down the bond. No cold pressure pushed back through it. Hunger remained, awake and listening, but it stayed behind the line.

*She came,* He said, and for once there was no triumph in it.

*Be still.*

*I am.*

Theo tightened his fingers once in Jackie's hoodie.

“We tried the door,” he said. “They had wards ready for me. For Him.” He did not lift his head from her chest; he could hear each beat beneath the words. “I didn't choose the bleeding. I went because I would not have Viola bring her court to Haven House. I thought I could hear her terms and leave. I was wrong.”

He swallowed. Her scent filled the back of his throat. The Malice did not move.

“After that, the only way out I could see was to stop holding Him.” Theo's hand flattened against her back, careful of the pressure. “I chose not to find out what that would do in a room I couldn't read. They used the choice. Every time they took more blood, I made it again.”

At the doorway, Draco lowered his wand completely. He came into the room, stopping beside her, and laid his free hand on Theo's uninjured shoulder. The contact landed lightly. The anger in it did not.

“You went because you thought you were keeping this from our door,” Draco said. “You brought it to us anyway.”

“I know.”

“Next time, you let us stand in the room with you before you decide what we can survive.”

Theo closed his eyes. There were answers that would have made the choice sound cleaner—Viola's summons, the court's reach, the danger of refusing. None of them changed who had been left outside it.

“Yes,” he said. “I was wrong. I'm sorry.”

His cheek remained over Jackie's heart. He did not ask her to stop shaking or to forgive him before the anger had finished existing.

“You don't have to finish that sentence,” he said quietly. “I know what he made you think. He was wrong. I'm here.”

His hand stayed at her back. Draco's stayed on his shoulder. For the first time since Viola's summons had arrived, Theo allowed both of them to carry some of his weight.

“And I'm done deciding alone.”

### Jackie

Jackie gave herself a moment. She gave all three of them a moment, and then stepped back. She wasn't cruel or stupid — she knew exactly how much not attacking her cost Theo, and she wasn't about to test the boundaries of his control over his hunger.

"We're getting you out of here."

She turned to Filippo, the hope painted on her face.

"Right?"

### Theodore

The moment Jackie stepped back, Theo released her.

His hands opened and fell to his thighs without reaching after her. The hunger followed the warmth of her for that single pace—bright blood retreating from beneath his cheek—but it stayed behind his teeth. The Malice watched her go from the back of Theo's mind and kept His silence.

Jackie's question crossed the room.

Theo looked at Filippo too.

### Filippo

Filippo had not interrupted the reunion. He stood beside the empty flask, one hand resting over its stopper, his expression still enough that the hope on Jackie's face struck against it without finding purchase.

Then he said, “Right.”

Not gently. Not as comfort. As an answer.

“Viola wanted to know where he lives and who answers for him. She has been given both.” His eyes moved to Theo, taking in the dressings, the open shirt, the effort it still cost him to sit without support. “He lives at Haven House. He remains my childe. I will answer before the Camarilla for the responsibility I neglected, and I will not offer Florence again as the price of his release.”

Theo's jaw tightened. “Don't promise her what you haven't won.”

“I am not promising her permission.” Filippo set the flask down on the bedside table with a small, precise click. “I am telling your wife what I intend to do.”

He looked back at Jackie.

“Viola asked for an undertaking. She will have one—from me. Not an undefined right to summon him whenever London finds it convenient. Not unnamed services. Not the surrender of his home or either of his marriages. If she wants obligations, she will name them, limit them, and put them before all three of you.”

Draco came to stand fully beside Jackie now, his wand still lowered in his hand.

“And if she refuses?” he asked.

Filippo's mouth changed by less than a smile.

“Then she may explain why a court that detained a childe to demand his sire's responsibility intends to overrule that sire the moment he accepts it.”

For the first time since he had entered the room, something in Theo's posture loosened—not relief yet, but the recognition of a weapon he understood.

Filippo drew his coat straight.

“I cannot promise that we walk out without an argument,” he said. “I can promise that I am finished leaving Theodore's place in this family unresolved.” His eyes settled on Jackie once more. “We are getting him out.”

### Jackie

As she listened to Filippo, her hands closed into fists. She knew Camarilla was dangerous, she knew what taking responsibility for Theo could have cost Filippo.
She also knew that Filippo was only in this situation, because he had agreed to try to save her husband, on her explicit request. If not for that, he would be in Florence now, free to live his life as he chose. Gratitude closed her throat slightly.
"We will do what is necessary. If he needs to join this court and answer Viola in order to stay with us, then that's what has to happen."
She stepped closer to Filippo, and placed her hand on his forearm.
"Thank you. We will never repay you for what you've done for us. And we will do as you say up there." She looked at the ceiling, to where Viola was undoubtedly waiting for them to return. "He just... We can't go to Florence." She let go of his arm, stepping towards Draco.
Jackie suspected that from Filippo's perspective, that would be the cleanest solution. He would have oversight over Theo, he could continue mentoring him, and wouldn't be alone. She also had a feeling she was part of the equation.
Even knowing that, she knew she and Draco couldn't uproot their lives in London. They couldn't sell Haven House — the first place where either of them truly felt at home — and more to Italy permanently.

### Filippo

Filippo looked down at Jackie's hand on his forearm.

He did not cover it with his own. He did not withdraw from it either. For those few seconds he stood perfectly still and accepted the gratitude with an expression that suggested it sat less comfortably on him than blame would have.

“No,” he said when she promised repayment. “You will not.”

His voice was quiet, but it stopped the room more effectively than volume would have.

“You asked me to help save your husband. That request did not create a debt. It reminded me that I had allowed my childe's position to remain unresolved because I believed there was time.” His gaze shifted briefly to Theo. “I believed his control, my standing and Viola's good sense would be sufficient when the matter was finally raised. I was wrong on every count that mattered.”

Theo watched him from the bed without interrupting.

“And don't promise to do as I say upstairs before you know what I intend to say,” Filippo continued. “I may make commitments as his sire. I will not purchase his release with your blind obedience.”

When Jackie let go and moved back towards Draco, Filippo's eyes followed her. Something old and carefully managed passed through his face.

“Florence would be simpler,” he said. “For me.”

Draco's hand found the small of Jackie's back as she reached him. He said nothing yet, but his stare sharpened.

Filippo met it without offence.

“I wanted all three of you there,” he said. “I wanted Theodore where I could teach him. I wanted the household somewhere the Accounting already recognised my authority.” His eyes returned to Jackie. “And yes. I wanted you near.”

The admission was bare, deprived of every flourish that might have turned it into persuasion.

“I still do. Wanting is not the same as taking.”

### Theodore

Theo lowered his eyes for a moment. The words reached something that four days of questioning had not: the possibility that Florence had never been only custody disguised as rescue, and that Filippo's mistake had been waiting for consent until waiting became neglect.

“I'm not leaving Haven House,” he said.

It cost him breath he did not need and strength he did. His voice remained steady.

“Neither are they. I will be presented to London. I will answer a lawful summons. I will accept responsibility for hunting in Viola's territory.” His gaze lifted to Filippo. “I will not promise unnamed services, and neither of them becomes collateral for my compliance.”

The Malice stirred at the last word—a cold shift, no more.

*Again,* He said.

*No,* Theo answered. *Never again.*

### Draco

“We aren't going to Florence,” Draco said. Not apologetically. Not as an accusation. “And we aren't selling our home to make Viola's paperwork neater.”

His palm remained against Jackie's back.

“But if presentation is what makes London acknowledge that he lives with us, then we do it properly. Tonight. With the terms spoken before anyone agrees to them.” He looked from Filippo to Theo. “All of us.”

Filippo inclined his head once.

“That,” he said, “is what I will take upstairs.”

He reached for the door, then paused with his hand short of the latch.

“I will ask for presentation, recognition of Haven House as Theodore's residence, and release into my custody for the remainder of the night. I will hear Viola's price. I will agree to nothing beyond my own responsibility until I bring that price back to this room.”

His attention settled on Jackie last.

“You do not have to repay me. You do have to tell me if the terms I return with are ones your family cannot live under.”

### Jackie

I wanted you near.
She knew. She understood why, better than anyone else in the room. She had recognized a kindred spirit in Filippo the moment she saw him in his fighting ring for the first time. Even when he used his glamour to make her attracted to him for a brief moment. She was ready to sell herself to him in exchange for Theo's life, because something in her recognized the man under the monster.
Their friendship was not the same as any other Jackie had. They saw each other rarely, they wrote to each other once a month at most. And yet, the letters were more meaningful than anything she had ever sent to Pansy. She respected Filippo and admired him. She knew how strong longing for a kindred soul could be for him, so she couldn't really blame his mind for using Florence as a solution.
"Thank you." She repeated, even though he said not to thank him. "We'll wait here then."
She then looked at Nell, who was still standing in the room, a little awkwardly.
"Are you done tending my husband's wounds?"
She couldn't help the clip in her voice. Even if the woman didn't hurt Theo herself, she saw it happen and did nothing.

### Nell

Nell's eyes dropped to Theo's arms.

“Not quite.”

She came forward without hurrying and set her case on the edge of the desk. The awkwardness left her the moment there was something physical to assess. She unfastened the dressing on Theo's right arm, checked the linen beneath it, then pressed two fingers lightly beside the wound rather than over it.

“I was waiting for him to finish being held,” she said. “Some things are more useful than a medic with cold hands.”

Theo's gaze flicked towards her. Nell ignored it.

“The wounds are clean. They aren't closing properly because he hasn't had enough blood to repair them. One flask stopped the deterioration; it didn't restore what was taken.” She wound fresh linen around his arm, firm and efficient. “He needs another feed before he sleeps. Stored blood. Not a convenient wrist, and certainly not hers.”

The last word came with a glance at Jackie. Not challenging. Exact.

“He doesn't take the stairs without support. He doesn't Apparate. He doesn't fight anyone because they say something intolerable upstairs.”

“I wasn't planning to,” Theo said.

“You have been planning it continuously since I met you.” Nell tucked the end of the bandage beneath itself. “Your restraint is not the same thing as an absence of intent.”

The Malice gave a low, private sound that might have been laughter.

Then Nell closed her case and met Jackie's eyes properly.

“I didn't watch them open his veins,” she said. The ease had gone from her voice. “I was called after. I knew why he was being kept too weak to heal, and I continued changing the dressings while the court continued doing it.”

She did not offer an excuse quickly enough to make it one.

“Refusing would have left him with whoever they sent next. Obeying meant I remained useful to the people holding him.” Her mouth tightened. “Neither of those facts makes me brave.”

Theo looked down at the new white linen.

“She kept them clean,” he said. “She didn't do this.”

“No,” Nell agreed. “I only made it survivable.”

She picked up the bloodied dressing and folded it in on itself until the stain disappeared.

“You don't have to like me, Mrs Nott-Malfoy. But when you leave, you need to know what condition he's in. And if Viola releases him without authorising more blood, you need to obtain it before you take him home.”

### Filippo

Filippo had remained beside the door through the exchange. He watched Nell close the case, then looked at Theo.

“Will you remain with him?”

“Yes.”

“With all three of them,” Filippo corrected.

Something passed through Nell's expression at the reminder that Draco and Jackie counted as people requiring protection here. It vanished before it could become agreement or offence.

“With all three,” she said.

Filippo opened the door.

“I will return with terms,” he told them. His attention paused on Jackie, carrying everything he had not allowed her gratitude to purchase. “Not a decision. Terms.”

Then he stepped into the corridor and shut the door behind him, leaving it unlocked.

### Jackie

She was just about to offer her blood, when Nell cut her off. She closed her mouth, disconcerted. Then the Malice actually laughed at what Nell said — or as close as he ever got to it — and Jackie's eyes darted to Theo in surprise.

She wanted to use the newfound bond to make some snide remark, but that would mean letting Him feel what she felt about and she really didn't want Him to know she was bothered by it.

"I guess I should thank you then?" She couldn't be bothered with surnames or titles. She didn't even remember if the woman gave her surname or not.

She came closer to the bed and sat next to Theo, bracing her hands on the edge of the mattress.
"So... I guess there's something I should tell you."
It had to be done, sooner or later, and Jackie came to the conclusion that doing it while Theo was too weak to properly deal with her might be a smart strategic move.

### Nell

Nell caught the question beneath Jackie's question and gave a small tilt of her head.

“You can, if it makes you feel better. It won't make me innocent.”

She snapped the last catch on her case. Whatever else she might have said died when Jackie sat beside Theo and announced a confession with both hands braced as though the mattress might move beneath her.

Nell looked from Jackie to Theo, then to Draco.

“I'll wait outside.”

“Filippo asked you to stay,” Draco said.

“And the door will be open, Mr Malfoy. If your husband falls over, shouts, attacks anyone or decides to prove he can walk, I will hear it.” She picked up her case. “If this is the sort of family conversation that produces all four, do them one at a time.”

She stepped into the corridor and left the door ajar behind her.

### Theodore

The Malice's approximation of laughter had already faded. In its place came a minute shift of attention towards the place Jackie had closed against Him.

*She shut the door,* He observed.

*Then leave it shut.*

The cold presence settled back without testing it. Fed, chastened, or simply curious enough to wait—Theo did not care which, provided the result held.

He looked at Jackie's hands beside his thigh. Her knuckles pressed into the mattress. Close enough to touch him, positioned so she could move first if she wanted to.

He shifted one hand from his thigh and laid it palm-up on the mattress between Jackie's braced hands. Not over either of them. An offer, nothing more.

“You've chosen the moment when I can't stand without assistance,” he said. His voice was dry; the weakness underneath it made the observation less threatening than it deserved to be. “Strategically sound.”

The corner of Draco's mouth moved despite himself.

Theo's did not. He watched Jackie, pale blue holding steady beneath the remnants of red.

“I won't ask Him,” he said. “And He won't look.”

*Rude,* the Malice murmured.

*Quiet.*

This time, He was.

Theo left his hand where it was.

“Tell me.”

### Jackie

She looked at his right hand, placed next to her left one. The openness of the gesture did nothing to ease her discomfort.
The worst part of it was, that it seemed like a joke mere hours ago. Reckless, dangerous, life-threatening, but a joke nonetheless. She expected Theo to understand. Now? It made her sick to her stomach, when she imagined what he was going through while she rocked her life on a whim.
She looked up at Draco, as if begging for salvation.
"No, Jackie. Sorry, but you have to be the one to tell him. I won't save you from that one." His voice was colder than it strictly needed to be, and Jackie could hear in it that he was still at least a little angry at her.
"Soo..." She started, looking at the mortar between the bricks on the other side of the room. Anything to help her ignore the rising anxiety.
"Yesterday, at the World Cup Final... You know, it was France versus Wales. Wales won by the way." She was stalling. She knew that, Draco and Theo knew her well enough to know that as well.
"Anyone, the Welsh had an actual dragon as their mascot. We went to see it and I kind of... Made a bet with Bastien on who's going to  come closer to the cage."
She looked at Draco again, helpless against guilt that now drilled a hole in her.
"He won't. But that's because the bet was on who would come closer... Outside of the wards."
She looked at the floor now, waiting for Theo to come to his own conclusions, because the entire unadorned truth refused to squeeze past her lips.

### Theodore

Theo's open hand remained between hers while Jackie spoke.

At *France versus Wales*, nothing changed. At *dragon*, his fingers twitched once. At *outside of the wards*, they closed against the mattress one by one until the offered palm became a fist.

The Malice rose behind his eyes—not forward, not towards the bond, but awake in every part of the mind they shared.

*She left the wards for a dragon.*

*I heard her.*

*While we were in a cage.*

*She didn't know.*

*The dragon would not have cared.*

Theo's jaw tightened.

Jackie had shut the bond against Him. The Malice could have pressed. Theo felt Him find the edge of that refusal and stop there, fury pacing behind it without so much as laying a claw against the door.

“Look at me,” Theo said.

His voice was quiet. The blood had restored enough strength that quiet no longer sounded fragile.

“Not the match. Not who won. Not Bastien.” His fist opened again, but he did not offer it a second time. “You crossed the wards protecting you from a live dragon because the wager specified that the distance would be measured outside them.”

He gave the sentence nowhere to hide. No joke-shaped corners. No unfinished implication she could leave for him to soften.

“How close did you get?”

Draco shifted beside her. “Theo—”

“No.” Theo's eyes stayed on Jackie. “You made her tell me. Let her tell me.”

Draco's mouth flattened, but he inclined his head and fell silent.

Theo leaned forward as far as his depleted body allowed, forearms settling on his thighs. The movement pulled at the fresh dressing; he ignored it.

“Did the dragon see you? Did it move towards you? Were you injured at all?” Each question came separately, precise as the cuts beneath the linen. “And what happened after you crossed the wards?”

The Malice moved again inside him, cold amusement threading abruptly through the rage.

*Ask her.*

*No.*

*You want to know.*

*It is not the point.*

*Coward.*

Theo closed his eyes for one second.

“Did you win?”

Draco made a sound through his nose that might have been disbelief or the murder of an inappropriate laugh before it could escape.

Theo opened his eyes again. There was no humour in his face, which made the question infinitely worse.

“That was Him,” he said. “The rest is me.”

His gaze held on Jackie.

“Tell me exactly how close you got.”

### Jackie

The grin threatened and she stifled it so ferociously that the mattress creaked under her hands. It was absurd. She was reckless, and stupid and she rocked her life.
And still, the Malice knew exactly which question to ask.
"Yes, it saw me. Yes, it moved. I ran alongside the cage and jumped back before it could incinerate me. I just got my tail singed." She knew she wouldn't have to explain that she changed forms.
The fucker was big, but slow. It would've never gotten me.
She sent it to Malice, for the first time since she sensed him in the Underground feeling that the bond might not be so bad after all.
Outside, she released the mattress and placed her hands in her lap. She still couldn't look at Theo. She didn't want to find out what showed on his face.
"And no, I lost. The wager said how close we could get to the wards, not past them."
She held her breath for a moment, and then exhaled.
"We had no idea that you... If I knew, I would've never..."
But that was a cheap explanation, and she knew it. Hinging her willingness to risk her life on whether Theo was currently fine or being tortured.

### Theodore

For several seconds, Theo said nothing.

His eyes had dropped to the hands in her lap. They were whole. So were her arms, her face, everything he could see. He looked anyway, with a concentration that made the silence worse, his own bandaged fingers resting motionless against the mattress.

Inside him, the Malice received her boast.

*It got your tail.*

The answer returned along the bond without hesitation, a low, almost conversational correction.

*That's attached to you, Little Wolf. Do keep track.*

Theo felt the amusement sharpen within their shared mind. He hadn't heard what she'd sent, and the sudden change irritated him almost as much as the prospect of being grateful for it.

*What?*

*She's explaining how it couldn't catch her.*

Theo's gaze lifted to her face.

“It set you on fire.”

“The tip of her tail,” Draco said. “The fur. She wasn't burned.”

Theo looked at him then. Draco met it squarely, but his thumb pressed hard against the side of his wedding band.

“I checked.”

Some small, rigid thing released beneath Theo's ribs. It left the anger with more room.

“And you lost.”

“Disqualified,” Draco supplied, with such exact distaste that Theo could almost hear the argument that had preceded it. “Crossing the ward was expressly forbidden.”

Theo looked back at Jackie. The corner of his mouth threatened to move. He caught it, too late to be entirely convincing.

*I like the crossing,* the Malice remarked privately. *Poor finish.*

*You would.*

*You asked whether she won.*

*You asked.*

*Your mouth.*

“Shut up,” Theo muttered.

Draco's eyebrows rose.

“Not you.”

“For once.”

The almost-smile went. Theo shifted towards Jackie, then stopped when the movement dragged the dressing against his skin. He hated that: the small physical veto, the reminder that even turning to face his wife required an accommodation. For a moment he simply sat with his jaw set, waiting for the pain to settle instead of giving it an audience.

When he spoke again, his voice was lower.

“Don't finish that. You didn't know where I was.”

He glanced at the white linen around his forearm.

“And if I'd been there, I'd probably have laughed when you challenged Bastien.” His eyes returned to her. “Right up until you went through.”

Draco breathed out through his nose. “Yes. That was roughly the sequence.”

“I would've wanted to see you run,” Theo admitted, and the admission came grudgingly, as though it had been dragged out of him by the absurdity of pretending otherwise. “I wouldn't have wanted to watch you burn.”

He reached across the short distance and set his hand beside hers, close enough that his smallest finger touched the outside of her hand. His fingers were cold. He left them there without closing around her wrist.

“I know you're fast, Menace. I know you're good.” His mouth tightened. “That's why I'm angry. You know exactly what a ward is for.”

Within him, the Malice had gone quiet again. Not absent; Theo could feel the attention, intent and unpleasantly patient. Then His voice reached for Jackie alone.

*Next time you want something dangerous to chase you, ask Me.*

Theo's fingers pressed briefly into the mattress.

*Not helping.*

*I wasn't talking to you.*

Theo swallowed the answer. He was still watching Jackie, still waiting for the eyes she hadn't raised to his.

“Was getting that close what you wanted,” he asked, “or did you decide to go through because he thought you wouldn't?”
