### Theodore

Viola looked at Jackie for a moment longer. Then she set one page atop another.

“I would expect you to use your connections,” she said. “It would be foolish to acquire them and then pretend you had none. I will take that as notice, not invocation.”

Theo felt a brief, wholly inconvenient satisfaction. It survived until Viola continued.

“Ferrara's backing remains yours to call upon. My authority here remains mine. We need not discover tonight how far either extends.”

Beside him, Draco said nothing. His thumb rested against the edge of his cuff, just below the dressing. Theo watched it move once and wished, with sudden intensity, that they were somewhere he could take that hand without every person present assigning it a value.

His legs were tiring. Feeding had restored strength, but standing still was spending it with infuriating efficiency.

*Sit,* the Malice said.

*Not yet.*

*Then stop wasting us on pride.*

Filippo shifted in the chair at Jackie's left.

“The responsibility I offered is still available,” he said. “He recovers at Haven House. I remain with him. If you require his return to discuss a service, arrange it through me.”

“You were also responsible for his presentation.”

“Yes.” Filippo accepted the blow without looking away. “This time I am in London.”

Viola regarded him. Whatever passed between them had very little to do with Theo's bandages, although he disliked being the occasion for it.

“Then let us make use of that.”

She turned to him.

“I am prepared to release you into your sire's keeping tonight, to recover at Haven House. Once he considers you fit, a return meeting will be arranged through him. You will attend with him, in person, to hear the terms of one service.”

Theo's attention narrowed to her mouth.

“Hearing is not acceptance,” she added. “No service is owed merely because you agree to the meeting.”

Draco's thumb stopped moving.

### Draco

He wanted to ask her to repeat the first sentence.

Not because he hadn't understood it. Because *tonight* had been absent from every version of the future he'd allowed himself to consider since seeing Theo downstairs, and now it was lying on the table with all the other things Viola could choose to give or withhold.

He kept his mouth shut. Theo was already speaking.

“And if I refuse the service?”

“Then we discuss your objection.”

“Here?”

“Where else would you propose?”

Theo's expression barely changed.

“Somewhere I can leave.”

Filippo rested his hands on the arms of his chair. Draco watched Viola rather than either of them.

“This return would be for negotiation,” she said. “I will not detain you merely for declining the undertaking I propose. Nor will I accept an unanswered summons as a substitute for declining it to my face.”

“You'll put that in writing,” Filippo said.

It wasn't quite a question.

Viola's gaze returned to him, cool and sustained. Then she drew a clean sheet towards her.

“The release and the conditions of return. Yes.”

Draco finally let his hand fall from his cuff.

“And Ferrara?” he asked.

“Has not been asked for anything.” She looked at Jackie. “You have made his involvement known. It need go no further tonight.”

The pen remained on the table beside the blank sheet. Viola had not yet picked it up.

“Your husband goes home under his sire's responsibility. He returns for a meeting, not an unspecified punishment and not a demonstration.” Her attention moved briefly to Theo on that last word. “If those are terms you can agree to, we can stop discussing what you might promise and write down what you actually will.”
