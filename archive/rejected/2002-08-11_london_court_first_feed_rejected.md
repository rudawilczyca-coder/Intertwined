# Rejected draft — non-canon

Rejected by Alice on 18 September 2026 for chaotic point-of-view switching and unnatural dialogue. Preserved as revision evidence only; do not use as played canon.

### Draco

The first swallow made Theo's face tighten.

It was blood, but cold and stored—the taste of glass and somebody else's kitchen beneath it. His body took it before pride could object. Hunger pulled at the muscles in his throat, turning the second swallow into something rougher, and Filippo adjusted his hand beneath the flask without trying to take it away.

*Slowly,* Theo told himself.

*Why?*

The Malice had been a pressure without language since the footsteps stopped across the landing: rage packed against his ribs, hunger scraping at the inside of his teeth, Jackie's presence on the other side of stone and spell. The blood sharpened Him enough to speak.

*Because if you choke us, de' Medici will hold the bottle himself.*

*He already is.*

Theo swallowed again. He could feel the tremor in his hands now that there was something in them to betray it. He hated that Draco could see. He hated more that Draco was trying not to.

Draco had fixed his eyes on the flask. It was safer than looking directly at the dressings around Theo's arms, and less cowardly than looking at the floor. Each time Theo swallowed, the dark level dropped by another small measure. Not enough to repair what had been taken. Enough, perhaps, to put something back behind his eyes besides the vivid red that had met Draco at the door.

Only it wasn't fading.

Theo paused with the mouth of the flask still against his lower lip. His gaze had gone unfocused, angled not towards anyone in the room but towards the wall across the landing.

"Theo," Draco said.

His eyes moved back. "I'm drinking."

"I can see that."

The answer came sharper than Draco intended. Nell looked up from the cloth she'd been refolding on the bedside table. Filippo did not. His hand remained beneath the glass, steady and impersonal.

Theo lowered the flask an inch. The blood had made the room cruelly distinct: Nell's stillness, the living beat in Draco's throat, Filippo's old scent beneath the wool of his coat. Beneath all of it was Jackie—not a smell, not through two closed doors, but the restless drag of her presence along the bond. Near. Moving. Still there.

*She is wearing a path into their floor,* the Malice observed.

*Leave her alone.*

*She thinks they have killed us.*

*She knows we're alive.*

*Those are not the same thing.*

Theo raised the flask again because it kept his mouth occupied. The Malice pressed against the bond with the same terrible lack of proportion He brought to everything: hunger as an avalanche, reassurance as possession. Theo caught at Him and found no clean edge to hold. They had been bled into weakness together. Only one of them had become easier to contain.

*Don't hurt her.*

The answering amusement was brief and without warmth. *Then stop making me do all the talking.*

The pressure left him in a single cold rush.

Across the landing, the Malice sent only words this time—stripped clean of hunger and fury.

*We're feeding, Little Wolf. Stop wearing a path into their floor.*

Draco saw Theo's fingers lock around the flask. Filippo's supporting hand came higher before the glass could tilt.

"What did He do?" Draco asked.

Theo's head turned towards him slowly. There was enough blood in him now for irritation to look like irritation rather than exhaustion. "Told her I was feeding."

Draco looked at the wall, then back at Theo. "She can hear Him."

It was not a question. He kept Jackie's confidence down to the smallest truth the room already held. Nell's hands went still over the cloth. Filippo's expression did not change, but something in his attention narrowed.

Theo took the flask from his mouth. "I know."

"Do you know what else she feels?"

The question landed. Theo looked down at the blood remaining in the glass.

*Careful,* the Malice murmured. *The pretty one is angry with us.*

*He should be.*

That silenced Him for the length of one swallow.

"Not all of it," Theo said. The admission cost him visibly; Draco watched it settle into the bend of his neck. "Enough. More here than in France. I can't close His side of it yet."

Yet. Draco held on to the word because everything else in the answer invited panic. Theo hadn't called the bond an attack or pretended it was harmless. He had named a failure of control as something he meant to learn.

"Then don't send anything else without asking her."

The red in Theo's eyes deepened. For one disorienting second Draco could not tell which of them had taken offence.

"I didn't send that," Theo said.

"I know. I'm talking to both of you."

Filippo's gaze moved to Draco. Nell became very still. Draco let them. He had spent too many months addressing the Malice only after He had already taken the room. If He could listen from behind Theo's eyes, He could be given the boundary before someone had to enforce it.

Theo's mouth shifted—not quite a smile, and not entirely his.

*He has improved,* the Malice said.

*Don't encourage him.*

*I wasn't speaking to you.*

Theo drank again.

When the flask was empty, Filippo eased it out of his hands rather than wait for them to release it cleanly. Theo sat with his palms open on his thighs. Colour would have meant nothing on a vampire, but some of the collapse had gone from his posture. He was still too thin beneath the torn line of his shirt, still bandaged, still sitting on a bed in a locked room. He no longer looked as though standing would split him apart.

Nell stepped closer. "Give it a minute."

Theo looked at the door.

Draco knew the look. It was the same one Theo had turned on every locked thing since they were seventeen: not calculation about whether it would open, but calculation about what breaking it would cost.

"I'll bring her," Draco said.

Theo's attention snapped to him. The Malice surged at the wording; Draco saw the crimson flare and did not step back.

"I'll ask her to come," he corrected. "And I will tell her exactly what room she's entering."

For a moment Theo said nothing. Then he lowered his eyes to the dressings at his wrists and nodded once.

Draco went to the door. Filippo moved aside for him, taking the empty flask with him. Nell stayed near the bed. No one tried to tell Draco that Viola had authorised a feeding and not a reunion.

He crossed the landing and lifted the Muffliato from the room where he had left Jackie. The ordinary silence beneath it returned: the faint settling of the building, fabric shifting on the other side of the door, his own breath. He knocked twice.

"It's me," he said. "He's fed. He wants to see you."
