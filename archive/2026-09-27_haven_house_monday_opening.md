# Haven House — Monday, 12 August 2002 — Opening

Source: Sable-authored bridge and scene opening following the accepted Sunday close.

## Accepted author rounds

### Sable

At eight-twenty on Monday morning, the front door closed and Haven House became quiet in a new way.

The wards settled first. Their faint pressure moved through the walls and floorboards, recognising two departures and sealing the threshold behind them. Then the human sounds receded—the last footfall from the hall, the brief metallic complaint of the garden gate, the distant crack of Apparition beyond the property line.

False sunlight filled the windows.

It lay across the parlour carpet in broad August squares, warm in colour and harmless to the two vampires inside them. Dust moved through it. Somewhere in the kitchen, the clock above the stove marked the half-hour with a small, officious chime.

Theo stood at the parlour window with one hand against the frame.

The donor’s blood had repaired the worst of the hollowness beneath his eyes. It had steadied his hands, restored the economy of his balance, quieted the Malice until His presence had become a pressure rather than a voice. It had not undone three days beneath Viola’s court. Bruises still shadowed Theo’s wrists. When he shifted his weight, he did it carefully.

Behind him, a page turned.

Filippo had occupied the armchair nearest the bookcase sometime before dawn. The curtains in the library had been checked twice; the wards, once. Now he sat inside a patch of manufactured morning with an open book in one hand and no apparent objection to the illusion.

Luna had chosen the low table between them. Her tail moved once across the print.

“You can stop pretending to read,” Theo said.

“I am reading.”

“You have been on the same page for eleven minutes.”

Filippo looked down at the book as if the page had personally disappointed him. He closed it over one finger.

“And you have been standing at a window you cannot open for nine.”

Theo’s reflection stared back from the glass—pale, dark-eyed, dressed in yesterday’s black trousers and one of Draco’s shirts because its cuffs did not press against the bruising.

“Perhaps we are both enjoying the view.”

“No.”

The word entered the room without force and removed the last excuse from it.

Filippo set the book on the arm of the chair. Outside, a Muggle woman crossed the square with a terrier straining ahead of her. Theo watched until both disappeared behind the plane trees.

“I want you to consider Florence,” Filippo said.

“I have.”

“You refused it before I finished offering it.”

“That was efficient.”

“That was fear wearing your manners.”

Theo turned then.

The movement was slow enough to betray him, though his face did not. A narrow brightness passed behind his eyes and vanished—the Malice stirring at the shape of an argument, interested but not yet invited.

“Be careful.”

“I have been careful with you for twenty months.” Filippo’s voice remained level. “Care is not the same thing as avoidance.”

Luna lifted her head. Neither man looked at her.

“London can recognise you on Wednesday,” Filippo continued. “I can discharge the formal responsibility afterward, if that is what you choose. None of that obliges you to remain here merely because you survived here.”

“This is my home.”

“Yes.”

The answer stopped Theo more effectively than contradiction would have.

Filippo leaned back, one pale hand resting beside the closed book.

“I am not asking you to abandon it. I am asking whether London is the only place from which you can belong to it.”

The clock in the kitchen advanced by one minute. The sound carried through the bright, empty house with irritating clarity.

Theo looked towards the hall, where two coats were missing from their hooks and the silence still held the exact shape of the people who had left for work.

Filippo waited.

“Tell me,” he said at last, “whether London is the future all three of you want—or merely the one none of you have yet been willing to question.”
