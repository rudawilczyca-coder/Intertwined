### Viola

The warning settled in the room.

Jackie had not raised her voice. She had not needed to. Viola regarded her for a long moment, the way one might examine a letter which had arrived through unexpected channels—its contents not surprising, but its address quite interesting.

"Mrs Nott-Malfoy."

She placed both hands flat upon the table.

"I have no doubt that you have ties in London. You are standing in my court at three in the morning because your husband is a vampire in my Domain, which rather demonstrates the point." Her voice carried no anger. "But you have mistaken the nature of this conversation."

Filippo's hand stilled against his knee.

"I am not negotiating a trade agreement," Viola continued. "I am not weighing your connections against your husband's usefulness and calculating a favourable margin. You are describing leverage. What I am describing is jurisdiction."

She rose from her chair. Not quickly—the movement was unhurried, the papers remaining undisturbed beneath her fingertips as they lifted. Standing, she was not tall. She did not need to be.

"Every vampire who resides in London answers to me. Not because I purchased their loyalty, not because they have no alternatives, and not because their families have been persuaded. Because that is what it means to live here."

Her gaze passed over Jackie and settled on Theo.

### Theodore

The room had gone very quiet.

Theo met Viola's eyes and did not reach for the back of the nearest chair, though his body offered that suggestion with some urgency. Beside him, Draco's breathing had acquired the particular evenness it took on when he was thinking faster than he wished to show.

*She is not wrong,* the Malice said.

The observation arrived without His usual hostility. That was considerably more alarming than if He had recommended violence.

*I didn't say I disagreed.*

*You are deciding how to stand, not what to say. I am offering the less urgent correction.*

Theo shifted his weight. His forearms ached under the dressings. Four days of careful reduction had left his body a catalogue of its own limits, and every one of them declared itself while Viola watched him from behind a table he had never been invited to sit at.

### Viola

"Lord de' Medici."

She turned to Filippo. The shift was deliberate—not dismissing the rest of the room, but returning the question to where it belonged. Two elders and the fledgling between them.

"You may take responsibility for your childe. You may remove him to Florence, present him there, and answer for his conduct under your own Domain. That is your right as his sire, and I will not contest it."

Filippo's expression did not change. Theo could hear Draco's sleeve move against his own.

"Or," Viola said, "you may release him."

She let the word arrive without ornamentation.

"If Mr Nott remains in London, he is presented here. Haven House is recognised as his residence. He answers for his feeding, his secrecy, his conduct, and his attendance when summoned—as every vampire in London does."

Her eyes moved back to Theodore.

"He answers to me."

### Filippo

Filippo did not rise.

He looked at Viola, then at Theo, and Theo could see the calculation behind the stillness—not cunning, but the particular kind of care that came from knowing what both options would cost.

"You are asking me to surrender my claim on my own childe."

"I am asking you to choose which claim you wish to keep." Viola's hands came together at her waist. "You cannot hold a foreign authority over a resident of my Domain and purchase my tolerance with one errand. He lives here. He has chosen to live here. Either bring him home, or let him be home."

*She is giving us to ourselves,* the Malice said. And beneath it, smaller: *Or to her.*

*Both. That's the point.*

### Draco

Draco watched Filippo's hands. They had not moved from his knees, but the left one had pressed fractionally harder against the cloth. It was, Draco thought, the first time he had seen Filippo genuinely surprised by something Viola had said.

Not displeased. Not outraged. Surprised, in the particular way of a man who had arrived prepared for one argument and found another waiting.

Draco understood the calculation. If Filippo released Theo, he surrendered his formal authority as sire—the right to recall, to compel, to answer for. He would remain Theo's maker, but the political line that had permitted him to walk into London and negotiate for Theo's liberty would dissolve. Theo would belong to London.

And if he kept the claim, he kept the right—but London would require the body that came with it.

*That,* Draco admitted privately, *is rather elegant.*

### Theodore

"Three days."

Viola said it before either of them could answer.

"I do not expect this decision tonight." She looked at Filippo with something that was not quite courtesy. "You have accepted responsibility for your childe's recovery. I accept the timeline your presence implies. Three days. When Mr Nott is well enough to stand before me without someone's blood holding him upright—"

A glance at Draco's wrist.

"—he will present himself. Without summons. And give me his answer."

She remained standing. Theo felt the Malice settle like a held breath.

*Three days,* He said.

*I heard her.*

*Three days to decide who holds our leash.*

*No one holds it. That's the answer she's offering.*

*Then she's offering something she intends to collect on.*

Theo looked at Filippo.

Filippo looked back at him, and for the first time since Florence, Theo could not read what his sire wanted him to choose.

"Three days is acceptable," Filippo said.

He rose.

"Then we are finished for tonight." Viola resumed her seat, the papers gathering beneath her hand. Her attention had already moved past them—not dismissively, but completely, the way a door closes when its function has been served.

Filippo inclined his head. Theo gave a second bow, shallow enough that his forearms did not protest. Draco's was precise and revealed nothing.

Theo turned toward the corridor without hurrying. His hand found the doorframe again, held it for half a breath, and let go.

### Draco

The corridor was cooler. Draco matched Theo's pace without comment, their shoulders close enough to touch at the turns. Filippo walked behind them, his coat settling with each measured step.

Theo did not speak until they were past the last of Viola's people.

"She's not offering freedom," he said quietly. "She's offering belonging. Those aren't the same thing."

"No," Draco said. "They aren't."

He looked at Theo's profile—the jaw set, the eyes steady, the bandages catching light at his wrists.

"But you already know which one you want."

Theo's mouth moved, and did not quite become a smile.

*Home,* the Malice murmured.

Theo said nothing. His hand brushed Jackie's as they walked, and he did not pull it away.
