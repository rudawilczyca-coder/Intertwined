### Theodore

*You're not helping.*

*You keep saying that as though it disappoints Me.*

Theo drew his thumb along the seam of the mattress. He could imagine the cage too easily now: not its particulars, but the challenge of it. Something dangerous on the other side of a line. He shifted his hand, brushing his knuckles against hers.

“But I want you back. Entire. Tail included.”

The last two words came with a flatness that wasn't quite a joke. His fingers remained beside hers, and he looked at them for a moment rather than at her face.

“I'm trying to be furious,” he admitted. “You understand that? I can hear exactly how stupid it was. I can picture how it could've ended.” His jaw tightened. “Then you move, or say something, and all I've got is that you're here.”

*Bring her here,* the Malice said inside him, abruptly impatient with the distance of a few inches.

*She's here.*

*Closer.*

Theo ignored the command, although wanting the same thing made it considerably less satisfying.

“Come on,” he said to Draco instead, touching the mattress on his other side. “Sit down. You're making the furniture nervous.”

Then he returned his attention to Jackie.

“I believe you wanted to live a little.” His voice quietened. “What I don't like is that someone only had to tell you no to choose how you'd do it.”

“Thank you,” he said.

His voice sounded more like his own.

### Theodore

The lamps were too bright after the stairwell.

Theo let his eyes adjust while Filippo entered. The long table remained, the papers at Viola's right hand, the same immaculate order in which she had been able to discuss his life while having him kept below it. His fingers missed the rail. He closed them once at his side and left them there.

Viola looked up.

Her attention moved from his face to the dressings visible beneath his cuffs, then to Draco's wrist. It returned to Theo without any change he could call regret.

*Let her look,* the Malice said.

Theo lifted his chin a fraction.

Filippo inclined his head.

“Prince. Theodore is ready to hear your proposal.”

“I see that your visit has been restorative.”

“My household has been generous,” Theo said.

He gave her a restrained bow. The movement drew unpleasantly across his forearms, but he straightened without reaching for either spouse. Draco offered his own acknowledgment beside him, exact and cool.

Viola's eyes remained on Theodore for a moment longer.

“Then let us put that generosity to practical use.”

Filippo drew out a chair without sitting.

“We have returned to hear the service you require. Its purpose, and its limits.”

“Yes. You were precise about that.” She touched the papers beside her. “Recognition of his residence here. No compulsory return to Florence. A defined undertaking, disclosed before acceptance.”

“Before acceptance,” Theo repeated.

“That is the arrangement, Mr Nott.”

The pause that followed was hers. Theo could feel Draco beside him resisting the urge to fill it; he found himself attending to that instead of the impulse to say something costly.

Viola leaned back.

“Your sire offers his responsibility. Your household has come here to insist that your place is with them. Very well.” Her gaze passed over Draco and settled on Jackie. “But affection is not a guarantee of conduct. I imagine all of you understand that distinction by now.”

Draco's expression closed.

“What, precisely, are you asking?”

“What happens if he agrees to my terms, then breaks them.” Viola did not look away from Jackie. “Would you undertake to bring him before me if I summoned him, Mrs Nott-Malfoy—or would your assistance end the moment he wished to refuse?”

### Jackie

Jackie went into the room a step behind Draco and Theo. She didn't want to be at the front, mainly because she wasn't sure if she had her nerves under control enough to make sure she wouldn't blow at the Prince of London. She walked up the stairs behind them too, so she could see how much every step cost Theo. The man who could traverse three flights of stairs in mere seconds had now problems with only one. And this was all Prince Viola's doing. Even if she didn't personally request him to be tortured, it happened under her roof and with her expressed or implied permission. She listened to the conversation, standing somewhere between Theo and Filippo. She knew her standing here was pretty low. If one could even say she had any standing at all. And yet, despite of that, it was her, Prince Viola, addressed directly. She looked straight into the old red eyes of the woman. You do realize, Your Majesty, that I do not have many options to bring him before you against his wishes. I am a wizard, but he is a vampire. However, I can assure you that avoiding responsibility that we would tie with you is not my intention, and neither it is his. I also secured three boons from Mr. Ferrara on Theo's behalf. If they have any value here, do let me know.

### Theodore

Theo noticed the weight of his own arms while Viola considered her answer. The bandages pulled when he straightened. Draco stood close enough that their sleeves touched, his dressed wrist held loosely at his side.

Viola left Jackie's assurance unanswered for a moment.

“I am not asking you to wrestle him into a carriage,” she said. “I am asking whether, if he refused a summons, I would find your household assisting that refusal. Whether your door would close against my people.”

Theo's fingers curled, then loosened.

“That depends rather heavily on what your people arrive to do.”

Her gaze moved to him.

“Yes,” she said. “I expected that qualification.”

Draco's sleeve brushed his again. Not restraint. A reminder of how little space there was between them, and how much there still was between standing here and getting home.

“And now,” Viola continued, returning her attention to Jackie, “you introduce Mr Ferrara.”

Her hands came apart on the table. She glanced at Filippo.

It was a small change. Theo watched it with considerably more interest than he had watched her courtesy.

*There,* the Malice said.

*I saw.*

*Then let her answer.*

For once, Theo did.

### Filippo

“Cosimo undertook to provide political backing for the household,” Filippo said. “Through three debts owed by Primogen.”

He did not move in front of Jackie. His hands remained at his sides, the black coat hanging open enough to show the silk beneath it.

“That is not the same as three unrestricted favours to be handed over here. But it is not nothing.”

“I did not suggest it was nothing.”

“No,” Filippo agreed. “You have not yet told us what you require, either.”

Viola looked at him for a long moment.

Theo could hear the quiet movement of paper beneath her fingers. He wanted to sit. The desire irritated him enough that he shifted his weight instead, carefully, without allowing the movement to become a stagger.

“Your childe's attendance,” Viola said. “His compliance with the obligations he accepts. And some reason to believe that the next disagreement will not require another discussion with his entire household.”

“An obligation he has actually accepted would be a useful beginning,” Filippo replied.

### Draco

Draco watched Viola leave that unanswered.

Until Ferrara's name, she had been asking what Jackie would do with Theodore. Now she was looking between Jackie and Filippo, and her questions had acquired an additional pause before she spoke them.

It wasn't respect. Draco knew better than to mistake an adjustment in price for one.

“If there are to be guarantees,” he said, “we need to hear what they guarantee. You are asking us about a refusal before you've told us what he might be refusing.”

“Because a promise made only when convenient is not especially valuable, Mr Malfoy.”

“Neither is one so broad that no one can keep it.”

Theo's mouth tightened beside him. Draco kept his eyes on Viola rather than discover whether that was approval or the beginnings of something unhelpful.

Viola rested her hand flat upon the papers.

“Very well. We will distinguish the service from the question of future summons.”

She turned her attention back to Jackie. No softening accompanied it, but neither did she dismiss the name Jackie had brought into the room.

“Before I set out that service, I would like to understand your position. Are you asking to invoke Ferrara's backing in this negotiation—or informing me that it remains available to you?”

Filippo remained still beside her.

This time, Viola waited for an answer without supplying the alternatives herself.

### Jackie

Jackie tried to follow the conversation, although she was never the kind of person who preferred standing around and conversing when something could be done. She liked books and words because they were written down and couldn't suddenly change their meaning depending on who spoke them. Conversations were like fleeting birds. One could think they caught a bunch of them only to discover they were actually just butterflies. So Jackie did not participate in the conversation. She saw Theo changing his weight, but she didn't acknowledge it in any way. She also wished Draco would keep his mouth shut. For some reason, she felt that whenever he spoke, he was only making things more difficult for Theo. When Viola addressed her again, Jackie looked at Filippo for a second. He was sitting down in the chair on her left side, close enough that if she wanted to, she could place her hand on his arm. But she knew she put herself in this predicament, and she needed to get herself out. She considered Viola's questions for a moment. If she invoked those boons, then they would be spent and couldn't help her anymore. On the other hand, she still didn't understand exactly what value it had and where it could help them and where it could prove difficult or cause harm. I just wanted to note, she said slowly, her voice measured, that while we do wish to remain as part of your court, we also aren't as new to London as you might think and consider us. We have our own ties here, and if needed, we will use them. She realized a little too late that it might have sounded threatening. But honestly, after less than three hours of sleep, not even sure what time of day it was, considering that there weren't any open windows in this entire household, and after witnessing Theo in such a state, Jackie's patience was wearing pretty thin. Some little voice in her head supplied that perhaps it wasn't Draco who was making things difficult for them.
