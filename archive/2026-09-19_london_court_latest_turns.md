## Jackie

Jackie went into the room a step behind Draco and Theo. She didn't want to be at the front, mainly because she wasn't sure if she had her nerves under control enough to make sure she wouldn't blow at the Prince of London. She walked up the stairs behind them too, so she could see how much every step cost Theo. The man who could traverse three flights of stairs in mere seconds had now problems with only one. And this was all Prince Viola's doing. Even if she didn't personally request him to be tortured, it happened under her roof and with her expressed or implied permission. When they sat, she sat as well. Oh, out of character, actually scratch that, she didn't sit down. She listened to the conversation, standing somewhere between Theo and Filippo. She knew her standing here was pretty low. If one could even say she had any standing at all. And yet, despite of that, it was her, Prince Viola, addressed directly. She looked straight into the old red eyes of the woman.

"You do realize, Your Majesty, that I do not have many options to bring him before you against his wishes. I am a wizard, but he is a vampire. However, I can assure you that avoiding responsibility that we would tie with you is not my intention, and neither it is his. I also secured three boons from Mr. Ferrara on Theo's behalf. If they have any value here, do let me know."

## Theodore

Theo noticed the weight of his own arms while Viola considered her answer. The bandages pulled when he straightened. Draco stood close enough that their sleeves touched, his dressed wrist held loosely at his side.

Viola left Jackie's assurance unanswered for a moment.

"I am not asking you to wrestle him into a carriage," she said. "I am asking whether, if he refused a summons, I would find your household assisting that refusal. Whether your door would close against my people."

Theo's fingers curled, then loosened.

"That depends rather heavily on what your people arrive to do."

Her gaze moved to him.

"Yes," she said. "I expected that qualification."

Draco's sleeve brushed his again. Not restraint. A reminder of how little space there was between standing here and getting home, and how much there still was between standing here and getting home.

"And now," Viola continued, returning her attention to Jackie, "you introduce Mr Ferrara."

Her hands came apart on the table. She glanced at Filippo.

It was a small change. Theo watched it with considerably more interest than he had watched her courtesy.

*There,* the Malice said.

*I saw.*

*Then let her answer.*

For once, Theo did.

## Filippo

"Cosimo undertook to provide political backing for the household," Filippo said. "Through three debts owed by Primogen."

He did not move in front of Jackie. His hands remained at his sides, the black coat hanging open enough to show the silk beneath it.

"That is not the same as three unrestricted favours to be handed over here. But it is not nothing."

"I did not suggest it was nothing."

"No," Filippo agreed. "You have not yet told us what you require, either."

Viola looked at him for a long moment.

Theo could hear the quiet movement of paper beneath her fingers. He wanted to sit. The desire irritated him enough that he shifted his weight instead, carefully, without allowing the movement to become a stagger.

"Your childe's attendance," Viola said. "His compliance with the obligations he accepts. And some reason to believe that the next disagreement will not require another discussion with his entire household."

"An obligation he has actually accepted would be a useful beginning," Filippo replied.

## Draco

Draco watched Viola leave that unanswered.

Until Ferrara's name, she had been asking what Jackie would do with Theodore. Now she was looking between Jackie and Filippo, and her questions had acquired an additional pause before she spoke them.

It wasn't respect. Draco knew better than to mistake an adjustment in price for one.

"If there are to be guarantees," he said, "we need to hear what they guarantee. You are asking us about a refusal before you've told us what he might be refusing."

"Because a promise made only when convenient is not especially valuable, Mr Malfoy."

"Neither is one so broad that no one can keep it."

Theo's mouth tightened beside him. Draco kept his eyes on Viola rather than discover whether that was approval or the beginnings of something unhelpful.

Viola rested her hand flat upon the papers.

"Very well. We will distinguish the service from the question of future summons."

She turned her attention back to Jackie. No softening accompanied it, but neither did she dismiss the name Jackie had brought into the room.

"Before I set out that service, I would like to understand your position. Are you asking to invoke Ferrara's backing in this negotiation—or informing me that it remains available to you?"

Filippo remained still beside her.

This time, Viola waited for an answer without supplying the alternatives herself.
