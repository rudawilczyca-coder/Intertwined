### Sable

Draco stayed beneath the spray after she left, the shampoo bottle still in one hand.

He knew.

Of course he knew. The word had been sitting between them since Paris, unspoken here because saying *children* inside Haven House would make it real in a different way. Not a promise made between two people in a hotel bed. A question that required a third answer.

He worked the shampoo through his hair quickly, rinsed, then shut off the water. In the sudden quiet, the house beyond the bathroom seemed to draw closer. Two vampires below them. One with hearing sharp enough to pick a heartbeat out through walls; the other old enough that Draco had no interest in testing his limits.

He took a towel from the rail and pressed it to his face before stepping onto the mat. Water had crept beneath the dressing at his wrist and ran along the bruising there. He frowned at it, then set the problem aside until he had his wand.

“I know.”

He kept his voice low. Not secretive. Merely private.

Draco wrapped the towel around his hips and followed her into the bedroom. His clothes waited in their careful stack beside the disorder she had left him to tend. The sight steadied him more than it ought to have.

“I’m not going to tell him while he’s half-starved, half-healed and being assessed by his sire.” He picked up his wand and drew the worst of the water from his hair, leaving the ends damp against his neck. “And I’m certainly not doing it with Filippo listening through the floorboards.”

His gaze went to the closed bedroom door.

“But I will tell him.”

The ease of the sentence surprised him. The rest did not.

“I’ll tell him what I told you. That I want children with you because I want them—not because my mother set a deadline, not because the Malfoy name requires an heir, and not because I think wanting them entitles me to anything from your body.”

### Sable

Draco stopped with the towel halfway through his hair.

For several seconds he only stared at her.

The flush began at his ears when she said *two Frenchmen*. By the time she reached Merlin’s balls, it had crept down the back of his neck and vanished beneath the towel around his shoulders.

“That is not—”

Her laughter broke over the words.

Draco shut his mouth. He glanced towards the bathroom, as though the shower might let him step back inside it and undo the last thirty seconds.

“Moreau drew a portrait,” he said, with the precision of a man laying evidence before the Wizengamot. “Morel danced with me. Neither event caused me to develop an interest in reproductive ethics.”

That did not help.

He dropped the towel onto the chair and dragged both hands through his damp hair. It came up standing in several directions at once, which only deepened the indignity.

“You said I needed to speak to Theodore. You meant the conversation about children. I was attempting to explain that I intended to approach it without sounding like my mother.” Draco heard the sentence only after it had left him. His eyes closed. “And now I sound like a pamphlet from the Ministry’s Department of Excessively Careful Husbands.”

He opened them again and found no mercy waiting in the room.

“Stop laughing.”

The order lacked every quality required to function as one.

Draco folded his arms. The movement tugged at the dressing on his wrist, and he unfolded them at once with a small wince, which did nothing whatsoever for his authority.

“For the record, I did not believe that wanting a child gave me proprietary rights over you. Not before France, not during France, and not after being admired by any number of Frenchmen.” His jaw tightened. “I was trying to be explicit because this conversation matters, and because Theodore deserves better than discovering we’ve built an entire future and added him afterward as an administrative amendment.”

The last line came without the fluster. It settled between them for a beat, plain and true.

Then Draco looked at her on the floor, at the tears her laughter had put in her eyes, and his expression collapsed into affront all over again.

“Two,” he said. “There were *two* Frenchmen. That does not constitute a pattern.”

### Sable

Draco looked at the bedroom door.

Beyond it, the staircase registered its measured complaint. One step. A pause. Another.

“He is not supposed to be climbing three flights of stairs.”

Draco snatched the first pair of trousers from the wardrobe and dragged them up over wet legs. The fabric snagged at one ankle. He nearly went down, caught himself against the wardrobe door, and straightened with whatever remained of his composure scattered across the carpet.

The staircase creaked again. Closer.

He seized a shirt and shoved his arms into it. Buttons could be a later problem.

A knock landed against the bedroom door.

“Malfoy.”

Theo’s voice was rough, thinned by exhaustion. It still came through the wood like a hand closing around the back of Draco’s neck.

Draco opened the door.

Theo stood with one hand braced against the frame. Whatever colour three bags of blood had restored downstairs was already draining from his face, and the climb had carved the hollows beneath his cheekbones deeper. None of it softened his stare.

His eyes travelled over Draco’s open shirt, his damp hair, his bare feet on the floorboards. Then they came back, very slowly, to his face.

“Who,” Theo asked, “is Monsieur Morel?”

Draco’s hand tightened on the edge of the door.

“A French Magical Games adviser.”

“How administrative.”

Theo stepped across the threshold.

His body betrayed him halfway through the movement. One knee softened; his hand found Draco’s shoulder instead of the frame. Draco reached for his arm without thinking.

Theo accepted the support—then used it, pressing forward until the open door met Draco’s back and Theo’s cold fingers had curled around his upper arm.

Weak, apparently, did not mean harmless.

“Did you enjoy France?” Theo asked.

“Parts of it.”

“Find it enlightening?”

Heat climbed into Draco’s ears.

Theo’s gaze flicked to them. “Your wife announced *bisexual* loudly enough for Filippo to hear it from the kitchen. I assume this enlightenment involved more than a committee briefing.”

“I danced with him.”

Theo went still.

Not surprised. Interested.

“You danced with Monsieur Morel.”

“One waltz.”

“Did you enjoy it?”

Draco could have said it was formal. He could have listed the boundaries, the careful distance, the exact bar of music at which Adrien let him go. Every word would have been true, and none of it would have answered Theo.

“Yes.”

Theo’s fingers tightened once around his arm.

“Did you want him?”

That one landed lower. Draco looked past Theo’s shoulder, into the dim corridor, then made himself look back.

“Yes.”

Something dark and intent shifted behind Theo’s eyes. The Malice was listening. Theo did not look away.

“And?”

“And nothing. I danced with him. I wanted him. I came upstairs with Jackie.”

“Those are three separate facts.”

“I’m aware.”

“Are you?” Theo’s voice dropped. “Because you spent years treating me as an exception you could lock in a room and refuse to name. Now I hear my wife shouting the word through two floors, and I learn about a Frenchman by his surname.”

Draco’s chin came up. The defence was out before he could stop it.

“I did not hide him from you. You were chained beneath London while it happened.”

Theo’s expression changed. The grip on Draco’s arm eased, but he did not step back.

“No,” he said. “You didn’t hide him.”

It had never been accusation. Draco saw that too late. Possession, yes. Curiosity. The raw irritation of having missed a change in someone Theo loved. But not betrayal.

Theo’s thumb moved once against the inside of Draco’s arm.

“Would you have gone with him?”

Draco swallowed.

“No.”

“Would you have wanted to?”

There it was. The question with nowhere polite to stand.

“I wanted him,” Draco said again. “I also wanted Jackie to watch me want him. I wanted to come back upstairs with her and tell her exactly what it had done to me.”

Theo held his gaze for a long moment.

Then one corner of his mouth lifted—not kindly.

“That does sound like you.”

Draco exhaled through his nose. “Have you finished?”

“Not remotely.”

Theo’s knee wavered again. Draco tightened his grip before he could fold, and Theo let himself be steadied without surrendering an inch of the space between them.

“You’re sitting down,” Draco said.

“You’re telling me everything.”

“Those are not mutually exclusive.”

Theo glanced at the bed, then back at Draco.

“Good.” His fingers slid from Draco’s arm to the open edge of his shirt and stayed there. “Start with why she called you bisexual as though it were breaking news.”

Theo ignored it.

“Then I kissed him behind the Wyvern’s Nest.” His gaze slid back to Draco. “He kissed me back.”

“For one second.”

“Long enough.”

Draco’s embarrassment hardened into affront. “Long enough for what, precisely?”

Theo’s fingers stayed curled in the loose edge of his shirt. He was leaning more of his weight on Draco than he had a moment ago, but the weakness only concentrated his stillness.

“To know straight wasn’t the answer.”

Draco stared at him.

“You knew from one kiss.”

“No.” Theo’s thumb moved once against the damp cloth over Draco’s chest. “I suspected from one kiss. I knew in Florence.”

That silenced him.

Florence had given Theo more than a kiss stolen in an alley. There, Draco had touched him by choice. Had looked at him without Jackie between them, and stayed. Had asked for things, stopped them, come back to them. Theo had been given every answer but the word itself.

“And you said nothing,” Draco said.

“It wasn’t mine to say.”

The reply came at once. Blunt enough to leave no room for nobility.

Draco’s eyes went from Theo to Jackie and back. Something appalled gathered in his expression as the shape of it came clear.

“Both of you.”

Theo’s mouth twitched.

“Apparently.”

“Both of you reached this conclusion independently, discussed none of it with me, and then waited for a Frenchman to make it administratively relevant.”

“I didn’t know about the Frenchman.”

“That is not the portion of that sentence requiring correction.”

Theo’s legs finally lost the argument his pride had been having with them. His grip tightened; Draco caught him around the waist before he could slide down the door.

“Bed,” Draco said.

“In a minute.”

“Now.”

Theo let Draco turn him, but kept his hold on the shirt and brought him along. He sat on the edge of the mattress without letting go, knees bracketing one of Draco’s legs.

“I didn’t wait for Morel,” Theo said. “I waited for you.”

The line took the theatre out of Draco’s indignation. Not all of it. Enough.

Theo looked up at him, pale and spent and entirely unwilling to soften the next question.

“So. You know now?”

Draco glanced at Jackie, then down at the man who had apparently been patient with a truth Draco hadn’t known he was keeping from himself.

“Apparently everyone does.”

Theo’s mouth shifted—not quite a smile.

“That wasn’t the question.”

Draco went still. The answer cost him visibly, caught somewhere between humiliation and the last useless instinct to deny what both of them had already seen.

“Yes,” he said at last.

Theo’s grip loosened on his shirt. Not releasing him. Only gentling.

“All right.”
